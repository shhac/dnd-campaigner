# Tsikhe-Dzveli (Old Fortress)

**Type**: Mtskhuri Ruin / Dungeon
**Region**: Deep Nalkamaa, ~8 days north of Kylmaharju
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

## Overview

The largest known Mtskhuri ruin, built directly above the prison of the Dreaming Chorus. On the surface, a ring of twelve standing stones — each fifteen feet tall, inscribed floor to tip with Mtskhuruli script — surrounds a depression in the permafrost where stone steps descend into darkness. Below the surface, an underground complex of vast chambers, spiraling corridors, and spaces that don't obey geometry. The subsonic hum emanates from here, growing louder with depth.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: The standing stone circle, visible for miles across the tundra. Faint luminescence from the stones at night. Below ground: walls of dark stone carved with geometric reliefs — spirals, nested circles, patterns that seem to move in peripheral vision. Architecture that curves in impossible directions. Rooms that are larger on the inside than the outside.
**Sounds**: The hum. Always the hum. It permeates the stone, the air, the body. Deeper in, it resolves into something almost rhythmic — almost a voice. Echoes that arrive before the sound that should cause them. Silence in places where there shouldn't be silence.
**Smells**: Cold stone. Copper. Ozone. Something organic and ancient, like the inside of a geode. The deeper levels smell warm — an animal warmth that doesn't belong underground in permafrost.
**Feeling**: Awe and dread in equal measure. The Mtskhuri built to endure millennia, and it shows. The ruin feels intentional — every angle, every carving, every acoustic property was designed. But the design serves a purpose the party doesn't yet understand, and that purpose is waking up.

## Key Features

### The Stone Circle (Surface)
Twelve standing stones arranged in a perfect circle, 60 feet in diameter. The stones hum in different tones, creating a chord that is felt rather than heard. The central depression contains stone steps descending into the ruin's entrance hall. Evidence of Seppanen's base camp surrounds the circle — abandoned tents, supply crates, Seppanen's journal.

### The Entrance Hall (Upper Level)
A vaulted chamber 50 feet across, carved from living rock. The ceiling is covered in Mtskhuruli script — the densest concentration of text in the ruin. Corridors branch in six directions. The architecture is conventional here — stone, right angles, comprehensible space. Seppanen's expedition explored this level thoroughly; their marks and notes are on the walls.

### The Shifting Galleries (Middle Levels)
Below the entrance hall, the ruin becomes unreliable. Corridors connect to different rooms at different times. Stairs descend to chambers that are at the same elevation they started from. The walls are warm. The carvings depict figures — Mtskhuri, apparently — walking into geometric patterns and not emerging. Aino's maps catalog the configurations, but the ruin has had weeks to change since she left.

### Dzmar-Etseri — The Root Cradle (Deep Level)
The bottom. A vast natural cavern expanded and shaped by the Mtskhuri into a cathedral-space. The containment mechanism's physical core: a column of dark crystal that extends from floor to ceiling, wrapped in Mtskhuri metalwork and inscribed with the original binding formula. This is where the Chorus is closest to the surface world. The air here tastes of copper and feels thick, as if the space itself has mass. Eero is here.

## Points of Interest

- **Seppanen's Journal**: Found at the base camp. Documents the expedition's progressive deterioration. Essential reading for the party.
- **The Cartography Room**: A middle-level chamber Aino discovered, containing Mtskhuri maps carved into stone tablets. These maps show the Sinatl'e network — every standing stone, every sealed site, every connection. Vieno can interpret them with effort.
- **The Binding Inscription**: The text on the Root Cradle's central column. The original formula for the containment. Reading it requires Mtskhuruli literacy; understanding it requires knowledge of the Mtskhuri's sacrifice.
- **The Sealed Door (Vardzia-Kari)**: A passage in the middle levels sealed with Mtskhuri metal. It leads to a chamber the Wardens sealed centuries ago. Behind it: records of the original binding, including the Mtskhuri's last messages before dissolution.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Psychic intrusion**: WIS saves against the Chorus's probing. Failure = vivid hallucination (personal fears, false memories, Chorus communication attempts) and +1 Drift.
- **Architectural confusion**: Navigation checks to avoid getting lost in the shifting galleries. DC 14 with Aino's maps, DC 18 without.
- **Ilmari Niskanen**: He's been here for weeks and knows the upper levels. He'll approach as a friendly survivor before revealing his agenda.

### Possible Encounters
- **Echo soldiers**: Remnants of consumed expedition members. Not undead — more like recordings. They walk patrol routes, respond to hails, and repeat their last coherent words. They are not hostile but deeply unsettling. They cannot be saved.
- **Sinatl'e flare**: The containment network surges, causing standing stones to glow intensely and the hum to spike. Anyone at Drift 1+ must save or experience a flash of Mtskhuri memory — fragmentary, overwhelming, and potentially informative.
- **The Chorus speaks**: At Drift 2+, characters hear direct communication. The Chorus is curious, not hostile — but its curiosity and its hunger are not clearly distinct from each other.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The shifting galleries reconfigure according to the Chorus's will — it is testing the party, observing how they respond to confusion and fear
- The consumed soldiers' patrol routes trace the shape of the containment formula. They are part of the seal now, their patterns reinforcing the binding. Interfering with them weakens the containment.
- Dzmar-Etseri contains the physical mechanism for both renewal (sacrifice) and collapse (destruction). The same device serves both purposes depending on how it's activated.
- The Mtskhuri consciousness within the Sinatl'e is aware of the party's presence. The standing stones' increased luminescence is not the Chorus — it's the Mtskhuri trying to communicate.

## History

Built approximately 4,000 years ago as the primary containment facility for the Dreaming Chorus. The Mtskhuri civilization spent decades constructing the ruin, incorporating their most advanced resonance technology into a structure designed to last forever. When the Chorus proved impossible to destroy, the Mtskhuri chose dissolution — binding themselves into the Sinatl'e to power the containment with their collective consciousness.

The ruin was sealed and the Mkhartveli were established to guard it. For millennia, this worked. The hum was dormant, the seals held, the Chorus slept. Then the permafrost began to shift. Then the hum returned. Then Seppanen came.

## GM Notes

Tsikhe-Dzveli should feel like a place that was built by people who knew what they were doing and were terrified anyway. The architecture is beautiful, precise, and designed to contain something that required an entire civilization's death to imprison.

The shifting galleries are a GM tool — they allow the ruin to feel vast without requiring a detailed map. Describe the space differently each time. Let the party feel lost without actually being unable to progress. Aino's maps provide some control; without them, the middle levels are a nightmare.

The transition from upper levels (conventional, comprehensible) to middle levels (unsettling, non-Euclidean) to Dzmar-Etseri (alien, alive, wrong) should mirror the Drift scale. The deeper the party goes, the more the environment reflects the Chorus's influence.

The echo soldiers are horror, not combat. They should make the party realize what Drift 5 actually means: you become part of the machine. This makes the stakes of Drift personal rather than abstract.
