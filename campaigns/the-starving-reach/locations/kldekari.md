# K'ldekari (Warden Fortress)

**Type**: Hidden Stronghold
**Region**: Deep Nalkamaa, cliff face overlooking a frozen river valley
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

## Overview

The hidden seat of the Mkhartveli order, built into a cliff face above a river that hasn't thawed in centuries. From the tundra, it's invisible — the entrance is a narrow fissure in the rock, concealed by carved wards that redirect attention. Inside, the fortress is modest but functional: living quarters for the thirty-odd remaining Wardens, an archive of fragmentary Mtskhuri records, a shrine to the ancestors, and the Elder's chamber where Sulkhan has held vigil for nearly four centuries.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: A narrow canyon approach, ice-slicked stone, the fissure entrance that appears to be a natural crack. Inside: warm stone corridors lit by bioluminescent moss the Wardens cultivate. Simple sleeping alcoves. The archive — a circular room with stone tablets set into the walls. The shrine — a chamber where a single standing stone has been incorporated into the fortress's foundation, humming softly.
**Sounds**: The wind outside, muffled to near-silence within. The hum of the shrine's standing stone — quieter here, almost soothing, like a heartbeat. Kartvelian speech echoing softly from corridors. The scratch of Nino's pen in the archive.
**Smells**: Beeswax, dried herbs, old stone, the metallic tang of the standing stone. Wood smoke from a central hearth. The faint sweetness of the bioluminescent moss.
**Feeling**: Sanctuary. The first place since Kylmaharju that feels safe — and unlike Kylmaharju, it actually is. The wards suppress the Chorus's influence within K'ldekari's walls. Characters at Drift 1-2 will feel immediate relief. The fortress feels ancient, cared for, and weary — like its inhabitants.

## Key Features

### The Archive
A circular chamber containing the Mkhartveli's collected records of the Mtskhuri civilization. Stone tablets, leather scrolls, charcoal rubbings of inscriptions. Fragmentary, incomplete, and in Kartvelian rather than the original Mtskhuruli — translations of translations, each generation losing fidelity. Vieno will be desperate to study here. Sulkhan will grant limited access.

### The Shrine
A standing stone — one of the original Sinatl'e nodes — incorporated into the fortress's foundation. The Wardens use it as a focus for their suppression rituals. Tamar knows the rituals. The stone hums at a frequency that counteracts the Chorus's influence, creating K'ldekari's warded zone. Sulkhan meditates here daily.

### The Warden's Hall
The central gathering space. Long stone table, a hearth that hasn't gone out in decades. Where the Wardens eat, plan, and argue. Sulkhan presides. Nino challenges. The remaining twenty-eight Wardens listen and try to figure out which of them is right.

## Points of Interest

- **The Forbidden Stacks**: A sealed section of the archive that Sulkhan restricts. Contains the most dangerous Mtskhuri texts — records of the original binding, the Chorus's nature, and the repair mechanism at Dzmar-Etseri. Nino has been accessing these secretly.
- **The Warding Chamber**: Where the Wardens perform their suppression rituals. Contains the tools and materials for Drift reduction. A long rest here reduces Drift by 1 automatically.
- **The Lookout**: A ledge above the canyon that provides a view across miles of tundra. The Wardens maintain a watch here. On clear days, the standing stone circle of Tsikhe-Dzveli is visible as a dark smudge on the northern horizon.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Sulkhan's judgment**: The Elder will assess the party's intentions, knowledge, and threat level. This is a social encounter with high stakes — it determines how much the Wardens help.
- **Nino's approach**: She'll seek out the party (especially Vieno and Tamar) to share her research and build support for her alternative plan.
- **Rest and recovery**: K'ldekari is a safe place to recover from exhaustion and Drift. The party may be reluctant to leave.

### Possible Encounters
- **The archive revelation**: If allowed access, Vieno discovers records that reveal the Mtskhuri's fate. This is a lore-heavy scene with emotional weight.
- **Tamar's reckoning**: If her Warden identity isn't already revealed, being in K'ldekari makes concealment impossible. Sulkhan will address her role in front of the party.
- **The standing stone whispers**: Characters at Drift 1+ who meditate near the shrine stone may hear Mtskhuri whispers — not the Chorus, but the dissolved civilization. Fragmentary, cryptic, and deeply sad.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The shrine stone is not just warding — it's a communication node. The Mtskhuri within the Sinatl'e can transmit fragmentary messages through it. Sulkhan has been receiving these messages for centuries and hasn't told the other Wardens.
- The forbidden stacks contain a complete description of the Dzmar-Etseri mechanism, including the repair option Nino found. They also contain a warning: the repair was never tested. The Mtskhuri didn't have time.
- K'ldekari's wards are weakening. Sulkhan has noticed but hasn't told anyone. The standing stone's hum is slightly off-pitch — the containment network's degradation is reaching even here.

## History

Built approximately 3,800 years ago by the first generation of Mkhartveli — Mtskhuri who refused the binding. They chose to survive, to guard the seals, to maintain vigil until they could find another way. Four millennia later, they're still here, and they still haven't found another way. K'ldekari has been continuously inhabited longer than any human civilization on this continent, and its inhabitants consider themselves failures for every year of that duration.

## GM Notes

K'ldekari is the campaign's emotional pivot. It's where the party learns the full truth (or as much as the Wardens know), where alliances form or fracture, and where the Act 3 decision begins to take shape.

The fortress should feel like relief — warm, safe, quiet. The Drift reduction makes it mechanically appealing. The party will want to stay. This creates tension with the urgency of the failing containment.

Sulkhan and Nino represent the two Warden responses to an impossible problem: acceptance (sacrifice) and hope (repair). Neither is wrong. The party's influence may tip the balance.

Tamar's position in K'ldekari is unique — she's both insider and outsider, Warden and companion. Her interactions here determine whether the party sees the Mkhartveli as allies or as another faction with their own agenda.
