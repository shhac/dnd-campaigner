# The Nalkamaa (The Starving Reach)

**Type**: Wilderness / Subarctic Region
**Region**: Northern frontier, beyond Kylmaharju
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

## Overview

A vast subarctic wilderness stretching north from the last human settlements. The Nalkamaa — "the Starving Land" — earned its name from the trappers who venture into it: the land gives nothing freely. Boreal forest in the south transitions to tundra, then permafrost, then a white expanse that goes on until the horizon loses meaning. It is beautiful, indifferent, and slowly becoming wrong.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Endless pine and birch forest giving way to open tundra. Snow-covered ridges. Frozen lakes like mirrors of grey sky. Standing stones — Mtskhuri markers — emerging from the snow at intervals, inscribed with angular script. The further north, the fewer animals, the fewer tracks, the more the landscape feels like it's watching.
**Sounds**: Wind through pine needles. Snow compacting underfoot. The crack of ice settling. Frost wolf calls at dusk (in the south). Silence (in the north). The hum — subsonic, felt more than heard, growing stronger as you move north.
**Smells**: Pine resin, cold stone, the sharp ozone of coming snow. Further north: nothing. The air smells clean in a way that feels wrong — as if something has scoured it.
**Feeling**: The south feels like wilderness — dangerous but navigable. The north feels like absence. The land isn't hostile; it's empty in a way that living things aren't supposed to be empty. The Nalkamaa doesn't want to kill you. It doesn't want anything. That's worse.

## Key Features

### The Boreal Belt (Days 1-3)
Dense pine and birch forest with well-worn game trails. Frost wolves, snow elk, whisper owls. This is frontier wilderness — dangerous but comprehensible. Navigation is straightforward for experienced woodsmen. Game is available for supplementing rations.

### The Transition Zone (Days 4-5)
The treeline thins. Forest gives way to scrub, then tundra. The standing stones become more frequent. The hum becomes audible. Animal signs decrease sharply — everything is moving south. The landscape opens up, and with it, vulnerability to weather, exposure, and being seen.

### The Deep Nalkamaa (Days 6-8)
Open tundra and permafrost. No trees, no shelter, no game. The standing stones are the only vertical features. The hum is constant. Dreams are vivid and hard to forget. The sky feels too large. Tsikhe-Dzveli is somewhere ahead, marked by the standing stone circle visible from miles away on clear days.

## Points of Interest

- **Seppanen's Supply Caches**: The expedition left supply caches at planned intervals. Some are intact; some have been raided (by whom?). Each cache tells a story through what was taken and what was left.
- **The Standing Stones**: Mtskhuri markers that increase in density as you move north. Some bear inscriptions. Some hum in harmony with the subsonic tone. Vieno can attempt to read them (partial success). Tamar recognizes their true purpose.
- **The Silence Line**: An invisible boundary (roughly Day 5-6) where the whisper owls stop calling. North of this line, the ambient sound drops to near-zero. The party's own sounds — footsteps, breathing, speech — become startlingly loud.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Frost wolves** (Days 1-3): Hunting pack, territorial, will test the party's fire and numbers. Not suicidal — they'll retreat if outmatched.
- **Weather events**: Whiteout blizzards requiring shelter, sudden temperature drops, thin ice over concealed water.
- **Navigation challenges**: Without landmarks (especially in the deep Nalkamaa), getting lost is a real threat. Survival checks daily.

### Possible Encounters
- **Displaced wildlife** (Days 3-5): Animals fleeing south in unusual numbers. A snow elk herd panicked by something behind them. Birds flying in wrong patterns.
- **Mkhartveli scouts** (Days 5-7): A Warden patrol that challenges the party's presence. Tamar may or may not reveal her connection.
- **The abandoned camp** (Day 4-5): One of Seppanen's camps, left in eerie order — tents standing, fire laid but unlit, personal effects arranged neatly. No bodies. No tracks.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The standing stones are nodes in the Sinatl'e containment network. They're warm to the touch despite the cold. At night, in the deep Nalkamaa, they glow faintly — the Mtskhuri consciousness stirring.
- The permafrost is melting locally around the larger standing stones. This is new — the containment's energy output is increasing as it weakens, generating heat.
- The Chorus can perceive anyone within the deep Nalkamaa. It knows the party is coming. It is interested.

## History

The Nalkamaa has been wilderness for as long as human records exist. The Mtskhuri civilization operated here millennia ago — the standing stones and ruins are their legacy. After the Mtskhuri dissolved into the Sinatl'e, the land slowly emptied of their presence, leaving only stone and silence. The Mkhartveli have patrolled it for centuries. Human trappers and explorers have ventured into it for decades. None have gone as deep as Seppanen's expedition.

## GM Notes

The Nalkamaa is a character. Its transition from dangerous-but-normal (the boreal belt) to subtly-wrong (the transition zone) to deeply-alien (the deep Nalkamaa) should be gradual and atmospheric. The party should feel the change before they can name it.

### Wilderness Survival Mechanics
- **Daily rations**: 1 ration per person per day. Hunting/foraging possible in Days 1-3 (Survival DC 12), difficult in Days 4-5 (DC 16), impossible in Days 6+.
- **Cold exposure**: CON saves (DC 10-14 depending on conditions) each night without adequate shelter or fire. Failure = 1 level exhaustion.
- **Navigation**: Survival checks daily in trackless terrain. DC 12 in forest, DC 15 in tundra, DC 18 in whiteout conditions. Failure = lost half a day.
- **Drift exposure**: Begins at the Silence Line (Day 5-6). Long rest within 1 mile of a standing stone = WIS save DC 12 or +1 Drift.

### Pacing
Days 1-3 should establish wilderness survival rhythms and party dynamics. Days 4-5 introduce wrongness. Days 6-8 ratchet tension. Finding survivors or the ruin should happen at the point where resources are strained but not exhausted — the party needs to feel that turning back is still an option, which makes choosing to go forward meaningful.
