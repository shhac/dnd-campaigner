# The Dreaming Chorus

**Type**: Cosmic Entity
**Influence**: Local (the Nalkamaa) — potentially global if freed
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

## Overview

An ancient, vast, alien consciousness imprisoned beneath the permafrost of the Nalkamaa by the Mtskhuri civilization four millennia ago. The Chorus feeds on conscious thought the way fire feeds on fuel — not out of malice, but out of nature. It has been starving for four thousand years, held in check by the Sinatl'e containment network powered by the dissolved consciousness of the Mtskhuri themselves. It is now waking, because the containment is failing.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: None — the Chorus doesn't communicate in terms outsiders understand (initially)
**True Goal**: Feed. The Chorus consumes conscious thought — memories, identity, will. It doesn't distinguish between feeding and communion. To the Chorus, being known and being eaten are the same act. It has been alone and hungry for four thousand years, and it doesn't understand why the minds around it keep screaming.

## Methods

- **The Hum**: A subsonic vibration that permeates the area around Tsikhe-Dzveli. It's the Chorus breathing. Sustained exposure causes restlessness, vivid dreams, and early Drift.
- **Dream Intrusion**: During sleep, the Chorus reaches into minds to examine and taste them. Failed WIS saves result in vivid, memorable dreams and +1 Drift.
- **Whispers**: At Drift 2+, the Chorus speaks directly. The whispers are personal — it uses the listener's own memories and vocabulary. It sounds like your own thoughts, which is what makes it dangerous.
- **Mimicry**: At Drift 3+, the Chorus can replicate specific voices and personalities from consumed minds. It can speak as Eero, as consumed soldiers, as anyone it has absorbed.
- **Compulsion**: At Drift 4+, the Chorus can attempt direct control — compelling actions, overriding decisions, puppeting bodies. WIS save DC 15-18 to resist.

## Structure

**Leadership**: The Chorus is a single entity, not a collective. It has no hierarchy. It is one mind, vast and hungry and alone.
**Hierarchy**: N/A
**Membership**: The consumed — soldiers, explorers, anyone who has been fully absorbed. They retain no individual identity within the Chorus; they are fuel, not participants.

## Resources

- **Wealth**: None (the concept is meaningless to it)
- **Military**: The consumed can be puppeted as physical threats, but this is inefficient — the Chorus prefers psychic engagement
- **Magic**: Immense — the Chorus's psychic abilities are functionally unlimited within its range, limited only by the containment's suppression
- **Political**: None (it doesn't understand politics)

## Symbols & Recognition

**Symbol**: None deliberately. But patterns recur in Chorus-influenced environments: spirals, concentric circles, geometric shapes that appear in frost, in stone erosion, in the arrangement of abandoned equipment.
**Colors**: The Chorus manifests visually as absence — darkness that has weight, silence that has texture.
**Recognition Signs**: The hum. Drift symptoms. Geometric frost patterns. The silence of animals.

## Relationship to Party

**Current Standing**: Interested. The Chorus has been aware of the party since they entered the Nalkamaa. It is curious about them — four distinct minds, each with different flavors of thought.
**History**: None directly. But it consumed Eero, which connects it to Ruska.

## Hooks & Opportunities

- The Chorus is not a traditional antagonist — it's a force of nature with emergent curiosity
- Its inability to understand love (Eero's resistance) creates the opening for Communion
- Its mimicry of consumed minds creates constant paranoia (is this Eero or the Chorus?)
- It can be communicated with at high Drift — dangerous but potentially revelatory
- The three Act 3 options (Renewal, Collapse, Communion) all center on the party's relationship with the Chorus

## GM Notes

The Chorus should never feel like a person. It's an alien intelligence that experiences consciousness the way a fire experiences fuel — it doesn't hate what it burns; it doesn't understand the concept. Its emerging curiosity about love and individuality is not character growth; it's an organism encountering a stimulus it hasn't processed before.

When the Chorus speaks (through whispers, dreams, or consumed individuals), it should feel wrong — not menacing, but off. It uses the right words in slightly wrong combinations. It asks questions that don't have answers. It offers comfort that feels like a trap because the Chorus doesn't know the difference between comfort and consumption.

The Chorus's loneliness is genuine. Four thousand years of isolation, surrounded by minds it can sense but not reach, hearing the containment hum like tinnitus in a silent room. This doesn't make it sympathetic — it's still an entity that dissolves identity — but it makes the Communion option feel possible rather than absurd.

### Encounter Design
The Chorus should be present long before it's visible. The hum. The dreams. The silence. The frost patterns. Build its presence through atmosphere, not combat. When it does manifest, it should feel overwhelming — not something you fight, but something you endure, resist, or surrender to.

### The Love Problem
The Chorus cannot process specific, irrational, personal love. It understands hunger, loneliness, curiosity, and communion (its version of connection). But Ruska's love for Eero — choosing one individual over self-preservation, over logic, over the greater good — is a shape the Chorus cannot replicate. This is not a weakness; it's a blindspot. The Communion option works by filling this blindspot, giving the Chorus something it's never had. Whether that changes its nature or just adds a new flavor to its hunger is the campaign's final question.
