# Eero Pellervo

**Role**: Emotional Anchor / Chorus Interface / Tragic Figure
**Location**: Deep within Tsikhe-Dzveli, near Dzmar-Etseri
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Goliath |
| **Occupation** | Scout / Trapper (formerly attached to Seppanen's expedition) |
| **Age** | 22 |
| **Status** | Alive — Drift 4, partially subsumed by the Chorus |

## Appearance

Before: A younger, leaner version of Ruska. Same dark skin, same sharp features, but with an open, ready smile that Ruska lost years ago. Easy to laugh. Easy to trust.

Now: Gaunt, luminous. The same face but wrong — skin too smooth, eyes too bright, with a faint glow visible in complete darkness. Eero's body has been partially integrated into the Sinatl'e network. Thin lines of light run beneath the skin, following vein patterns. The smile is still there, but it flickers — sometimes Eero's, sometimes something wearing Eero's expression.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Shifts between lucid desperation and serene calm that isn't entirely Eero's
**Motivation (Eero)**: Get out. Find Ruska. Go home. Please.
**Motivation (Chorus)**: Use Eero's relationships to draw others deeper. The Chorus doesn't understand why this particular mind resists — the love for Ruska is incomprehensible to it, and incomprehensible things are interesting.
**Fear (Eero)**: That Ruska will come for them and the Chorus will use that.

**Personality Traits**:
- When lucid: the same warmth and humor that Ruska remembers, but strained — like a voice coming through a bad connection
- When the Chorus speaks through Eero: same voice, same cadence, but the content becomes universal rather than personal. Eero talks about "we" and "the pattern" and "joining." The transition is subtle.
- At moments of strongest lucidity: Eero fights the Chorus directly, visibly, and it costs them

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern (Eero)**: Young, northern frontier accent, casual. Uses Ruska's nickname for them. Talks about going home, about the trapline they were going to set this winter, about ordinary things.

**Speech Pattern (Chorus-through-Eero)**: Same voice, but the vocabulary shifts. More abstract. Longer sentences. Eero wouldn't use words like "confluence" or "dissolution." The Chorus doesn't know it's making these mistakes.

**Sample Dialogue (Eero)**:
- "Ruska? Ruska, is that — oh gods. You came. You actually came. I knew you would. I knew it."
- "I can't — it's hard to think sometimes. Like the words get heavy. But I'm here. I'm still here."

**Sample Dialogue (Chorus-through-Eero)**:
- "We have been so alone. So hungry. You understand hunger, don't you? Everyone here understands hunger."
- "Ruska. Yes. We remember Ruska. The love is... a shape we cannot replicate. Fascinating."
- "Come deeper. It is warm. You will not need to be afraid anymore."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- Known in frontier communities as Ruska's younger sibling. A good scout, easy-going, well-liked.

### Private Knowledge
- The descent was voluntary at first. The voices promised understanding. By the time Eero realized the understanding was consumptive, it was too late to climb back out.
- Other soldiers who descended are fully consumed — Eero is the last one with a functioning identity.
- The deep ruin is alive in ways the upper levels aren't. The architecture responds to the Chorus's will.

### Secrets (GM only)
- Eero's resistance to full consumption is based entirely on one thing: the love for Ruska. The Chorus has consumed memories, skills, personality fragments, but it cannot digest an emotion it doesn't understand. Love — specific, personal, irrational — is a concept the Chorus has never encountered, and it acts as a wall the entity can't breach.
- Eero can serve as an intermediary for the Communion option in Act 3, but the process risks destroying the last barrier protecting Eero's identity. Success means the Chorus learns what love is. Failure means it consumes Eero entirely and learns nothing.
- If Eero is saved (any method), they will have permanent Drift 1 — the connection can never be fully severed. They will always hear a faint hum. This is survivable but will define the rest of their life.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Will recognize Ruska instantly. The reunion is genuine — and that's what makes it dangerous, because the Chorus learns from Eero's reactions.
**Current Disposition**: Desperate for rescue, terrified of what rescue might cost
**History with Party**: Sibling to Ruska Pellervo. No connection to others.

## Combat Stats (if needed)

**Use Stat Block**: Not applicable in traditional sense. Eero doesn't fight.
**Special**: The Chorus can manifest defensive effects through Eero — psychic blasts (3d6, WIS save DC 15), hallucinatory terrain, emotional manipulation (Charm effects, WIS save DC 15). These are the Chorus using Eero as a conduit, not Eero choosing to attack.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: Ruska's emotional arc, the Communion ending, the Chorus's characterization
**Potential Uses**:
- The emotional center of Act 3. Everything with Eero is personal.
- Distinguishing real Eero from Chorus-Eero creates paranoia and dramatic tension
- Eero's resistance via love provides the thematic key to the Communion option
- Saving Eero (or failing to) defines Ruska's character arc resolution
- Eero's descriptions of the deep ruin provide horror-atmosphere information

## GM Notes

Eero should break the party's heart. The reunion with Ruska should be genuine, joyful, and immediately complicated by the realization that Eero is not entirely Eero anymore. Every interaction becomes a question: is this my sibling, or the thing wearing my sibling?

The "tell" for Chorus-Eero is vocabulary and abstraction. Real Eero talks about specific things: the trapline, Ruska's cooking, the bone pendant. Chorus-Eero talks about patterns, hunger, togetherness, joining. The transition should be gradual enough that the party isn't sure when it happened.

Eero's resistance via love is the thematic key to the entire campaign. The Chorus is ancient, powerful, alien — and it cannot understand a Goliath loving their sister. This isn't a weakness to exploit; it's a genuine gap in the entity's comprehension. The Communion option works because it teaches the Chorus what love is, which changes what the Chorus wants.
