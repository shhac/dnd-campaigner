# Kaleva Seppanen

**Role**: Quest Target / Authority Figure
**Location**: Unknown — last known position: en route to Tsikhe-Dzveli
**Campaign**: The Starving Reach

| | |
|---|---|

| **Status** | Unknown (alive, leading ~15 survivors in the wilderness) |

## Appearance

A broad-shouldered man gone lean from weeks of poor rations. His military bearing remains — straight back, steady gaze — but the flesh beneath it has been hollowed out. Grey-streaked beard, untrimmed. Deep-set eyes that stay focused even when the rest of him is shaking. His uniform is filthy but still buttoned correctly.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Controlled, authoritative, fraying at the edges
**Motivation**: Get his surviving soldiers home. Everything else — the mission, his reputation, the ruins — is secondary.
**Fear**: That he led these people to their deaths. That the ones who went deeper did so because he failed to maintain discipline.

**Personality Traits**:
- Maintains military formality even when everything is falling apart — it's the only structure left
- Will not give an order he isn't willing to carry out himself
- Cannot forgive himself for splitting the expedition, even though it saved the survivors

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Formal military cadence, but slower than it should be. Pauses mid-sentence to collect thoughts that used to come instantly. Addresses everyone by rank or title. Uses "we" instead of "I" — the commander's habit.

**Mannerisms**: Counts his people obsessively. Scans treelines. Flinches at sounds below the range of hearing.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- "We made a tactical decision to withdraw. Some chose not to follow. That is the extent of my report."
- "You have ten days of rations. We have been surviving on less than one for three weeks. Do the math."
- "I will debrief fully when my soldiers are safe. Not before."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- Respected military commander with twenty years of frontier service
- Led the original expedition of forty soldiers into the Nalkamaa six weeks ago
- Known for competence, fairness, and an unwillingness to take unnecessary risks

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- The expedition fractured on the eighth night at Tsikhe-Dzveli. Roughly half the soldiers refused to leave the ruin, insisting the voices were guiding them to something important
- He ordered the retreat knowing he was abandoning people. He has not slept properly since.
- The Crown's briefing included intelligence about the ruin that was not shared with the rank and file — he was told to assess "strategic applications"
- Two members of his expedition were not who they claimed to be (Ilmari and Esko — Crown's Shadow agents)

### Secrets (GM only)
- He has Drift 2. He hears the hum constantly and has been hiding the severity from his soldiers. His dreams are vivid and repetitive — always the same image: a vast dark space, and something in it breathing.
- He suspects the Crown knew the ruin was dangerous before sending the expedition. He considers this a betrayal but has no proof.
- He recognized the Chorus's influence as similar to classified reports he'd read about previous incidents at other Mtskhuri sites — reports that were supposed to be sealed.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Relief (they're rescue) tempered by suspicion (the Crown sent four people to find forty — that's not a rescue, that's a probe).
**Current Disposition**: Neutral — wants to cooperate but doesn't trust the party's mission parameters
**History with Party**: None

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: The primary quest, the expedition's fate, Crown's Shadow subplot
**Potential Uses**:
- Provides firsthand account of what happened at Tsikhe-Dzveli
- Can confirm or deny Crown's Shadow involvement
- His soldiers provide additional party support or complications
- His Drift symptoms foreshadow what awaits deeper in the ruin
- His guilt over the split mirrors Toivo's guilt over the mountain pass

## GM Notes

Seppanen is what Toivo could become — a competent leader broken by a decision that haunts him. He should feel like a warning, not a villain. His information is critical to the party's understanding of what happened, but he'll be reluctant to give it all at once. He's been surviving on discipline alone, and debriefing means admitting the discipline wasn't enough.

The party may find him hostile to further exploration of Tsikhe-Dzveli. He wants out, not deeper in. Convincing him to help rather than hinder will require demonstrating that they have a plan he didn't.
