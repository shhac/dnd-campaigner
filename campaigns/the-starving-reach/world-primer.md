# World Primer: The Nalkamaa

What any frontier settler or traveler would know.

---

## The World

The Nalkamaa — "the Starving Land" — is a vast subarctic wilderness on the northern frontier of a declining empire. Boreal forest in the south gives way to open tundra and permafrost in the north. The land is beautiful in summer (endless twilight, wildflowers on the tundra) and murderous in winter (whiteout storms, temperatures that kill exposed skin in minutes). Most people live south of it. The few who venture in do so for furs, minerals, or because they have nowhere else to go.

## The Settlements

- **Harmaakallio** ("Grey Rock"): The regional capital, two weeks south of the frontier. A proper town with Crown administration, temples, markets. Most people's idea of "the north."
- **Kylmaharju** ("Cold Ridge"): A frontier outpost of roughly 200 souls at the Nalkamaa's southern edge. Part trading post, part military waystation. The last place to buy supplies before the wilderness. Governed by a reeve who answers to Harmaakallio in theory and to practical necessity in fact.
- **Valkeasuo** ("White Marsh"): A wetland settlement between Harmaakallio and Kylmaharju. Known for peat harvesting and as a rest stop on the northern road.

## The Crown

The empire that claims this territory. Once powerful, now overextended and decaying. The Crown's interest in the north is primarily strategic — rumors of ancient sites with military potential keep funding flowing, but actual support (soldiers, supplies, infrastructure) arrives slowly if at all. Frontier folk are loyal to the Crown in the way they're loyal to the weather: it exists, it affects them, and complaining about it changes nothing.

## The Mtskhuri Ruins

Ancient stone structures dot the Nalkamaa — standing stone circles, partially buried walls, entrances sealed with slabs that no known tool can cut. The locals call them "the old bones." Nobody knows who built them. The accepted theory is a long-dead civilization; the frontier folk mostly avoid them. Strange sounds near the larger sites are attributed to wind, subsidence, or superstition.

The Mtskhuri script appears on some ruins — angular, dense, unreadable. Scholars in Harmaakallio have studied it for decades without breakthrough. Frontier folk consider the script bad luck and won't touch inscribed stones.

## The Mkhartveli

A reclusive order of druids and rangers who live in the deep Nalkamaa. Locals know they exist — occasionally one appears in Kylmaharju to trade furs or herbs — but their numbers, location, and purpose are mysterious. They speak with Kartvelian accents and use words no one else recognizes. Most settlers consider them harmless eccentrics. A few old-timers insist they guard something.

## Common Knowledge

- **The hum**: For the past several months, people near the northern Nalkamaa have reported a low vibration — not quite sound, more felt than heard. Animals avoid the area. The Crown sent an expedition to investigate. They haven't come back.
- **Dreams**: Settlers in Kylmaharju have reported unusual dreams since the hum began. Vivid, repetitive, hard to remember on waking. Most dismiss it as winter madness.
- **The lost expedition**: Commander Kaleva Seppanen led forty soldiers north six weeks ago. No word since. The Crown is assembling a follow-up party. This is not unusual — the Nalkamaa swallows expeditions — but forty soldiers is a lot to lose.

## Creatures of the Nalkamaa

- **Snow elk**: Massive, white-furred elk. Primary game animal. Dangerous during rut.
- **Frost wolves**: Pack hunters, intelligent, territorial. Respect fire and large groups. A lone traveler is prey.
- **Whisper owls**: Large arctic owls whose calls carry for miles. Hunters use their calls to gauge distance and weather. Silence from the owls means something worse is out there.
- **Permafrost crawlers**: Centipede-like creatures that live in the frozen soil. Mostly harmless unless you camp on a nest. Their emergence in unusual numbers is considered a bad omen.

## Distances and Travel

- **Kylmaharju to Harmaakallio**: Two weeks south on the northern road (wagon pace)
- **Kylmaharju to Valkeasuo**: Five days south on the northern road
- **Kylmaharju to Tsikhe-Dzveli**: Approximately eight days north through trackless wilderness (the expedition's estimated route)
- **Kylmaharju to K'ldekari**: Unknown to most; the Wardens don't share directions

Travel in the Nalkamaa is slow. There are no roads north of Kylmaharju. Snow depth, ice conditions, and weather dictate pace more than distance. Summer travel is faster but complicated by meltwater, mud, and insects. Winter travel is slower but the frozen ground is more predictable. Spring and autumn are the most dangerous — thin ice, sudden storms, unpredictable terrain.
