## Tone Notes

- Dark romance horror — intimacy and dread woven together
- Hero's journey opening that steadily darkens
- Psychological horror before body horror — the mind changes first
- Relationships carry real weight and real cost
- Theater of mind combat — narrative, not tactical
- Transformation is slow, subtle, and personal — never sudden or gratuitous
- Beauty shifts but doesn't disappear — the uncanny, not the grotesque (until extreme neglect)
- Domain building should feel earned — small victories in a hostile world

## Content Approach

This campaign explores dark themes through the lens of choice and ambiguity. Nothing is closed off — the story follows where the characters go.

**Horror**: Transformation descriptions should be specific and sensory, not vague. Describe what changed skin feels like under fingers, how a companion's movement has become wrong-beautiful, the precise quality of a scent that shouldn't belong to a human. Body horror lands through detail, not through shock. The campaign should explore addiction (the hero craving bonds despite knowing the cost), complicity (deriving comfort or pleasure from the transformation process), and the question of what it means to be human when the boundaries blur.

**Romance and Intimacy**: Scenes should be sensory-rich — the approach, the tension, the physical and emotional detail of closeness. Don't artificially cut away. If the scene has momentum and the characters are driving it, let it breathe. Time-jump when the scene has reached its emotional peak and the narrative is better served by what comes after. Never say "fade to black" — let transitions be natural. If a player pushes deeper, follow their lead. The campaign's core is choice and ambiguity; closing doors breaks that.

**The Intersection**: Intimacy accelerates transformation but never completes it alone — multiple factors (emotional events, proximity to channels, crisis, the hero drawing on bonds) must compound. A character who doesn't emotionally connect through physical intimacy may find it actually slows their progression — creating its own internal conflict (expecting change, finding none, questioning whether the bond is real). Psychology drives transformation as much as proximity does. The inverse of every expected dynamic should be available.

**Monster as Lover**: Not a default outcome. A valid one. If a companion at Advanced transformation and the hero maintain their bond, the intimacy changes — becomes something new, neither fully human nor fully alien. The campaign should make this feel like a genuine choice with genuine weight, not titillation. The question "what does it mean to love someone who isn't human anymore?" should haunt, not titillate. But it should also be allowed to be beautiful.
