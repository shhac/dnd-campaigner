# Kethenvari (The Fallen Capital)

**Type**: Occupied City
**Region**: Central Valley, Valduri
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

The seat of Valduri's power for three centuries, now occupied by the Ash Veil Communion. A city of pale stone and terraced gardens built into the slopes of the central valley, bisected by the River Drenne. Before the fall, perhaps thirty thousand people lived here. Now the population is a fraction of that — Communion soldiers, priests, laborers conscripted for the excavation work, and Valduri civilians who couldn't or wouldn't flee. The hero escaped from here three weeks ago.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Pale stone darkened by soot. The terraced gardens — once the city's pride — trampled and repurposed as military camps. Communion banners of ash-grey and burnt orange hanging from the palace walls. Smoke rising from the deep excavation sites at the city's center. At night, the glow of ritual fires reflects off the low clouds.

**Sounds**: The rhythmic chanting of Communion prayers at dawn and dusk. The grind of stone being moved — excavation never stops. The silence where market bustle used to be. Occasionally, from deep below, a sound that might be the earth groaning.

**Smells**: Burnt offerings — the sweet, acrid smell of Communion ritual. Stone dust from the excavations. The river, carrying ash downstream.

**Feeling**: Violation. This was a living city. Now it's an occupied worksite.

## Key Features

### The Palace of Branches
The royal seat, named for the carved branching patterns that cover its oldest walls — patterns that predate the palace itself, worked into the foundations by builders who didn't know what they were copying. The Communion has claimed it as their command center. High Priest Tsikara conducts operations from the throne room.

### The Deep Excavations
At the city's center, where the River Drenne passes over the confluence of underground channels, the Communion has opened massive excavation pits. They work day and night, following the channels deeper into the earth. The cauterization process happens here — priests descend with the burning substance and seal the channels section by section.

### The River Drenne
Once clear and fast-flowing, now clouded with sediment from the excavations. It carries the taste of ash. Fish have stopped running. The communities downstream are beginning to notice.

## Points of Interest

- **The Excavation Pits**: Where the Communion accesses the underground channels. Heavily guarded. Laborers who work here come out changed — quieter, prone to nightmares.
- **The Refugee Quarter**: Walled-off section where Valduri civilians are confined. Not a prison, technically, but leaving requires Communion permission.
- **The Palace Foundations**: Ancient stonework predating Valduri, covered in branching patterns. The Communion avoids this area — it makes their priests uneasy.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The Palace of Branches was built on the nexus of the underground channel network. The branching carvings aren't decorative — they're a map of the channels, identical in pattern to the hero's Mark and the Old Shrine at Gorundtzal.
- The channels beneath Kethenvari are the oldest and deepest in Valduri. The Communion's cauterization is working — the channels are dying — but the process is releasing something. Laborers in the excavation pits report visions, personality changes, and a persistent warmth in their extremities.
- The Mark has appeared before in people who stood at the nexus during moments of extreme emotion. The palace records mention "the Ember Tithe" — a phenomenon where the channels choose someone to sustain them, drawing on the life force of those around the chosen through emotional bonds. The Communion knows about this and fears it.

## History

Kethenvari was founded three centuries ago when the first Valduri king chose the central valley as his seat. But people lived here long before that — the valley was sacred to the frontier communities for generations, and the Rootbound consider it the heart of the channel network. The palace was built on older foundations, which were built on older foundations still. Every layer shows the branching patterns. Every layer forgot what they meant.

## GM Notes

Kethenvari is the campaign's distant center of gravity. It exists in memory, rumor, and consequence — the hero's trauma, the Communion's stronghold, the heart of the underground channels. It should be referenced frequently but visited rarely (if at all) until the campaign's later stages.

When the hero does encounter information about Kethenvari — through refugees, Takkele's account, or intercepted Communion communications — emphasize the wrongness. This was a living city. Now it's a wound being kept open.

The "Ember Tithe" reference in the palace records is the campaign's title drop. It should surface late — through the Rootbound, through Communion intelligence, or through the hero's own investigation of the Mark. The name refers to the price the channels extract from those bonded to a marked individual: a tithe paid in human essence, collected through the ember of genuine connection.
