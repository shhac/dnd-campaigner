# Gorundtzal

**Type**: Ruined Waystation / Emerging Settlement
**Region**: Northern Frontier, Valduri
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

A waystation town on the northern trade road, two days' ride from the mountain passes. Before the fall, it serviced merchant caravans heading north — stone walls, a market square, stables, warehouses, an inn. A Communion advance patrol swept through ten days ago, raiding supplies and burning what they couldn't carry. They didn't garrison it — too far from their lines, too few soldiers to spare. What remains is a defensible shell: walls scarred by fire, buildings with holes in their roofs, and a well that still draws clean water. About thirty survivors have gathered here, drawn by the walls and the water and the absence of better options.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Blackened stone walls with the pale bones of the original construction showing through. The market square is empty except for a few salvaged crates arranged as a makeshift supply station. Cook fires at dusk — the only warmth. People moving with the careful purposelessness of refugees who haven't yet found a routine.

**Sounds**: Wind through broken rooflines. Muted conversation — no one speaks loudly anymore. The creak of the well mechanism. At night, the frontier forest pressing close: owl calls, branch-crack, the distant sound of something large moving through undergrowth.

**Smells**: Woodsmoke, always. The mineral cleanness of well water. The sour undertone of too many unwashed bodies in close quarters. Occasionally, from the south, the faintest scent of something burnt and chemical — the Communion's camps, carried on the wind.

**Feeling**: Precarious hope. This place could become something. It could also collapse. Everyone here is waiting to see which way it tips.

## Key Features

### The Walls
Gorundtzal's stone walls were built to stop bandits, not armies, but they're solid — six feet high, two feet thick, with a proper gate that still closes (though the hinges need work). The Communion patrol broke through the south-facing section, leaving a gap of about fifteen feet. This is the settlement's most critical vulnerability.

### The Well
Deep, cold, reliable. The well predates the waystation — some say it predates Valduri itself. The water tastes faintly of iron and is always a few degrees cooler than it should be. It's the reason anyone settles here. The well mechanism is hand-cranked, built from iron and hardwood, and still functional.

### The Market Square
An open area paved with flat stone, surrounded by the shells of merchant buildings. Before the fall, traders would set up stalls here. Now it serves as the settlement's common area — supply distribution, cooking, sleeping for those without a roof. Currodze has organized the remaining supplies along the north wall of the square.

### The Inn (The Tallow Post)
The largest building, partially intact. Its ground floor still has three walls and most of a roof — the fourth wall faces the square and was never more than a serving counter. The upper floor is exposed to weather. Before the fall, it was a modest roadside inn. Now it serves as the closest thing Gorundtzal has to a common hall.

### The Stables
Stone-built and largely intact — horses are valuable, so the Communion took the horses but didn't bother burning the building. Currently being used as overflow shelter. Room for perhaps twenty people if they don't mind the smell.

## Points of Interest

- **The Gap**: The breach in the south wall. Whoever repairs it earns the settlement's trust — and takes the first step toward making Gorundtzal defensible.
- **The Root Cellar**: Beneath the inn. Partially collapsed but accessible. Contains the remains of the inn's stores — mostly empty, but worth checking. Deeper than expected.
- **The Watchtower**: A squat stone tower at the northeast corner of the walls. Its upper level is intact and provides a view of the northern road and surrounding terrain. Currently unoccupied.
- **The Old Shrine**: A flat stone in the market square with faded carvings — pre-Valduri, possibly much older. Frontier folk leave small offerings here. The carvings show branching patterns that might remind the hero of the Mark.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Refugee arrivals**: New survivors trickle in every few days. Some are helpful. Some are desperate. A few may be Communion informants.
- **Supply disputes**: Not enough food, blankets, or shelter. Currodze manages this, but tensions rise.
- **Frontier wildlife**: The forests close to Gorundtzal hold wolves, boar, and elk. Hunting is possible but risky — gunshots (if anyone has a weapon) attract attention.

### Possible Encounters
- **Communion patrol**: A mounted patrol passes within sight of Gorundtzal every five to seven days. They haven't returned in force yet. The question is when, not if.
- **Bandit scouts**: Not everyone displaced by the fall is a good person. Small groups of desperate or predatory survivors probe the settlement's defenses.
- **Rootbound contact**: Someone from the western forest communities makes contact — cautiously, cryptically, on their terms.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The Old Shrine's carvings are connected to the underground channels that run beneath Valduri. The branching patterns are a map — not of geography, but of the channel network. No one alive knows how to read it.
- The root cellar beneath the inn goes deeper than it should. Below the collapsed section, there's an older structure — a pre-Valduri chamber that connects to the channel network. The entrance is currently blocked by rubble but could be cleared.
- The well draws from the same underground source that feeds the channels. This is why the water tastes of iron and stays cold. If the Communion's cauterization reaches this far north, the well will fail.

## History

Gorundtzal has been a waystation for longer than Valduri has been a kingdom. The name is old — frontier dialect, meaning roughly "the gathering place" or "where roads meet." The stone foundations and the well predate the current buildings by centuries. Before it was a trade waystation, it was a meeting place for the frontier communities. Before that, the Rootbound say, it was something else — a place where the land's veins came close to the surface. They built the shrine here, and then forgot why.

## GM Notes

Gorundtzal is the campaign's home base and the center of the domain-building arc. It should start miserable and gradually improve through the hero's actions. Key milestones:

1. **Repairing the wall gap** — first major project, earns trust
2. **Organizing the survivors** — establishing roles, rules, and purpose
3. **Securing food sources** — hunting, foraging, or trade arrangements
4. **Establishing defenses** — the watchtower, patrols, early warning
5. **Discovering the deeper chamber** — connects the settlement to the campaign's larger mysteries

The Old Shrine is a deliberate visual echo of the hero's Mark. If the hero examines it closely, describe the branching patterns in terms that mirror their own mark — this seeds the connection without explaining it. The shrine should feel old and significant, not magical.

The root cellar / deeper chamber is a mid-campaign discovery. Don't rush it. Let the hero stumble on it through necessity (need for storage, expansion, or shelter during an attack) rather than investigation.

Communion patrols should create persistent low-level tension. The patrol schedule is predictable enough to plan around, which makes the eventual deviation from that schedule (when the Communion decides to take Gorundtzal seriously) genuinely frightening.
