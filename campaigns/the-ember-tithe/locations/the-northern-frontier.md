# The Northern Frontier

**Type**: Region / Wilderness
**Region**: Northern Valduri, between the settled provinces and the mountain passes
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

The borderlands between settled Valduri and the wild uplands. Before the fall, this was trade country — merchant caravans heading north to the mountain passes, waystation towns every day's ride, watchtowers on the hilltops. Now the trade has stopped, the waystations are abandoned or repurposed as refugee camps, and the watchtowers stand empty. The Communion's presence thins here — patrols, not garrisons. For survivors willing to endure the frontier's hardships, this is the closest thing to freedom left in Valduri.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Dense mixed forest broken by rocky clearings and stream valleys. Old stone watchtowers on hilltops, some intact, some crumbling. The trade road — once maintained, now deteriorating — cuts north through the trees like a scar. Abandoned waystations, their doors hanging open. On clear days, the mountains are visible to the north, snow already on the peaks.

**Sounds**: Forest sounds dominate — birdsong, wind in canopy, running water. The trade road is quiet where it used to carry the creak and rumble of carts. At night, wolves. Occasionally, the distant sound of hooves — Communion patrol, merchant, or bandit, and there's no telling which until they're close.

**Smells**: Pine and loam. The mineral sharpness of mountain streams. Woodsmoke from hidden camps. The frontier smells alive in a way the occupied provinces don't anymore.

**Feeling**: Wild sovereignty. The land doesn't belong to anyone right now, and it doesn't care who claims it.

## Key Features

### The Trade Road
The main artery connecting Valduri's central provinces to the northern mountain passes. Built and maintained by the merchant guilds for two centuries. Stone-paved in the lowlands, packed earth in the hills. Now unmaintained, with washouts, fallen trees, and the occasional deliberate obstruction. Still the fastest way north.

### The Watchtower Chain
A line of stone watchtowers built during border conflicts a century ago, spaced about half a day's ride apart along the frontier's eastern edge. Most are intact — they were built to last. They offer shelter, high ground, and line-of-sight communication (signal fires). Currently unoccupied except by birds and squirrels.

### The Western Forests
Old-growth forest that deepens and darkens as it stretches west. The Rootbound communities live here — scattered, secretive, connected to the land in ways that mainstream Valduri finds uncomfortable. The forest is dense enough to hide in, rich enough to live in (if you know how), and old enough to hold secrets.

### The Mountain Passes
Two days north of Gorundtzal. The passes lead to the mountain clans — neutral peoples who trade with Valduri but owe it no loyalty. They've closed the passes to large groups but still allow individual travelers and small parties. Potential trade partners or refuge of last resort.

## Points of Interest

- **The Supply Caches**: The merchant guilds maintained hidden supply caches along the trade road — emergency stores in case of weather or banditry. Most were raided during the fall, but Pellinaste knows the locations of two that might still be intact.
- **The Old Watchtower (Haelford Tower)**: The best-preserved tower in the chain, two hours east of Gorundtzal. Solid walls, intact roof, a cistern on the upper level. Could serve as an outpost or early warning station.
- **The Rootbound Grove**: A clearing in the western forest where the trees grow in a perfect circle around a flat stone covered in branching patterns. The Rootbound use this as a meeting place. The hero's Mark responds to this place — a warmth, a recognition.
- **The Grey Patch**: A circle of dead earth in the western forest, perfectly round, where nothing grows. The soil is warm and smells of ash. This is the Grey Wasting — proof that the Communion's claims about underground corruption aren't entirely fabricated.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Refugees**: Moving north in small groups, looking for safety. Some are skilled (smiths, farmers, soldiers). Some are just scared.
- **Communion Patrols**: Mounted groups of 6-8 soldiers, passing through every 5-7 days. Disciplined, not looking for a fight, but will investigate anything unusual.
- **Wildlife**: Wolves, boar, elk, and the occasional bear. The forest is healthy and wild. Good hunting for those with skill and patience.

### Possible Encounters
- **Bandit Groups**: Displaced people turned predatory. Small (3-5), desperate, poorly equipped. A threat to individuals, not to a prepared group.
- **Communion Deserters**: Soldiers who've had enough. Dangerous because they're trained, desperate, and know Communion tactics.
- **Rootbound Watchers**: Glimpsed at the edge of vision. The western forest communities watch the frontier carefully and approach only those they choose.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The underground channel network runs beneath the entire frontier. The channels are closer to the surface here than in the central provinces — this is why the frontier has always felt different, why things grow faster, why the water tastes of iron. The Rootbound know this. Everyone else just calls it "the old ways."
- The Grey Patch in the western forest is where a channel node has already been cauterized — not by the Communion (they haven't reached this far north), but by something else. The Rootbound know what did it and won't say willingly.
- The watchtower chain was built on channel nodes. The original builders didn't know this — they chose the hilltops for tactical reasons and the channels beneath made the stones easier to quarry and stack. But the towers resonate faintly with the network, and someone attuned to the channels (like the hero, eventually) can feel it.

## History

The northern frontier has always been liminal — between civilization and wilderness, between Valduri and the mountain clans, between the settled world and whatever came before it. The Rootbound say people have lived here since before Valduri, since before the frontier had a name, tending the channels the way farmers tend fields. Mainstream Valduri dismissed this as mysticism. The Communion calls it evidence of corruption. The truth, as usual, is more complicated.

## GM Notes

The frontier is the campaign's sandbox. It provides:
- **Exploration**: Watchtowers, supply caches, Rootbound sites, ruins, and wilderness encounters
- **Resources**: Hunting, foraging, salvage, and trade with the mountain clans
- **Threats**: Communion patrols, bandits, wildlife, weather, and the slow approach of winter
- **Mystery**: The Grey Patch, the Rootbound, the channel network, and the Mark's resonance with the land

Let the frontier feel big and real. Not everything is plot-relevant. Some encounters are just survival — hunting for food, repairing a bridge, dealing with a wolf that's been stalking the camp. These mundane challenges ground the supernatural horror and make the domain-building feel earned.

The Rootbound should approach the party, not the other way around. The hero's Mark draws their attention. Their first contact should be cryptic and slightly unsettling — they know something about the hero that the hero doesn't know about themselves, and they're not sure whether to help or to run.
