# The Mark — GM Guide

**Classification**: GM-Only. Do NOT share this file with player agents.

This is the mechanical backbone of The Ember Tithe. Every session, the Mark drives the campaign's central tensions: connection vs. consumption, intimacy vs. horror, the hero's survival vs. their companions' humanity. Track it carefully. Narrate it consistently.

---

## Bond Formation

A bond forms when the Mark recognizes a genuine connection between the hero and another person. Not every relationship qualifies. The Mark is selective — it responds to authenticity, not proximity.

### Bond Criteria (3 of 5 must be met)

1. **Mutual vulnerability** — Both parties have shared something true about themselves. Not performative honesty — actual risk. A confession that could be used against them, a fear they haven't spoken aloud, a desire they're ashamed of.

2. **Chosen presence** — The companion chose to be near the hero when they could have left. Not obligation, not circumstance — a deliberate decision to stay, return, or seek out.

3. **Emotional resonance** — A moment where both parties felt the same thing at the same time. Shared grief over a loss, shared laughter at absurdity, shared rage at injustice. The feeling must be genuine and recognized by both.

4. **Physical catalyst** — A moment of meaningful physical contact. Not incidental — intentional. A hand offered and taken, an embrace, a touch that communicates what words can't. The Mark responds strongly to this.

5. **Sacrifice or risk** — One party endangered themselves (physically, socially, emotionally) for the other's benefit without expectation of return.

When the third criterion is met, the Mark flares. The hero feels a rush of warmth spreading from the branching lines — pleasurable, almost overwhelming. The companion, if touching the hero, feels it too. If not touching, they feel a sudden pull toward the hero, a sense of something clicking into place.

**GM narration cues for bond formation:**
- The ember lines brighten visibly, even in daylight
- The hero's skin radiates heat that nearby people can feel
- A low hum — more felt than heard — emanates from the Mark
- Both parties experience a brief, intense sense of euphoria followed by calm
- The companion's eyes are drawn to the Mark involuntarily

### Bond Types

Each bond develops a character based on the emotional foundation it was built on. Track the type — it determines the transformation vector and the drain profile.

| Bond Type | Foundation | Drain Rate | Transformation Vector | Emotional Signature |
|-----------|-----------|------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Romantic** | Desire, intimacy, passion | High | Shifts toward the object of desire — beauty, magnetism, obsessive devotion | Consuming warmth, jealousy, need |
| **Devotional** | Admiration, faith, reverence | Medium-High | Shifts toward the idealized version the companion sees — perfection at the cost of individuality | Awe, certainty, loss of doubt |
| **Protective** | Fear of loss, guardianship, duty | Medium | Shifts toward physicality and vigilance — territorial instincts, sensory sharpening, aggression toward threats | Constant alertness, possessiveness, anger at vulnerability |
| **Professional** | Shared purpose, mutual respect, competence | Low | Shifts toward optimization — the companion becomes more effective but less human. Efficiency replaces empathy. | Clarity, detachment, impatience with weakness |
| **Reluctant** | Forced proximity, grudging respect, denial | Very Low (but volatile) | Unpredictable. Transformation direction changes based on which emotion breaks through the resistance. | Resentment, confusion, sudden flares of unexpected feeling |

Bond types can shift over time. A professional bond can deepen into devotional. A protective bond can bleed into romantic. Track the dominant type but note secondary influences.

### Bond Strength Scale

| Strength | Label | What It Looks Like |
|----------|-------|-------------------|
| 0 | No bond | Mark does not respond to this person |
| 1 | Noticed | Mark registers their presence — faint warmth, a flicker. Hero feels a pull they might ignore. |
| 2 | Recognition | Repeated proximity, early emotional exchanges. Mark warms reliably near them. Hero finds excuses to be nearby. |
| 3 | Connection | Genuine emotional exchange has occurred. Mark pulses during conversation. Companion notices warmth. First criterion met. |
| 4 | Deepening | Multiple criteria met. Bond is forming. Mark responds to the companion specifically — not just when they're close, but when the hero thinks about them. |
| 5 | Established | Bond is real. Mark tracks this companion like a compass. Warmth during contact is unmistakable. Separation causes discomfort. Psychological transformation begins. |
| 6 | Deep | Vulnerability has been shared and reciprocated. The bond sustains the hero noticeably. Companion's psychological changes are clear to anyone paying attention. |
| 7 | Profound | The bond is a defining relationship. Mark flares at the thought of this person. Early physical transformation signs appear. Separation is agonizing. |
| 8 | Consuming | The bond dominates both parties' emotional lives. Physical transformation is visible to those who know the companion well. The hero draws significant sustenance. |
| 9 | Overwhelming | The companion is changing in ways that can't be hidden or denied. The hero's stability depends heavily on this bond. Breaking it would be catastrophic. |
| 10 | Complete | Total intertwining. The companion's transformation is visible to strangers. The bond is the strongest force in both their lives. Maximum drain, maximum sustenance, maximum cost. |

## Bond Strength Progression

### What Pushes Bonds Up

- **Shared danger survived together** (+1, once per distinct threat)
- **Emotional vulnerability reciprocated** (+1 per genuine exchange, max +2 per session)
- **Physical intimacy escalation** (+1 per meaningful new threshold — first touch, first embrace, first kiss, first night)
- **Confession of something hidden** (+1, the confider must feel genuine risk)
- **Sacrifice for the other's benefit** (+1-2, scaled by cost)
- **Choosing to stay when leaving was easier** (+1)
- **Defending the other against social or political pressure** (+1)

### What Pushes Bonds Down

- **Betrayal of trust** (-2 to -4, scaled by severity)
- **Prolonged deliberate separation** (-1 per 3+ sessions apart, minimum 1)
- **Emotional withdrawal** (-1 per session of deliberate coldness or avoidance)
- **Choosing someone else in a moment that mattered** (-1 to -2)
- **Discovering the truth about the Mark** (-1 to -3, depending on how the companion processes it; may recover)

### Natural Drift

Bonds are living things. They don't hold steady.

- **Unattended bonds** (no meaningful interaction for 2+ sessions): drift -1 per session of neglect
- **Maintained bonds** (regular positive interaction): drift +0.5 per session (round down, applied every 2 sessions)
- **Strained bonds** (ongoing tension without resolution): drift -0.5 per session

The hero cannot maintain maximum strength with every companion simultaneously. This is intentional — it forces prioritization, which forces consequences.

## Hero Stability

The Mark sustains the hero's life, but it requires fuel. That fuel is the emotional energy drawn through active bonds.

### Stability Scale

| Stability | Total Bond Strength | Physical State |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| 9-10 | 20+ | Vital. The hero radiates health and charisma. The Mark glows steadily. Others are drawn to them instinctively. |
| 7-8 | 15-19 | Strong. Healthy, energetic, sharp. Occasional awareness of the Mark's warmth but no distress. |
| 5-6 | 10-14 | Functional. The hero is fine — mostly. Occasional fatigue, moments where concentration slips. The Mark's ember lines are dimmer. Others might not notice, but the hero does. |
| 3-4 | 5-9 | Declining. Visible deterioration. Pallor, persistent low-grade fever, the Mark's lines bleeding color into surrounding skin like a bruise. The hero tires quickly. Companions notice something is wrong. |
| 1-2 | 1-4 | Dying. The Mark is consuming inward. The branching pattern spreads beneath the skin — visible as dark lines radiating from the original pattern. Coughing, bleeding from the nose and gums, organ pain. Without new bonds or strengthened existing ones, death within sessions. |
| 0 | 0 | Dead. The Mark consumed its host. The body is found with the branching pattern covering every surface, frozen in ember luminescence that fades over hours. |

### Stability Calculation

Sum all active bond strengths. Apply to the table above. Recalculate after every session.

**Modifiers:**
- A bond that was strength 7+ and drops below 3 in a single session: additional -2 to stability (shock of severance)
- A bond broken by death: additional -3 to stability, persists for 2 sessions before normalizing
- Hero actively restraining a bond (choosing to limit intimacy to slow transformation): that bond contributes only half its strength to stability

### GM Narration Cues by Stability Tier

**9-10**: The hero looks better than they should, given the circumstances. Wounds heal slightly faster. Their voice carries. People trust them instinctively. Lean into the magnetism — this is the Mark at its most seductive.

**7-8**: Normal. The hero is healthy and capable. The Mark is a background warmth. No special narration needed unless contrasting with a previous lower state.

**5-6**: Introduce fatigue at inconvenient moments. A hand that trembles when reaching for something. A thought that slips away mid-sentence. The hero feels cold when away from companions. These should be noticeable but not alarming.

**3-4**: This is a crisis. The hero sweats through their clothes. The Mark's lines look inflamed, bruised. Blood appears at the corners of the mouth during exertion. Companions should be visibly worried. NPCs who see the hero comment on how unwell they look.

**1-2**: The hero is dying and everyone can see it. They cannot hide it. Movement is painful. The Mark is spreading — new lines appear daily, creeping across the torso, down the other arm, up the neck. The hero coughs blood. Sleep brings nightmares of being consumed from the inside.

## Bond Capacity

There is no hard cap on the number of bonds the hero can form. The Mark will accept any genuine connection.

However, more bonds create competing pressures:

- **More bonds = more stability** — Total bond strength rises, keeping the hero healthier
- **More bonds = more transformation** — Every bonded companion is being drained. More bonds means more people changing.
- **More bonds = more emotional bandwidth consumed** — The hero feels every bond. Five profound bonds means five sources of intense emotion, five people whose feelings the hero experiences at heightened intensity. This is exhausting and can lead to emotional numbness as a coping mechanism.
- **More bonds = harder maintenance** — Natural drift means unattended bonds weaken. The hero must invest time and emotional energy in each bond to maintain it.

The ideal number varies by player approach:
- **2-3 deep bonds** (strength 7+): High stability, intense transformation, manageable attention
- **4-5 moderate bonds** (strength 4-6): Good stability, slower transformation, stretched thin
- **Many shallow bonds** (strength 1-3): Unstable — shallow bonds contribute little and drift down fast

## The Mark's Deeper Nature (Act 3 Revelation)

**Gate**: Do NOT reveal this information until Act 3. Read `story-arcs/UNLOCK.md` for preconditions.

### Connection to the Channel Network

The Mark is not a curse, a blessing, or a disease. It is an interface.

Beneath Valduri — beneath the entire continent — runs a network of channels. The Rootbound call them the veins of the land. The Communion calls them corruption. Neither is wrong. The channels carry something that is simultaneously life-force, magical energy, and ecological substrate. Everything that grows in Valduri draws from them. The Grey Wasting spreading across the Communion's homeland is what happens when channels die.

The Mark connects its bearer to this network. The bearer becomes a living node — drawing energy from the channels through human connection. Bonds are the conduit. Emotion is the current. The transformation that companions undergo is not decay — it is the channel energy expressing itself through human vessels, reshaping them toward forms that are more efficient conduits.

This is the symbiosis interpretation: the Mark creates a human network that mirrors the underground channels, ensuring the land's energy flows through people as well as stone. The bearer and their bonded companions become a living extension of the ecosystem. The transformation is not consumption — it is integration.

### The Parasitism Interpretation

But there is another reading, and it is equally valid.

The Mark takes. It amplifies the bearer's emotions because heightened feeling creates stronger bonds. It makes connection feel euphoric because the Mark needs bonds to survive. It transforms companions because it is consuming something essential from them and replacing it with channel energy that serves the network's needs, not theirs.

The bearer is not a node — they are a lure. The bonds are not symbiosis — they are feeding tubes. The companions don't become part of an ecosystem — they lose themselves to it.

Both interpretations are true. Both are incomplete. The campaign's final revelation is that the distinction between symbiosis and parasitism depends entirely on the bearer's choices.

### Previous Bearers

The Mark has appeared before. At least three times in recorded or remembered history.

**The First Bearer** (approximately 400 years ago) — Name lost. A Rootbound elder who bore the Mark during a period of channel instability. They formed bonds with an entire community — dozens of people, all shallow, all consenting. The community transformed collectively into something between human and elemental, tending the channels as living gardeners. The Rootbound remember this as a golden age. Others remember it as the generation that stopped being human. Outcome: **the first path** — willing collective integration.

**Mesfin** (approximately 200 years ago) — The most documented bearer. A man who loved three companions deeply: Birukta (who loved fiercely), Zelalem (who loved gently), and Dessalegn (who resisted longest). Rather than fight the transformation or let it consume them, Mesfin chose what the Rootbound call "the third path" — he integrated with the channel network. His three companions followed. They became the old-growth forest west of Gorundtzal. The oaks are Birukta. The willows are Zelalem. The thorns are Dessalegn. The Rootbound have tended this forest for two centuries as a sacred duty. See `npcs/the-previous-bearer.md` for full details. Outcome: **the third path** — willing individual integration, companions followed.

**Note on names**: The current Rootbound elder Birukta and the young tender Dessalegn share names with Mesfin's companions. Whether this is tradition, spiritual invocation, lineage, or something the channels arranged is deliberately ambiguous. The GM should lean into this ambiguity — it unsettles the hero.

**The Unwilling Bearer** (approximately 80 years ago) — A farmer's daughter whose name the Rootbound will not speak. She bore the Mark and refused it. She isolated herself, formed no bonds, and died within a year as the Mark consumed her. Her body was found in the manner described in the stability scale — branching pattern covering every surface, frozen in fading ember light. The Rootbound buried her at a channel junction. A grove grew from the site within a season, unnaturally lush. Something of her persists there — not alive, not dead, but part of the network against her will. Outcome: **refusal** — the Mark takes what it needs regardless.

### Why the Communion's Cauterization Awakened It

The Mark appears when the channel network is threatened. It is the network's immune response — or its reproductive strategy, depending on interpretation. The Communion's campaign to cauterize the channels is the most severe threat the network has faced in centuries. The Mark activated in the chaos of Kethenvari's fall because the burning of the capital destabilized a major channel junction beneath the city.

The hero did not earn the Mark. They were simply nearest to the junction when it broke. The network chose proximity over worthiness. Whether this makes the hero a chosen one or a convenient victim is — like everything else about the Mark — a matter of interpretation.

## Running the Mark: Session-by-Session Principles

1. **The Mark should feel good before it feels terrible.** Players should want bonds. The warmth, the connection, the stability — these are real rewards. Don't rush to the horror. Let the hook set deep.

2. **Track bonds quietly.** Don't announce strength changes to players. Let them infer from narration — the Mark brightens, the Mark dims, the hero feels stronger, the hero feels cold.

3. **Transformation is always the companion's story.** The hero causes it, but the companion experiences it. Give companions agency in how they process their changes.

4. **Every bond should feel different.** A romantic bond with one companion should not feel like a romantic bond with another. The Mark responds to the specific dynamic, not the category.

5. **The hero's stability is a narrative lever, not a punishment.** Declining stability creates urgency and forces choices. It should never feel like the GM is punishing the player for making interesting decisions.

6. **Recovery exists.** Never let the horror feel hopeless. The costs of recovery are terrible — but they are costs, not impossibilities. The campaign's emotional core requires that fighting for the people you love matters, even when it hurts.
