# The Mark

**Type**: Supernatural Phenomenon / Bond Catalyst
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## What It Looks Like

A branching pattern across the hero's chest and left arm, like the veins of a burning leaf pressed into skin. The lines are dark — near-black in daylight — but threaded with a faint ember glow visible in low light. The pattern is organic, asymmetric, and beautiful in a way that unsettles people who look too long. It cannot be covered by ink, scarred over, or magically concealed. Clothing hides it, but the hero always feels it.

The Mark is warm to the touch. Not feverish — more like sun-warmed stone. Someone pressing a hand to the hero's chest would feel it immediately.

## Observable Behaviors

**Near certain people**: The Mark warms noticeably when the hero is near someone they're forming a genuine connection with. The hero feels a pull — not compulsive, but persistent. Like recognizing someone you've never met. This pull is strongest during moments of emotional honesty.

**In low light**: The ember threading brightens. Firelight, candlelight, twilight — the Mark becomes faintly luminous. Not enough to read by, but enough to notice. The glow pulses slowly, like breathing.

**During strong emotion**: The pattern flares. Grief, desire, fury, tenderness — the stronger the feeling, the brighter the lines. The hero can sometimes feel the branching spread slightly at its edges during intense moments, though it always settles back.

**During physical contact with bonded companions**: The warmth intensifies and companions report feeling it radiate through their own skin. Brief touches produce a pleasant heat. Sustained contact — holding hands, an embrace — creates a deep, almost narcotic warmth that both parties find difficult to break.

## What the Hero Experiences

The Mark amplifies everything the hero feels. This began during the fall of Kethenvari and hasn't stopped.

- **Joy hits harder.** A shared laugh over a campfire feels euphoric. The hero's mood lifts the room.
- **Grief is devastating.** Loss doesn't dull over time the way it should. The hero carries every death, every ruin, at full intensity.
- **Desire is overwhelming.** Attraction isn't a gentle pull — it's a tide. The hero must actively manage their responses to people they find compelling.
- **Rage burns clean.** Anger arrives without hesitation and with terrible clarity. The hero knows exactly what they want to do. Restraint takes effort.

Beyond the amplification, the hero feels a sense of rightness when connecting with others. Sharing something true — a confession, a fear, a moment of unguarded honesty — produces a sensation like warmth flooding through the Mark and settling deep in the chest. It feels like being fed after starving.

The hero may also notice that they feel physically worse when alone for extended periods. Fatigue, low-grade headaches, a chill that won't break. Being near their companions alleviates this. The hero might attribute this to loneliness, to the trauma of the fall, to anything but the Mark. For now.

## What Companions Might Notice

Companions bonded to the hero experience subtle effects they may not immediately connect to the Mark:

- **Warmth**: Standing near the hero feels good. Touching the hero feels better. There's a physical comfort to proximity that goes beyond normal companionship.
- **Being seen**: Companions report feeling deeply understood by the hero — as if the hero perceives them more clearly than anyone else ever has. This is intoxicating and a little frightening.
- **Dreams**: Bonded companions occasionally have vivid dreams they can't quite remember upon waking. The emotional residue lingers — warmth, longing, a sense of being pulled toward something vast.
- **The pull**: Over time, companions feel an increasing reluctance to be far from the hero. This manifests as anxiety during separation, relief upon reunion, and a quiet dread at the thought of leaving permanently.
- **Energy**: Some companions notice they tire more easily than they used to, or that their concentration wanders in ways it didn't before. This is subtle and easily attributed to the hardship of frontier life.

## What the Party Doesn't Know Yet

- Why the Mark appeared on this particular person during the fall
- Whether the Mark can be removed, and what that would mean
- What the Rootbound know about it (they clearly recognize it, but won't speak plainly)
- Why the Communion seems to fear it — and whether their fear is justified
- Whether the warmth the hero feels when bonding is the Mark rewarding them, or the Mark feeding
- What happened to anyone who bore a similar mark before
