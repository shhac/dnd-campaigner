# Domain Building — GM Guide

> **GM Eyes Only.** This document contains the full mechanical system for Gorundtzal's development, including hidden effects tied to the Mark.

## Gorundtzal — Settlement Status

```
Population: 30
Morale: 3  (1=desertion, 5=functional, 10=thriving)
Defenses: 2  (1=open, 5=basic walls, 10=fortified)
Supplies: 2  (1=starving, 5=adequate, 10=surplus)
Reputation: 1  (1=unknown, 5=known refuge, 10=regional power)
```

Update these values at the end of each session. Record changes and their causes in `story-state.md` so the trajectory is visible over time.

## Improvement Projects

Projects are how the party shapes Gorundtzal. They aren't menu options — they emerge from play. But when a player says "I want to fix the wall," this table tells you what that takes and what it yields.

| Project | Requires | Duration | Effect |
|---------|----------|----------|--------|
| Repair the Wall | 10+ laborers, timber | 2 sessions | Defenses +2, Morale +1 |
| Clear the Well | 5 laborers | 1 session | Supplies +1 (reliable water) |
| Organize Survivors | Leadership check (hero) | 1 session | Morale +1, unlocks delegation |
| Establish Hunting Parties | 3+ able-bodied, 1 tracker | Ongoing | Supplies +1 per session |
| Repair Shelters | 8+ laborers, materials | 2 sessions | Morale +1, winter protection |
| Build Watchtower | 15+ laborers, stone/timber | 3 sessions | Defenses +1, early warning |
| Trade Route (Pellinaste) | Trust with Pellinaste, goods to trade | Ongoing | Supplies +1, Reputation +1 |
| Rootbound Alliance | Complete Beat 6, trust built | Varies | Access to grounding rituals, frontier knowledge |
| Excavate Deeper Chamber | Discover it first, 5+ brave laborers | 2 sessions | Reveals channel node, Mark reacts strongly |

### Running Projects in Play

Projects don't need detailed scene time unless something goes wrong. Between sessions, ask: "What is Gorundtzal working on?" Narrate progress in opening descriptions. Save detailed scenes for complications — the timber run that encounters a Communion patrol, the well repair that uncovers something strange, the leadership moment where two factions disagree about priorities.

Multiple projects can run simultaneously if population supports it. Each laborer committed to a project is unavailable for others. This forces prioritization.

"Organize Survivors" is a prerequisite for efficiency. Before it's completed, the hero must personally oversee any project (limiting them to one at a time). After, they can delegate — freeing them for adventuring while Gorundtzal improves in the background.

## Population Growth

### Base Growth
- **Sessions 1-4**: +5-10 refugees per session (word hasn't spread far, but the frontier is full of displaced people)
- **Sessions 5-8**: +3-8 per session (early rush slows, remaining refugees are further away or more cautious)
- **Sessions 9+**: +1-5 per session (most nearby refugees have arrived or settled elsewhere)

### Modifiers
- **Reputation**: Each point above 3 adds +2 to arrival rate. Below 3, subtract 2.
- **Morale**: If Morale drops below 3, departures offset arrivals. Below 2, net population loss.
- **Specific events**: A Communion atrocity in a nearby area drives a surge (+15-20). A competing settlement failing sends its people here (+10-15). Word reaching a Valduri Remnant cell brings organized arrivals (+5-10 plus skilled individuals).

### The Mark's Effect on Growth

The hero's amplified charisma is not metaphorical. People are drawn to them — literally. Refugees who meet the hero are more likely to stay. Settlers who interact with them regularly feel a pull to remain, a conviction that this place is worth building. Population grows faster than it should for a ruined waystation on a frozen frontier.

This is a feature AND a warning. More people near the hero means more ambient influence from the Mark. The settlement's unusual cohesion is borrowed from the hero's bond network. If the hero leaves for an extended period, morale drops faster than it should. If the hero weakens (bonds severed, Mark suppressed), the settlement's unnatural resilience falters.

Don't explain this mechanically to the player. Let them notice that people seem unusually loyal, that Gorundtzal feels more alive than it should, that strangers arrive saying they "heard about a place up north" without being able to explain how.

## Crisis Events

When stats drop to critical levels or dramatic circumstances demand it, introduce a crisis. Roll or choose based on what serves the story.

| Trigger | Crisis | If Unresolved |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Supplies ≤ 2 | **Starvation** — rationing required, theft from stores | Morale -2, Population -5 |
| Morale ≤ 2 | **Desertion** — families pack up and leave | Population -10, Reputation -1 |
| Defenses ≤ 2 + Communion patrol | **Raid** — Communion patrol tests the weak point | Supplies -2, possible casualties |
| Reputation ≥ 7 | **Unwanted attention** — Communion command notices Gorundtzal | Military response escalation |
| Population ≥ 50 | **Faction tensions** — old Valduri class divisions resurface | Morale -1 unless hero mediates |
| Population ≥ 50 | **Mark ambient effect** — settlers near hero are unusually cooperative, outsiders notice | Suspicion, potential fear of the hero |

### Running Crises

Crises are scenes, not stat penalties. The stat changes above are what happens if the party ignores the problem or fails to address it. If they engage — rationing food fairly, convincing families to stay, organizing a defense — the crisis becomes a character moment instead of a mechanical loss.

The best crises force choices between stats. Sharing supplies with new arrivals costs Supplies but gains Reputation and Morale. Fortifying the walls pulls laborers from shelter repair. Sending hunters out weakens defenses. Every improvement has a cost somewhere else.

## Seasonal Calendar

Time in The Ember Tithe is tracked loosely by weeks. One session covers roughly 1-2 weeks of in-game time, depending on pacing.

| Week | Season | Event | Effect |
|------|--------|-------|--------|
| 0 | Late autumn | Campaign begins | Baseline stats |
| 2 | Late autumn | First frost | Outdoor work slowed, shelter priority increases |
| 4 | Early winter | First snow | Travel penalties, supply consumption +50% |
| 6 | Winter | Deep cold sets in | Without shelter repairs: Morale -1/session, possible illness |
| 8 | Deep winter | Hardest month | Supplies consumed at double rate, patrols rare (Communion retreats to garrisons) |
| 10 | Late winter | Thaw begins | Travel resumes, spring refugees arrive, Communion patrols resume |
| 12+ | Spring | New growth | Building season, reputation spreads faster, Communion's spring offensive? |

### Using the Calendar

Don't announce "it is now Week 4." Describe the weather changing, the frost on the stones, the first snow falling on the market square. Let the players feel the pressure without seeing the countdown.

The calendar creates natural act structure. Act 1 is the race against winter — survival, shelter, basic organization. The Communion breathing room in deep winter gives the party space for internal drama, companion development, and the first stirrings of the Mark's effects. Spring reopens the world and the threats that come with it.

Flex the timeline to serve the story. If the party is deeply engaged in a crisis at Week 4, don't force snow early. If they're coasting, accelerate.

## The Mark's Domain Effect

This is the section that matters most. Everything above is a functional settlement system. This is where it becomes horror.

### The Blessing Phase (Sessions 1-5)

The hero's presence makes Gorundtzal better. Not dramatically — subtly. In ways that feel earned.

- The well water tastes sweeter than it should. Settlers comment on it.
- Wounds heal a little faster within the walls. A cut that should take a week closes in four days.
- Crops planted (if anyone tries) sprout sooner than the season warrants.
- People sleep better here. Nightmares that plagued them on the road ease.
- Morale recovers faster from setbacks than it should.

Present all of this as the settlement working. The hero is doing a good job. People feel safe. Of course things are going well.

### The Cost Phase (Sessions 6-10)

The health of Gorundtzal is borrowed. The Mark extends through the hero's emotional connections to the land itself, drawing vitality from the surrounding territory and concentrating it within the walls.

- The forest nearest to Gorundtzal thins. Trees lose leaves early. Game moves further out.
- Hunting parties have to range further each session for the same yield.
- A frontier homestead a day's walk south fails inexplicably — crops wither, well goes brackish.
- The contrast between lush Gorundtzal and the dying frontier around it becomes visible to anyone who travels.

Don't connect these dots for the players. Let them notice. Let companions mention that the forest "feels different." Let Pellinaste comment that trade routes are drying up because the settlements along them are struggling.

### The Dependency Phase (Sessions 8+)

Settlers who spend extended time near the hero — especially those without a direct bond — develop a low-grade dependency. Not transformation. Not corruption. Just... reluctance.

- People who leave Gorundtzal to trade or scout feel uneasy away from the walls. They return sooner than planned.
- Settlers describe other places as feeling "wrong" or "empty" compared to here.
- New arrivals are struck by how content everyone seems. It's unsettling if you're paying attention.
- If the hero leaves for more than a day, morale drops a point. If they return, it recovers immediately.

This is the Mark treating Gorundtzal as an extension of the hero's bond network. The settlement isn't a community — it's a territory. The hero hasn't built a home. They've built a nest.

## Domain Building and Companion Roles

Each companion naturally gravitates toward a domain role based on their personality, skills, and the situations they encounter. These roles aren't assigned — they emerge.

- **A protective companion** becomes the settlement's defender. They organize patrols, train settlers, maintain the walls. They start thinking of Gorundtzal as theirs to guard.
- **A scholarly companion** tracks resources and plans improvements. They map the surrounding territory, catalog supplies, design building projects. They start thinking in systems rather than people.
- **A social companion** handles refugee integration and morale. They mediate disputes, welcome newcomers, organize communal meals. They start absorbing everyone's grief.
- **A pragmatic companion** handles trade, scouting, and external relations. They negotiate with Pellinaste, range into the frontier, assess threats. They start seeing people as assets.

### Domain Roles and Transformation

This is where domain building intersects with the campaign's central horror. A companion's domain role accelerates specific transformation vectors:

- The defender becomes more territorial. Protective instinct sharpens into possessiveness. They react with disproportionate aggression to perceived threats. Their body begins reflecting it — broader, harder, more imposing.
- The scholar becomes more detached. Analytical thinking crowds out empathy. They see patterns in the settlement's growth that disturb them but can't stop analyzing. Their perception sharpens unnaturally.
- The social companion becomes more empathic — past the point of health. They feel what settlers feel. A dispute in the square gives them a headache. A family's grief makes them weep. Their boundaries dissolve.
- The pragmatist becomes more calculating. Every relationship is an equation. Trust is leverage. They're effective and they hate what they're becoming.

Track which companions take which roles. When transformation moments arise, lean into the direction their role suggests. If a companion resists their role (the defender who tries to be gentle, the scholar who forces themselves to feel), that resistance is the drama.

### The Key Principle

The hero built something worth protecting. The cost of protecting it is paid by the people closest to them. The settlement thrives because the hero pours themselves into it — and the Mark pours the hero's companions into the hero.

By mid-campaign, the question isn't whether Gorundtzal survives. It's whether the people who built it are still themselves.
