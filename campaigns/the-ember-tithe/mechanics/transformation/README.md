# Transformation

**For**: Companions bonded to the hero
**What this is**: A guide to what you're experiencing. Not a mechanic. Not a system. Just honest words about something that's happening to you.

---

## Changes You Might Notice

These come first. Before anything visible, before anyone else can tell, you'll feel it.

**New urges.** Things you never wanted before, arriving without invitation. A scholar suddenly craving hard physical labor — not exercise, but the ache of it. A gentle person imagining violence in vivid detail and finding it satisfying rather than disturbing. A pragmatist stopped dead by the way light moves through water, unable to look away. These urges feel like yours. That's the unsettling part. They don't arrive as foreign objects — they settle in as though they've always been there, and you just never noticed.

**Shifted priorities.** What used to matter fades. Not dramatically — you don't wake up a different person. But the thing you were passionate about last month feels slightly less urgent. Something new has taken up residence in your attention, and it's hard to say when it arrived. You might catch yourself mid-sentence, realizing you've been talking about something you didn't care about a week ago.

**Thoughts that don't belong.** This is the one that frightens people. A thought surfaces — a desire, a judgment, a reaction — and something in you says: *that wasn't mine.* It felt like it came from somewhere adjacent. You dismiss it. It happens again. You start watching your own mind more carefully, and the watching itself becomes exhausting.

**Emotional amplification in one direction.** Not everything gets louder. One channel opens wider than the others. Maybe anger arrives faster and burns longer than it used to. Maybe desire hits you like a wall. Maybe protectiveness sharpens into something closer to possession. You notice because it's asymmetric — the rest of your emotional range stays roughly the same, but this one axis is stretching.

## Physical Signs Others Mention

People who know you well might say things. Small things. Easy to dismiss.

- "You feel warm." Said during an embrace, a hand on your arm. Not feverish. Just warmer than they expect.
- "You smell different." Not bad. Something subtle shifted. They can't name it.
- "Your eyes look different in this light." They can't say how. Might not mention it again.
- "You moved strangely just now." A turn of the head that went a degree too far. A reach that extended slightly past what looked natural. You didn't notice.
- "You haven't been sleeping well." Said because your breathing at night has changed — not restless, but oddly rhythmic, as if something else is setting the tempo.
- "Your skin feels different." The texture under someone's fingers. Faintly smoother, or with a grain that wasn't there. Still pleasant. Just not quite the same.

These observations arrive over weeks. No two people will mention the same thing. You can explain each one away. Stress. The frontier. Bad sleep. Hard living. And maybe that's all it is.

## Things That Help

If any of this resonates, there are things that slow it down. You won't know why they help. They just do.

**Do the things you did before.** Whatever defined you before the bond — practice it. If you were a musician, play. If you were a healer, treat wounds with your hands and your knowledge, not borrowed warmth. If you studied, study. The familiar rhythms of who you were act as an anchor. They remind your body and mind of their original shape.

**Spend time alone.** Not in isolation — that brings its own pain. But time away from the intensity. An afternoon walking by yourself. A night sleeping apart from the group. The pull to return will be strong. Sit with it.

**Name what you're feeling.** Out loud if you can. To someone you trust. The alien thoughts lose some of their power when you drag them into the open. "I wanted to hurt that person and I don't know why." "I can't stop thinking about the river." "I feel like my skin doesn't fit right." Saying it doesn't fix it, but it keeps you aware that *you* are noticing, which means *you* are still there.

**Physical grounding.** Cold water. Hard stone underfoot. Intense flavors — bitter tea, sharp cheese, raw garlic. Sensations that are unmistakably real and unmistakably yours. When the strangeness is at its worst, grab something that anchors you to the physical world you've always lived in.

**Talk to each other.** If other companions are experiencing similar things, knowing you're not alone matters. Comparing notes might reveal patterns you can't see from inside your own experience.

## What You Don't Understand

You will have questions. They won't have easy answers.

*Why do I feel better near the hero?* Not emotionally — everyone feels that. Physically. The fatigue lifts. The headache clears. The chill you didn't realize you were carrying recedes. When you step away, it comes back. You assumed it was comfort. But comfort doesn't explain the way your body responds to proximity like a plant turning toward light.

*Why does food taste different?* Not everything. But some things. Meat tastes richer than it should. Fruit tastes sharper. Water from certain springs is intoxicating and you don't know why. Meanwhile, things you used to love taste muted.

*Why do I notice things I never noticed before?* The wind's direction before it shifts. The tension in someone's shoulders across a room. The exact moment twilight begins. Your senses haven't sharpened — not exactly. It's more like filters have been removed. You're receiving information you always could have, but your mind wasn't processing it.

*Why do I feel possessive about places?* A corner of Gorundtzal that feels like yours. A stretch of trail. A particular fire pit. You want to be near it, and you feel a flash of irritation when someone else occupies it carelessly. This isn't normal territoriality. It's deeper and harder to explain.

*Why do my dreams feel like somewhere else?* Not nightmares. Something stranger. Vast spaces. A sense of connection to something enormous and patient. Warmth that goes deeper than skin. You wake up with the emotions but not the images, and the longing takes hours to fade.

---

**These changes are slow.** You may go many sessions noticing only psychological shifts. The physical signs, if they come, arrive gradually enough that you can question whether they're real. This is not a countdown. There is no inevitable destination. What happens to you depends on what you do, what the hero does, and what you both choose when the time comes to choose.

You are still you. That matters. Hold onto it.
