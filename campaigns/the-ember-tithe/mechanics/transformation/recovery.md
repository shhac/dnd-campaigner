# Transformation Recovery — GM Guide

**Classification**: GM-Only. **GATED**: Do not read until after Rootbound contact (Beat 6+).

Recovery is possible. It is never clean. Something is always lost. But the campaign should make clear that fighting for someone's humanity matters.

---

## The Core Principle

The cure is thematically opposite to the flaw the transformation exploits. A companion transforming through rage must find peace. A companion losing themselves to desire must choose solitude. A companion becoming territorial must let go. The recovery path is always the hardest version of the thing the companion least wants to do.

Recovery does not restore the companion to who they were. It stabilizes them as someone new — still marked by the transformation, still carrying its echoes, but human. Changed, not consumed.

---

## Recovery Paths by Transformation Vector

### Predatory (aggression, hunting instinct, physicality)
**The cure**: Deliberate vulnerability. The companion must place themselves in harm's way without fighting back. Not suicidal — strategic surrender. Allowing themselves to be hurt, captured, or humiliated when every instinct screams to strike. Each act of restraint reverses a measure of the predatory transformation.
**The horror**: The companion must do this repeatedly. And each time, the predatory instinct fights harder. They may lash out during recovery, hurting people they love. The cure looks a lot like self-destruction.
**The cost**: The companion loses some of their combat edge permanently. They're slower, weaker, more human. They may resent this.

### Elemental (attunement, temperature shifts, weather sensitivity)
**The cure**: Confinement. The companion must spend extended time in enclosed, artificial spaces — away from the sky, the wind, the earth. Stone rooms with no windows. Underground chambers. Every part of them rebels against this. The elemental transformation has given them a deep connection to the natural world, and cutting it off feels like suffocation.
**The horror**: While confined, the companion can still feel the channels through the walls. They hear the earth's pulse. The confinement drives them toward madness before it drives them toward recovery. Someone must stay with them — and that someone will witness the worst of it.
**The cost**: The companion's sensitivity to the natural world dims permanently. Beauty they could once perceive becomes invisible to them.

### Empathic (emotional absorption, boundary dissolution)
**The cure**: Emotional isolation. The companion must stop caring — deliberately, consciously. They must build walls they've never had, refuse to help people in pain, walk away from suffering they could ease. Each act of chosen coldness reclaims a piece of their dissolving identity.
**The horror**: The companion is good at caring. It's who they are. Asking them to stop is asking them to betray their deepest nature. And while they practice coldness, people around them suffer unnecessarily. The companion watches it happen and does nothing. This may be the most psychologically damaging recovery path.
**The cost**: The companion never fully regains their empathic depth. They feel emotions through a gauze now — present but muted. They're more resilient and less alive.

### Territorial (possessiveness, space-claiming, guardian instinct)
**The cure**: Voluntary exile. The companion must leave what they've claimed — the settlement, the hero, the people they've designated as "theirs." Not temporarily. For real. They must go far enough that the bond's pull becomes agony, and they must stay there long enough for the territorial instinct to break.
**The horror**: Every day away is torture. The companion feels the distance physically — a tearing sensation, phantom pain, the constant conviction that something terrible is happening to the thing they left. They may hallucinate threats. They may try to go back. If the hero visits them during exile, it resets the clock.
**The cost**: The companion returns gentler, less fierce. The guardian instinct that made them formidable is diminished. They may never feel truly safe again.

### Dissolution (identity blurring, shape instability)
**The cure**: Radical honesty. The companion must tell truths that cost them everything. Not small truths — confessions that destroy relationships, reveal vulnerabilities, burn bridges. Each truth locks their shifting identity a little more, anchoring them to a single self.
**The horror**: The truths must be real. Not performative, not calculated for maximum drama — genuinely painful revelations that the companion has been hiding, possibly for years. And the damage those truths cause is permanent. Relationships shattered by recovery don't mend because the companion got better.
**The cost**: The companion is known, finally, completely. Every secret is gone. Every mask is burned. What remains is honest and raw and defenseless. Some people can't bear being that exposed.

### Symbiotic (merging with channels, earth-sense, root-growth)
**The cure**: Severance from the channels. The companion must be physically removed from all channel influence — brought to cauterized land where the channels are dead. This feels like dying. The connection to the earth, the warmth, the vast network of life they've been growing into — all of it goes silent. They're alone in their own skin for the first time in months, and the silence is deafening.
**The horror**: On cauterized land, the companion's transformation reverses rapidly — painfully. The physical changes recede over days, not weeks. Bark-like skin softens and bleeds. Root-like growths retract, leaving wounds. The companion feels the channel network dying around them and mourns it. They may beg to be brought back.
**The cost**: The companion loses their connection to the channels permanently. They can never feel the earth's pulse again. For someone who experienced the vast warmth of the network, this loss may feel worse than the transformation itself.

## Rootbound Grounding Rituals

Ayantu and the Rootbound can teach practices that slow transformation and provide temporary stability. These are not recovery — they're maintenance. Think of them as managing a chronic condition rather than curing a disease.

### The Rooting
The companion sits barefoot on living earth, hands pressed flat to the ground, and breathes in a specific pattern while reciting words the Rootbound provide. The ritual takes an hour. During it, the companion feels the channels beneath them — warmth, connection, belonging. The ritual grounds the transformation's direction, preventing chaotic vector drift. For 1-2 sessions afterward, transformation advancement slows significantly.

**The unsettling part**: The ritual feels *good*. Too good. The companion doesn't want it to end. They emerge feeling centered and calm, but also slightly more connected to the channels than they were before. The ritual manages symptoms by deepening the underlying condition. The Rootbound know this and consider it acceptable. The hero may disagree.

### The Naming
The companion speaks their own name — their full name, the name they were given, the name they chose, every name they've been called — while holding an object from their life before the bond. The ritual reaffirms identity, slowing dissolution-vector transformations specifically. It works best for companions losing themselves.

**The unsettling part**: Sometimes the companion can't remember all their names. The gaps are where the transformation has already rewritten them.

### The Severance Touch
Ayantu can temporarily interrupt the bond between the hero and a companion by placing her hands on both of them simultaneously and channeling her own connection to the network. This reduces bond strength by 1-2 points for one session — giving the companion breathing room.

**The cost**: Ayantu absorbs what she interrupts. Each severance touch ages her. She won't do it often. She won't explain why it ages her. She'll simply say: "Not today. Ask me next week."

## Recovery Timing

- Recovery cannot begin until the companion is at **Visible** stage or higher. Before that, the transformation is too subtle to fight — there's nothing to push back against.
- Recovery takes **3-5 sessions** of active, consistent effort. It cannot be rushed.
- If the companion reaches **Threshold** without beginning recovery, recovery becomes exponentially harder — requiring all of the above PLUS the hero's direct involvement (which risks destabilizing the hero's own stability through bond strain).
- Recovery that fails (the companion gives up or the cost proves too high) accelerates transformation by one full stage. The attempt itself wakes something up.

---

## The Question Recovery Doesn't Answer

Even successful recovery leaves a mark (lowercase). The companion is human again — mostly. But they dream differently now. Food tastes different. Their skin remembers a texture it no longer has. In quiet moments, they hear something beneath the earth that might be memory or might be the channels, still reaching for them.

Recovery saves the person. It doesn't erase the experience. And some companions, given the choice, may decide they don't want to be saved.

That decision — and the hero's response to it — is the campaign's moral center.
