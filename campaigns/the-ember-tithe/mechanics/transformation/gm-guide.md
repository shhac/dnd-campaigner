# Transformation — GM Guide

**Audience**: GM only. Do NOT share with players.
**When to read**: On-demand, when transformation advances or you need adjudication guidance.

---

## Transformation Stages

Each bonded companion progresses through these stages. Advancement is not automatic — it requires triggers (see below). Track each companion's current stage separately.

### Stage 1: Dormant

The bond exists but transformation has not begun. The companion is entirely themselves. They experience the bond's warmth and the pull toward the hero, but their mind and body are unchanged.

**Duration**: Variable. Some companions enter Stage 2 within sessions of bonding. Others remain Dormant if the bond stays shallow.

**What to narrate**: Normal behavior. The companion feels comfort near the hero, maybe an unusual sense of being understood. Nothing they'd flag as strange.

### Stage 2: Psychological

The mind changes first. New urges surface, priorities shift, alien thoughts intrude. No physical signs — nothing a healer, an observer, or even the companion themselves could point to as evidence. The horror is entirely internal: the companion notices their own mind changing and doesn't know why.

**Duration**: 2-4 sessions minimum at this stage. Do not rush past this. Psychological horror is the campaign's strongest tool, and it only works if you give it room.

**What to narrate**: Behavioral shifts during scenes. A companion says something out of character — then catches themselves. A flash of emotion that doesn't match the situation. A new interest or craving that the companion finds puzzling. Frame these as character moments, not mechanical updates. Let the companion's player (or AI) discover and react.

**Key principle**: The companion should be able to articulate "something is different about me" but not "I am transforming." Confusion, not certainty.

### Stage 3: Early Physical

Extremely subtle physical changes that are only noticeable through intimate contact or sustained close observation. Temperature, scent, skin texture, hair feel. A companion's partner or close friend might notice. A stranger never would.

**Duration**: 2-3 sessions.

**What to narrate**: Weave sensory details into moments of physical closeness. A hand on a shoulder that feels warmer than expected. Hair that catches the light differently. A scent that's new and hard to place. Use the sensory-guide.md reference sheet — pick 1-2 details per scene, never more. Subtlety is everything.

**Key principle**: Every physical sign should be individually deniable. "Could be the weather." "Could be stress." "Could be nothing."

### Stage 4: Visible

Changes are noticeable to those who know the companion well. Still deniable with effort — "they've changed since the fall, who hasn't?" — but the accumulation is becoming hard to ignore. The companion's appearance has shifted: still attractive, perhaps more so, but in a direction that wasn't there before.

**Duration**: 2-4 sessions.

**What to narrate**: Descriptions that lead with beauty and follow with wrongness. "Her eyes have taken on a depth that makes you want to stare — and then you realize you can't quite see the bottom." Companions themselves may catch their reflection and pause. Other characters in the world start noticing.

**Key principle**: Attractiveness is preserved or enhanced. The uncanny valley effect — compelling and unsettling simultaneously. This makes the hero's position harder, which is the point.

### Stage 5: Advanced

Visible to strangers. The companion reads as other — not necessarily inhuman, but clearly not entirely ordinary. Inhuman capabilities are emerging: enhanced senses, unusual physical abilities, reactions that are too fast or too precise. The companion is compelling but unsettling. People stare. People move aside without knowing why.

**Duration**: Open-ended. A companion can remain at this stage indefinitely if the bond stabilizes.

**What to narrate**: Open descriptions of difference. Don't hide it anymore. The companion moves through the world differently and the world responds. NPCs react with fascination, unease, or fear. The companion themselves may have stopped fighting the changes — or may be fighting harder than ever.

**Key principle**: The companion is still themselves inside. Personality, memories, bonds — all intact. The horror at this stage is the question: for how long?

### Stage 6: Threshold

The point of no return without active recovery (see recovery.md, available after Rootbound contact). The companion is more *other* than human. Their body, their senses, their instincts have crossed a line where returning to normal through willpower alone is impossible. They need help — and the help will cost something terrible.

**What to narrate**: Moments where the companion's old self surfaces through the new — a laugh that sounds exactly like it used to, a gesture remembered from before the bond, a flash of the person they were. These moments are what make recovery worth pursuing. Without them, the companion risks becoming something beautiful and alien and no longer who they were.

**Key principle**: This stage should feel like a crisis, not a destination. The campaign is designed so that reaching Threshold is a consequence of choices — the hero's neglect, the companion's surrender, or circumstances that prevented intervention. It should feel avoidable in retrospect.

## Transformation Direction — The Vector System

Transformation does not follow a predetermined path. Instead, track VECTORS — tendencies that accumulate based on the companion's experiences and choices. Each vector represents a direction the transformation might lean. Most companions develop 2-3 dominant vectors, producing amalgamated transformations that feel unique.

### Vectors

**Predatory** — Aggression, hunting instinct, physicality, the thrill of pursuit.
- *Triggered by*: Combat, anger, protective violence, killing, physical dominance
- *Manifests as*: Faster reflexes, sharper senses (especially smell and hearing), restless energy, a tendency to stalk rather than walk, teeth and nails that feel different
- *Psychological signature*: The companion begins categorizing people as threats or prey without consciously deciding to

**Elemental** — Attunement to environment, temperature shifts, weather sensitivity, resonance with natural forces.
- *Triggered by*: Extended time outdoors, exposure to channel energy, surviving elemental crises (storms, fire, flooding), extreme temperatures
- *Manifests as*: Skin temperature fluctuations, weather sense, comfort in conditions that should be dangerous, hair or skin taking on tones that echo the environment
- *Psychological signature*: Growing indifference to shelter and civilization. The outdoors feels more real than any building.

**Empathic** — Emotional absorption, boundary dissolution, feeling others' pain and desire as if it were your own.
- *Triggered by*: Emotional labor, healing others, romantic intensity, sustained intimacy, absorbing grief or fear from others
- *Manifests as*: Physical responses to others' emotions (flushing when someone nearby is angry, tears when someone nearby grieves), skin that seems to ripple faintly in emotional atmospheres, eyes that shift color with mood
- *Psychological signature*: Increasing difficulty distinguishing own emotions from absorbed ones. "Am I angry or is she angry?"

**Territorial** — Possessiveness, space-claiming, guardian instinct that borders on obsession.
- *Triggered by*: Defending a place, nesting behavior (improving or personalizing a space), jealousy, protecting the hero or another companion from a rival
- *Manifests as*: Awareness of boundaries (who enters and leaves a space), physical tension when territory is violated, a presence that makes others uneasy in the companion's claimed space
- *Psychological signature*: A place becomes an extension of self. Threats to the place feel like threats to the body.

**Dissolution** — Identity blurring, shape instability, becoming multiple, losing the edges of self.
- *Triggered by*: Deception, identity conflict, loss of self-narrative, forced role-playing, sustained pretense, denial of the transformation itself
- *Manifests as*: Features that seem slightly different depending on who's looking, voice shifts, moments of looking like someone else for a heartbeat, reflections that don't quite match
- *Psychological signature*: "Who am I?" becomes a genuine, frightening question rather than a philosophical exercise.

**Symbiotic** — Merging with the channel network, earth-sense, root-growth, connection to the deep systems beneath Valduri.
- *Triggered by*: Time near channel nodes, Rootbound contact, Mark resonance events, deep meditation or grounding practices near living stone or old growth
- *Manifests as*: Awareness of underground channels, skin patterns that echo root systems, an ability to sense the health of the land, warmth that rises from the ground through the companion's body
- *Psychological signature*: The companion begins feeling the land as a body — wounded, alive, patient. They lose interest in individual-scale concerns.

### Tracking

Keep a simple tally per companion, per vector. When a trigger event occurs during play, add a point to the relevant vector(s). A single event can feed multiple vectors (protecting a place during combat: +1 Predatory, +1 Territorial).

The dominant vector(s) — whichever have the most points — shape the transformation's visible and psychological direction. Ties produce blended transformations. A companion with Predatory 4, Elemental 3 develops differently from one with Empathic 5, Dissolution 2.

Review vectors at the end of each session. Note any that advanced. Consider whether the transformation stage should advance based on accumulated vector points and trigger events.

## Advancement Triggers

What pushes a companion from one stage to the next. Advancement requires at least one trigger event AND sufficient time at the current stage (see durations above).

- **Bond strength increase**: When the bond deepens meaningfully — a moment of radical honesty, a shared crisis survived, physical intimacy that carries genuine emotional weight. Crossing a bond threshold (e.g., from casual connection to deep bond) is the strongest trigger.
- **Intense emotional event involving the hero**: A fight, a confession, a betrayal, a rescue. The emotion must be real and directed at or shared with the hero.
- **Proximity to channel nodes**: Extended time near places where the underground channels surface or concentrate. The Mark's power flows through these channels; proximity amplifies it.
- **Using transformation-granted capabilities**: If a companion leans into an emerging ability (enhanced senses, unusual strength, emotional reading), using it accelerates advancement. The body learns what the mind accepts.
- **The hero drawing heavily on the bond during crisis**: When the hero needs power — to survive, to protect, to fight — the Mark pulls from bonded companions. This is the parasitic cost. If the hero draws hard, the companion pays.

### Advancement Pace Guidelines

- **Sessions 1-3**: No companion should advance past Stage 2 (Psychological). Even rapid bonds need time.
- **Sessions 4-6**: Stage 3 (Early Physical) is the ceiling. Only companions with strong bonds and multiple trigger events should reach it.
- **Sessions 7-10**: Stage 4 (Visible) becomes possible for the most deeply bonded companion. Others should still be at Stage 2-3.
- **Sessions 10+**: Stage 5 (Advanced) only for companions with sustained, intense bonds and multiple trigger events. Most companions should be at Stage 3-4.
- **Threshold**: Never before Session 12+, and only through deliberate narrative escalation or the hero's neglect.

When in doubt, advance slower. The slow burn is the horror. Rushing stages undermines the entire emotional arc.

## Slowing and Delaying Transformation

### Behavioral Anchors

When a companion consistently engages in activities tied to their pre-bond identity — practicing their craft, maintaining old habits, pursuing original goals — subtract 1 advancement point per session. "Consistently" means the companion actively chooses these activities during play, not just mentions them in passing.

This requires the companion to know (or suspect) that staying grounded in who they were matters. The player README hints at this without explaining the mechanics.

### Hero's Restraint

The hero can consciously limit bond intensity: pulling back emotionally, maintaining distance, refusing to draw on the bond during crisis. This slows advancement for the relevant companion but costs the hero:
- -1 hero stability per session of sustained restraint (the Mark pushes back against restraint)
- The companion feels the withdrawal and may interpret it as rejection

### Rootbound Grounding Rituals

Available after Beat 6 (Rootbound contact). Specific practices taught by the Rootbound that can freeze transformation at its current stage for 1-2 sessions. The rituals are uncomfortable — they involve pain, cold, isolation, and confronting the alien thoughts directly rather than suppressing them.

Mechanically: freezes stage advancement for 1-2 sessions. Does not reduce vector tallies. The transformation pressure builds during the freeze and may advance more rapidly when the ritual lapses.

### Physical Separation

Distance from the hero slows advancement. The Mark's influence weakens with physical distance. However:
- Both the hero and the companion must make a Wisdom save (DC 12) each day of voluntary separation to maintain it. Failure means overwhelming anxiety and a compulsion to reunite.
- The companion experiences emotional pain during separation — restlessness, insomnia, a cold ache that doesn't respond to warmth.
- The hero weakens without the bond's sustenance (standard Mark withdrawal symptoms).

Separation is a blunt instrument. It works, but the cost is real and sustained separation becomes harder, not easier, over time.

## Attractiveness Preservation — Narration Guidelines

This is the campaign's most delicate tonal balance. Physical transformation should enhance or shift beauty, not destroy it, through Stage 5. Only at Threshold does the beauty become secondary to the otherness.

### The Rule

**Always lead with the attractive element, then the unsettling one.** The reader/listener should feel the pull before they feel the discomfort.

### Stage 3 Examples (Early Physical)

- "Her skin is warmer under your hand than it should be — not feverish, something deeper, like she's generating heat from somewhere inside. It feels good. You don't want to let go."
- "His hair has taken on a sheen that catches firelight like copper wire. It's striking. You only notice it's new because you remember what it looked like before."
- "She smells like petrichor — rain on hot stone — and you find yourself leaning closer before you realize what you're doing."
- "The scar on his forearm is fainter than it was last week. You're almost sure."

### Stage 4 Examples (Visible)

- "Her eyes have deepened to a color that doesn't exist in nature — somewhere between amber and burnt gold, with a luminosity that makes candlelight redundant. They're beautiful. They're wrong."
- "He moves through the crowded room without touching anyone, his body finding gaps you didn't see. It's graceful — fluid, almost liquid — and several people turn to watch without knowing why."
- "Her skin has taken on a faint bronze undertone, warm and rich, and in certain light you can see something beneath it — faint branching lines, like the ghost of the hero's Mark. They vanish when you look directly."
- "His voice when he speaks has a resonance it didn't used to have, as if the words are coming from a larger chest. People listen. They can't help it."

### Stage 5 Examples (Advanced)

- "She is beautiful in a way that makes your teeth ache. The proportions of her face have shifted — not distorted, perfected, but toward an aesthetic that isn't human. You stare because you can't stop. You look away because something in your hindbrain says *danger*."
- "He stands in the rain without flinching, water running down skin that has the depth and grain of polished heartwood. His eyes reflect light like a cat's. When he smiles, it's his smile — warm, self-deprecating — and that's what makes your chest hurt."
- "Her hair moves in still air. Not much. Just enough to notice if you're watching. You're always watching."
- "When he catches your arm, his grip is precise — exactly strong enough, no more — and his hand is fever-warm. His fingers are slightly longer than they were a month ago. You both pretend not to notice."

### Threshold Examples

- "She is radiant and terrible. The branching patterns visible beneath her skin pulse with the hero's heartbeat, not her own. Her eyes hold depths that go deeper than a skull should allow. When she speaks, her voice carries harmonics. She is still herself — she remembers your name, she laughs at the same bad jokes — but *herself* is losing the argument with whatever is growing inside her."
