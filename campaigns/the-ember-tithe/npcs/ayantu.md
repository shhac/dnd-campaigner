# Ayantu

**Role**: Ally / Knowledge Source / Gatekeeper to the Rootbound
**Location**: Western forests, near Gorundtzal
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Human |
| **Occupation** | Rootbound elder and channel tender |
| **Age** | Late 50s |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

A compact, weathered woman with deep brown skin etched by decades of sun and wind. Her hair is silver-white, kept in tight coils close to her scalp, threaded with thin strips of undyed cloth that look decorative but are knotted in patterns that mean something to the Rootbound. Her hands are the most striking thing about her — broad, strong, soil-darkened at the nail beds and in the creases, with faint branching lines across the backs that could be old scars or could be something else. She wears layers of rough-spun cloth in muted browns and greens, wooden jewelry carved with channel patterns at her wrists and throat. She moves through the forest with an ease that makes visitors feel clumsy by comparison — no wasted motion, no hesitation about where to step.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Patient and watchful, with an authority that comes from competence rather than position. She is still before she speaks and speaks less than she could.
**Motivation**: Protect the channels, protect her people, understand what the Mark's reappearance means — and decide whether the hero is a gift or a catastrophe before either outcome becomes irreversible.
**Fear**: That the hero is the one the oldest stories warn about — a bearer who doesn't fight the Mark but embraces it, becoming a conduit for the channels to reclaim the surface world. She's also afraid of what she'll have to do if that's the case.

**Personality Traits**:
- Speaks in metaphors drawn from the forest and the seasons, but switches to blunt pragmatism when the situation demands it — the metaphors are habit, not affectation
- Tests people constantly. Small requests, oblique questions, silences that last long enough to be uncomfortable. She's mapping character, not making conversation.
- Has survived fifty years on the frontier by trusting slowly and cutting ties quickly. She wants to help the hero. She is not certain she should.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Measured, unhurried, with a rhythm that sounds like someone translating from an older dialect. She pauses before important words, giving them weight. Uses "the land" as a subject the way others use "God" — as an authority that doesn't need justification. Rarely asks direct questions; prefers observations that invite response.
**Mannerisms**: Touches the bark of trees the way others touch walls for balance — casually, habitually, as if drawing information. Closes her eyes when listening. Places her palm flat on the ground when she needs to think. Tends a small fire with the absent precision of someone who's done it ten thousand times.
**Sample Dialogue**: "A tree doesn't ask whether it should grow toward the light. It grows. The question is what it shades when it does."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- She is an elder of the Rootbound, the frontier communities that tend the old places
- She has lived in the western forests for her entire life and knows the land intimately
- She has been aware of Gorundtzal's refugee population and has been watching from a distance
- She recognizes the hero's Mark immediately and names it — the Ember Tithe

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- She has tended channel nodes for over forty years and can sense the network's health through physical contact with the earth and trees — warmth means healthy, cold means damaged, silence means dead
- She knows the Communion's cauterization is killing the channels. She can feel it — a creeping numbness spreading from the south, like frostbite in the ground.
- She knows grounding rituals that can slow transformation in bonded companions. They work. They are also deeply unsettling — involving buried offerings, prolonged skin contact with channel nodes, and chanted repetitions that leave participants feeling hollowed and raw for days.
- She knows of previous bearers of the Mark. The last one, roughly two hundred years ago, was named Mesfin. He loved three people. Those three people became the old-growth forest she tends. She considers this neither tragedy nor triumph — it simply is what the channels do.
- She has been in contact with Dessalegn, who tends the Rootbound Grove near Gorundtzal. Dessalegn is frightened. Ayantu told him to watch and wait.

### Secrets (GM only)
- She fears the hero may be the fulfillment of the Rootbound's oldest warning — not a bearer who transforms companions, but a bearer who accepts the transformation willingly, becoming a conduit through which the channels reclaim the surface. In the oldest stories, this is how the channels went underground in the first place: the surface world rejected them. If a bearer opens the way again, the channels might not be content to stay beneath the soil.
- Her ability to sense the channels borders on a bond of its own. She hears them. Not words — rhythms, pressures, something like breath. She has never told anyone this, because it sounds like madness and because she fears what it means about her own nature. She is not marked, but she may not be entirely unchanged by decades of proximity.
- She has seen the old-growth forest — Mesfin's forest — do things that trees don't do. Branches that move without wind. Roots that redirect paths. A warmth in winter that shouldn't be there. She doesn't worship the forest, but she tends it with the care of someone maintaining a grave. Or a prison. She isn't sure which.
- She knows the Rootbound's understanding of the Mark is incomplete. The oral tradition has been passed down for two centuries through a community of perhaps two hundred people. Things have been lost, distorted, mythologized. She suspects the Communion's texts hold pieces the Rootbound are missing, and vice versa. She would never admit this to an outsider.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Awe held in check by caution. She has waited her entire life to see the Mark and is unsure whether this is an answered prayer or a confirmed nightmare.
**Current Disposition**: Guarded, testing, willing to help — but help has conditions.
**History with Party**: None yet. She initiates contact after observing the hero from a distance.

## Combat Stats (if needed)

**Use Stat Block**: Druid (modified — no spells, but channel sense replaces them)
**AC**: 11 (no armor) | **HP**: 27
**Notable Abilities**: Channel sense (can detect the health and location of underground channels within several hundred feet), deep knowledge of herbalism and natural medicine, survival expertise in forested terrain. Not a combatant — will withdraw rather than fight.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: The Rootbound, the channel network, Mesfin's legacy, the grounding rituals, the old-growth forest, Dessalegn, Birukta
**Potential Uses**:
- First meaningful Rootbound contact — she names the Mark and connects the hero to the larger tradition
- Source of grounding rituals that slow companion transformation (mechanically significant, narratively unsettling)
- Gateway to the deeper Rootbound knowledge that Birukta holds
- Her account of Mesfin and the three companions seeds the Act 3 revelation about the "third path"
- Her channel sense provides strategic intelligence about the Communion's cauterization progress
- Her fear about the hero's potential can become a source of tension or a self-fulfilling prophecy depending on the hero's choices
- The old-growth forest she tends is a location the party can visit — and an encounter with something that used to be human

## GM Notes

Ayantu is the campaign's first window into the Rootbound's world and the Mark's history. She should feel like a real person with a lifetime of experience, not a quest dispenser with exposition. Let her test the hero. Let her be reluctant. Let her help in ways that are genuinely useful but also genuinely uncomfortable.

Her grounding rituals are a key gameplay element — they give the hero a tool to manage transformation, but the rituals themselves have a folk horror quality. They involve burying objects that belonged to the companion, pressing bare skin against channel stones until the warmth becomes painful, repeating phrases in the old dialect until they lose meaning. The rituals work because they reconnect the companion to their original self — but the process feels like it's pulling something out of them rather than putting something back.

Her relationship with the hero should be defined by a tension she can never fully resolve: she wants to believe the hero is different from the bearer in the stories, but she knows the Mark doesn't care about the bearer's intentions. The channels take what they need. The question is whether the bearer can shape what they give.

She should reveal information in layers. First meeting: she names the Mark, shows recognition, offers cautious help. Second meeting: she tells the story of Mesfin, obliquely. Third meeting: she teaches the first grounding ritual. Only after significant trust does she admit her fears about the oldest stories, or her suspicion that her own understanding is incomplete.

She is not a romance candidate. Her bond with the land runs deeper than anything the Mark could offer, and she knows it. If the hero tries to bond with her, she will recognize what's happening and refuse — gently, firmly, and with the weight of someone who has already chosen what she loves.
