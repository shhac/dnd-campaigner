# Takkele

**Role**: Ally / Information Source / Connection to the Rootbound
**Location**: Arrives at Gorundtzal (sessions 1-2)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Human |
| **Occupation** | Former Communion acolyte (deserter) |
| **Age** | Early 20s |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

Young and visibly haunted. Dark skin with ritual scarification on her forearms — parallel lines in groups of three, the marks of a Communion acolyte. She's tried to obscure them with mud and bandages but they're visible to anyone who looks. Her hair is shorn close, growing back unevenly — Communion acolytes keep their heads bare. She has deep-set eyes that seem older than her face, and she's thin in a way that suggests weeks of poor eating, not natural build. She carries almost nothing — a waterskin, a knife, a bundle of herbs wrapped in cloth.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Wary, earnest, desperate to be believed. Alternates between guarded silence and sudden, urgent confession.
**Motivation**: Warn someone — anyone — about what the Communion is actually doing beneath Valduri. She broke her vows and fled because of what she saw, and the guilt and terror of that decision consume her.
**Fear**: Being sent back. Being caught. Being dismissed as a liar or a spy.

**Personality Traits**:
- Genuinely devout — she didn't leave the Communion because she lost faith, but because what she saw violated her understanding of that faith
- Struggles with the cultural habits of her upbringing — she still flinches at waste, still whispers purification prayers under her breath, still feels guilt for eating before others
- Young enough to be idealistic, old enough to know that idealism got her into this

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Formal Valduri with a Communion accent — consonant clusters creep in, word order shifts when she's stressed. She translates Communion concepts into Valduri imperfectly. Uses "purify" and "cauterize" as synonyms for "fix" or "heal" without noticing.
**Mannerisms**: Rubs her scarification marks when anxious. Sits with her back to walls. Watches the sky — Communion scouts use signal fires.
**Sample Dialogue**: "They told us the channels were poison. Corruption flowing under the earth. I believed that. I still believe the channels are real. But what they're doing to them — that isn't healing. That's butchery."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- She's a Communion deserter. She doesn't hide this — the scars give her away regardless.
- She claims the Communion is doing something terrible beneath Valduri, something that goes beyond cauterizing infected channels.
- She traveled north alone for over a week, avoiding patrols, eating whatever she could find.

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- The Communion has begun deep excavations beneath Kethenvari, accessing underground channels that run for miles beneath Valduri
- She was assigned to a ritual team — junior acolytes who maintained purification rites at the excavation entrances. What she saw inside didn't match what she'd been taught.
- The channels aren't dead stone — they pulse. They're warm. They respond to sound and touch. The Communion's "cauterization" involves burning them with a substance that makes the channels scream. She uses this word deliberately.
- Some Communion priests have begun arguing among themselves. The Cauterists want to accelerate. The Ashen Penitents question whether they have the right. The Covenant Remnant demands more sacrifices to sanctify the work.

### Secrets (GM only)
- She saw someone in the excavations who wasn't Communion — a figure in the deep tunnels who watched the work and then vanished. They had branching patterns on their skin, like the hero's Mark. She hasn't connected this to the hero yet, but she will if she sees the Mark.
- She stole a small quantity of the cauterizing substance when she fled. It's in the herb bundle she carries. She doesn't know what it does beyond what she witnessed, but she took it as evidence.
- Her desertion wasn't entirely voluntary. She was caught questioning the senior priest Gvirokha about the screaming channels. Gvirokha gave her a twelve-hour head start instead of reporting her — an act of mercy or manipulation that Takkele can't parse. She doesn't know if Gvirokha is an ally or if she's being used.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Desperate trust. She needs these people to believe her. She knows she looks like a spy and hates it.
**Current Disposition**: Cautiously hopeful
**History with Party**: None yet. Arrives at Gorundtzal in the first 1-2 sessions.

## Combat Stats (if needed)

**Use Stat Block**: Acolyte (modified — no spells)
**AC**: 10 | **HP**: 9
**Notable Abilities**: Knowledge of Communion tactics, rituals, and organization. Speaks the Communion's language fluently. Basic herbalism. Not a combatant.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: The Communion's internal fractures, the underground channels, the Grey Wasting, the Mark's history, Gvirokha
**Potential Uses**:
- Primary source of information about the Communion's true activities
- Her Communion knowledge helps the party understand their enemy's structure and weaknesses
- The stolen cauterizing substance is a physical clue that can be studied or traded
- Her account of the figure in the tunnels connects the Mark to the ancient channels
- Her relationship with Gvirokha creates a potential backchannel to a Communion subfaction
- Her faith crisis mirrors the campaign's themes — what do you do when the thing you believed in turns out to be monstrous?

## GM Notes

Takkele is the campaign's first major information dump, but she should feel like a person, not an exposition device. She's young, scared, and carrying a burden of knowledge she doesn't fully understand. Let her reveal information gradually — she'll share the broad strokes quickly (the Communion is doing something terrible), but the details (the screaming channels, the figure with the Mark) should emerge over conversations.

She's a strong candidate for bonding with the hero. If it happens, her transformation would likely follow her faith — the devotion that drew her to the Communion becoming something inhuman. A need to believe, to serve, to purify. The acolyte who left one religion slowly becoming something that demands worship itself.

Her relationship with Gvirokha is a deliberate loose thread. Gvirokha may have let her go out of genuine doubt about the Communion's mission, or may be using her as an unwitting agent to spread fear among the refugees. This ambiguity should persist.

The cauterizing substance she carries is mechanically significant — it damages the underground channels and, by extension, anything connected to them (including, potentially, the Mark). This makes it both a weapon and a threat.
