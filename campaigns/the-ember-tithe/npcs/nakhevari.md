# Nakhevari

**Role**: Antagonist / Potential Ally / Moral Complication
**Location**: Northern frontier patrol route (passes near Gorundtzal)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Human |
| **Occupation** | Communion patrol captain (Ashen Penitent) |
| **Age** | Early 40s |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

A lean, angular man with sun-weathered olive skin and a close-trimmed dark beard streaked with early grey. His face is all planes and shadows — deep-set eyes, prominent cheekbones, a jaw that looks clenched even when it isn't. He wears the Communion's grey-and-ash field uniform with the discipline of a career soldier: every strap buckled, every piece of kit in its place, the ash-mark across his forehead renewed each morning with mechanical precision. His hands are scarred across the knuckles from years of patrol work. He carries a short sword and a crossbow, both well-maintained and undecorated. He moves with the controlled economy of someone who has marched ten thousand miles and expects to march ten thousand more.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Quiet, methodical, carrying a weight he doesn't discuss. He follows orders the way a man follows a road in fog — one step at a time, because stopping is worse.
**Motivation**: Survive his posting, keep his soldiers alive, and avoid the attention of zealots above him in the chain of command. Somewhere beneath that pragmatism is a man who joined the Communion because he believed in something. That belief is eroding.
**Fear**: That the cauterization is wrong — not failing, but morally wrong — and that he's spent twenty years serving a cause that will be remembered as atrocity.

**Personality Traits**:
- Respects competence above rank, faith, or nationality. A skilled enemy earns more of his attention than an incompetent superior.
- Speaks simply and precisely. Does not waste words on justification or philosophy. If pressed on the Communion's actions, he'll state facts rather than arguments.
- Takes no pleasure in his work but performs it thoroughly. The line between professionalism and complicity is one he does not examine closely, because examining it would require him to act.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Clipped, direct, with the formal cadence of Communion military speech — verb-final constructions, rank before name, impersonal phrasing. "The patrol observed movement" rather than "I saw someone." He drops into simpler, more human speech when caught off guard or speaking one-on-one with someone he respects.
**Mannerisms**: Checks sightlines when entering a space. Eats standing up. Inspects his weapons in the evening the way others pray — methodically, silently, as a form of self-regulation. Rubs the back of his neck when something troubles him.
**Sample Dialogue**: "I don't need to understand the theology. The order is to hold this road. I hold this road."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- He commands a patrol of twelve Communion soldiers assigned to the northern frontier route
- His patrol passes within half a day's march of Gorundtzal on a regular cycle — roughly every nine days
- He's an Ashen Penitent, the Communion subfaction that questions whether the cauterization should continue in its current form. This is not a secret within the Communion, but it limits his advancement.
- He's a competent field commander with two decades of service

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- He knows the cauterization is failing in some areas — channels that were sealed are reopening, or new ones are forming. The Communion's leadership is accelerating rather than reconsidering.
- He's seen the underground channels firsthand. He was assigned to a deep excavation escort in the central provinces before his transfer north. What he saw — the warmth, the pulsing, the way the channels responded to the cauterizing agents — shook him.
- He despises the Covenant Remnant subfaction. Their demand for greater sacrifice and spiritual purification strikes him as insanity dressed in doctrine. He considers the Cauterists misguided but honest. The Covenant Remnant he considers dangerous.
- He knows the Communion's supply lines in the north are stretched thin. His patrol is underequipped and his requests for resupply go unanswered. The occupation is overextended.

### Secrets (GM only)
- He served with Takkele before her desertion. Same unit, central provinces excavation detail. When word came that she had been marked for recapture, he was the one assigned to pursue. He tracked her to the northern road and then reported the trail gone cold. He let her go. If this is discovered, his execution is certain.
- He found a body in the deep tunnels during the excavation escort — half-buried in the channel wall, skin covered in branching patterns identical to the channel structure. The body was warm. He didn't report it. He told himself the tunnels played tricks on the mind. He's been dreaming about it since — the branching lines, the warmth, the sense that the body was not dead but waiting.
- He has considered desertion. Not as a plan — as a thought that surfaces in the grey hours before dawn and disappears when duty reasserts itself. He doesn't know where he'd go. The Communion is all he's been since he was twenty.
- He has a daughter in the Communion's rear territory — fourteen years old, being raised by her mother's family. He hasn't seen her in three years. Every order he follows is partially justified by the knowledge that desertion would make her the child of a traitor.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Professional assessment. He sees Gorundtzal as a refugee camp, not a threat — yet. He has no orders regarding it. His concern is efficiency, not cruelty.
**Current Disposition**: Neutral. Gorundtzal is not his problem until someone makes it his problem.
**History with Party**: None yet. His patrol route will bring him close enough for an encounter, voluntary or otherwise.

## Combat Stats (if needed)

**Use Stat Block**: Knight (modified — no magic)
**AC**: 16 (chain mail) | **HP**: 52
**Notable Abilities**: Tactical command (can coordinate his patrol in combat), proficient with martial weapons and crossbow, field fortification expertise. Experienced soldier — fights defensively, prioritizes his unit's survival over personal glory.
**Patrol**: 12 soldiers (use Guard stat block, AC 14, HP 11 each). Disciplined but not elite.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: The Communion's internal fractures, Takkele's past, the underground channels, the body in the tunnels, Communion patrol routes and supply lines
**Potential Uses**:
- First direct military threat to Gorundtzal — his patrol's regular route creates a ticking clock for the settlement's visibility
- His connection to Takkele is a lever for both sides. The hero can use it to negotiate. Takkele can use it to appeal to whatever conscience he has left. The Communion can use its discovery to destroy him.
- His account of the body in the tunnels connects to Mesfin and the deeper mystery of the Mark's history
- His knowledge of Communion supply lines and internal politics provides strategic intelligence
- He represents the Ashen Penitent perspective — people within the Communion who aren't zealots but serve anyway. The hero's treatment of him shapes the campaign's moral landscape.
- If turned, he could provide military expertise, a defector's intelligence, and eventually become a late-campaign companion. If pushed, he becomes a dangerous enemy who knows the frontier as well as anyone at Gorundtzal.

## GM Notes

Nakhevari is a mirror for the campaign's themes of complicity and impossible choices. He's not a villain — he's a professional who serves a monstrous institution because the alternatives (desertion, execution, his daughter's disgrace) are worse. Every interaction with him should carry the weight of a man who knows he's on the wrong side and can't figure out how to leave.

His connection to Takkele is the highest-stakes relationship in his arc. She was young, earnest, and devout — everything he used to be. He let her go because killing her faith felt worse than the risk to himself. If the party discovers this connection, it changes everything: Takkele's reaction (gratitude? anger? confusion about his motives?), Nakhevari's vulnerability, and the possibility of turning him.

The body in the tunnels is a critical plot seed. He doesn't know what he found — but the description (branching marks, warmth, a sense of waiting) connects to Mesfin, the previous bearer. Nakhevari won't volunteer this information easily. It frightens him in a way that undermines his professional composure, and professional composure is all he has left.

His patrol's regular route is a mechanical element: every nine days, twelve armed Communion soldiers pass within striking distance of Gorundtzal. Early on, this is background tension. As the settlement grows, it becomes a countdown. The hero can avoid detection, negotiate passage, ambush the patrol, or attempt to turn Nakhevari. Each choice has cascading consequences.

If the hero bonds with Nakhevari (unlikely but possible in a long campaign), his transformation direction would involve his discipline and control — the soldier's restraint becoming something rigid, absolute, a body that hardens and stiffens, a mind that narrows to pure tactical assessment. The professional who followed orders becoming something that can't stop following them, even when the orders come from inside.
