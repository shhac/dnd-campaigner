# Pellinaste

**Role**: Neutral / Merchant / Information Source
**Location**: Gorundtzal (temporary — she moves)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Human |
| **Occupation** | Frontier trader and salvager |
| **Age** | Mid 30s |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

Lean and weather-worn, with dark auburn hair kept in a practical braid that reaches her mid-back. Her skin is a warm olive, tanned deeper by years on frontier roads. She has quick, dark eyes that catalogue everything in a room before she's finished crossing the threshold. She wears layered traveling clothes — leather over linen over wool — each piece scavenged from different sources and none matching. A thin scar bisects her right eyebrow, giving her a permanently skeptical look. She moves with the loose-limbed ease of someone who's spent more of her life walking than sitting.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Watchful, charming when it serves her, casually amoral about property in a collapsed kingdom
**Motivation**: Profit and survival, in that order. She sees the fall of Valduri as a terrible thing that also happens to be the opportunity of a lifetime for someone who trades in salvage.
**Fear**: Being trapped. Walls, obligations, loyalties that prevent her from leaving when things go bad. She's survived this long by always having an exit.

**Personality Traits**:
- Genuinely enjoys people — she's not cold, just careful about what she gives away for free
- Has an instinct for what people need before they know it themselves — this makes her an excellent trader and a dangerous negotiator
- Never stays anywhere longer than she has to. If she's still at Gorundtzal, it's because the trading is good or the roads are too dangerous.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Warm and conversational, with a frontier drawl that thickens or thins depending on her audience. Uses trade metaphors. Asks questions that seem casual but aren't.
**Mannerisms**: Touches her scar when thinking. Sits where she can see the door. Unpacks and repacks her cart constantly, like she's always ready to leave.
**Sample Dialogue**: "Everything has a price, friend. The trick is figuring it out before someone else sets it for you."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- She's been trading the northern frontier for years — she knows every settlement, every path, every ruin worth picking through
- She arrived at Gorundtzal with a cart of salvaged goods: tools, preserved food, blankets, lamp oil, a few weapons
- She trades fairly but not generously — this is how she eats
- She's seen Communion patrols and knows their patterns in this region

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- She's been inside Communion-held territory recently — she trades with their camps too, under a different name. She sells them information about the frontier in exchange for safe passage.
- She knows where several abandoned supply caches are along the northern trade road — leftovers from the merchant guilds
- She's heard about strange things in the western forests — lights at night, sounds that don't belong to any animal, frontier folk acting strangely
- She knows a way through the mountains that avoids the main passes — a smuggler's route she learned years ago

### Secrets (GM only)
- She has been selling information to the Communion. Not out of loyalty — purely transactional. She tells them where refugee groups are heading, how many, how well-armed. She tells herself this is harmless intelligence they'd gather anyway. She's not entirely wrong, but people have died because of it.
- She's seen the Grey Wasting firsthand. Not in Communion territory — here, in Valduri. A patch of dead earth in the western forests, perfectly circular, where the ground is warm and smells of ash. She didn't tell anyone because she doesn't want to be the one who confirms the Communion's justification for invasion.
- She's noticed the hero's Mark and recognized something about it — not what it is, but that it reminds her of the patterns she saw in the dead earth patch. Branching lines, like veins. She hasn't connected the two consciously, but if pressed, she might.

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Interested. New arrivals mean new customers, new information, and new variables in the local power dynamics.
**Current Disposition**: Neutral, commercially friendly
**History with Party**: None yet. Present at Gorundtzal when they arrive.

## Combat Stats (if needed)

**Use Stat Block**: Scout
**AC**: 13 (leather armor) | **HP**: 16
**Notable Abilities**: Survival expertise, stealth, knows the terrain intimately. Not a fighter — will flee before she fights.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: Supply lines, Communion intelligence, the Grey Wasting, the western forests
**Potential Uses**:
- Source of supplies, trade goods, and frontier knowledge
- Her Communion contacts make her both useful and dangerous
- Her knowledge of the Grey Wasting patch can seed the deeper mystery
- Her smuggler's route through the mountains is a potential escape valve or trade connection
- Her observation about the Mark and the dead earth patterns can connect the hero's condition to the larger threat

## GM Notes

Pellinaste is morally ambiguous by design. She's not evil — she's practical in a way that crosses lines she doesn't examine too closely. Her Communion intelligence work is the kind of compromise that survivors make, and how the hero handles learning about it (if they do) should be a meaningful character moment.

She's unlikely to bond with the hero romantically (she doesn't stay), but she could become a valuable ally, a dangerous liability, or both. Her frontier knowledge makes her irreplaceable in early sessions; her Communion contacts make her a time bomb.

If she does bond with the hero (against her instincts), her transformation direction would involve her restlessness and freedom — becoming rooted, territorial, unable to leave. The trader who always had an exit becoming something that guards a place with inhuman devotion.

The Grey Wasting patch she found is real and important — it connects the Communion's claims to Valduri's underground channels and eventually to the Mark itself. But this is a long-game revelation.
