# World Primer: Valduri

What any inhabitant of Valduri would know.

---

## The Kingdom

Valduri was a kingdom of moderate size and modest ambition, nestled between mountain ranges to the north and the Greyveil Sea to the south. Its people farmed, traded, and lived without grand aspirations. The capital, Kethenvari, sat in the central valley — a city of pale stone and terraced gardens, home to perhaps thirty thousand souls. Three weeks ago, it fell.

The kingdom had no significant magical tradition. Healers used herbs. Scholars studied history, not arcana. The occasional strange occurrence — a grove that healed faster, a ruin that hummed at certain hours — was attributed to "the old ways" and largely ignored.

## The Fall

The Ash Veil Communion arrived from the east, across the mountains. They came in overwhelming force — disciplined soldiers in ash-grey armor, priests who spoke of spiritual corruption beneath the earth. They took Kethenvari in a single night. The king is dead. The army is scattered. The nobility fled or were executed.

The Communion claims they came not to conquer but to heal — that underground channels beneath Valduri are spreading a blight called the Grey Wasting into their own lands, and the kingdom's destruction was necessary to reach and cauterize those channels. Most Valduri see this as a conqueror's justification.

## The Communion

The Ash Veil Communion is a religious military order from the eastern empire of Tskhenavri. They worship through purification — fire, ash, sacrifice. Their theology holds that spiritual corruption flows through the earth like infected blood, and cauterization is the only cure.

They are not mindless zealots. Their soldiers are disciplined. Their priests are educated. Their cruelty, when it comes, is methodical and justified (in their minds) by necessity. This makes them more dangerous, not less.

Common Valduri know the Communion by their ash-grey armor, their ritual scarification, and the smell of burnt offerings that follows their camps. They speak a harsh, consonant-heavy language. Their officers sometimes speak passable Valduri.

## The Current Situation

The central provinces are occupied. The Communion holds Kethenvari and the major roads. But Valduri is large, and they don't have enough soldiers to hold everything. The frontiers — north, west, and the coastal regions — are contested or ungoverned.

Refugees stream outward from the center. Some head for the borders. Some gather at old waystations and fortifications, hoping for safety in numbers. Some simply hide.

There is no organized resistance yet, though rumors circulate of former officers and nobles trying to rally survivors.

## The Northern Frontier

The borderlands between settled Valduri and the wild uplands. Before the fall, this was trade country — merchant caravans heading north to the mountain passes, waystation towns servicing the traffic. Now the trade has stopped. The waystations are half-empty or repurposed as refugee camps.

The frontier is harsh but survivable. Forests, hills, old watchtowers from border wars a century past. The Communion's presence here is thin — patrols, not garrisons. People who know the land can avoid them.

## Gorundtzal

A waystation town on the northern trade road, two days' ride from the mountain passes. Before the fall, it was a resupply point for caravans — stone walls, a good well, stables, a market square. Now the merchants are gone and the buildings are half-ruined from a Communion raid that swept through and moved on.

Survivors have been gathering here. It offers walls, water, and enough shelter to survive the coming winter. It is not comfortable, not safe, and not organized — but it's something.

## The Grey Wasting

A slow blight that the Communion claims is spreading from underground channels beneath Valduri. Most Valduri have never seen it and consider it a foreign superstition or a pretext for invasion. But some frontier folk have noticed patches of earth where nothing grows, water that tastes of ash, and animals that sicken without visible cause. Whether this is the Grey Wasting or simply poor soil is a matter of debate.

## The Old Ways

Valduri has legends about the land being alive — channels of energy running beneath the earth, groves where the sick recover faster, ruins that hum with purpose. These are folk beliefs, not organized religion. Most people treat them the way they'd treat any superstition: with a shrug and a reluctance to test them.

Some frontier communities maintain old practices — offerings at certain stones, avoidance of certain clearings, rituals at planting and harvest that have nothing to do with any god. These communities are called "rootbound" by their neighbors, usually dismissively.

## Common Knowledge

- Valduri had no standing magical tradition. If someone can do something inexplicable, it's strange and noteworthy.
- The Communion is the occupying force. They are organized, dangerous, and believe they are righteous.
- The frontier is the safest place for Valduri survivors, but "safe" is relative.
- Winter is coming. Whoever doesn't have shelter and supplies by the first frost is in serious trouble.
- Trust is scarce. Strangers might be refugees, deserters, Communion scouts, or bandits.
- The old trade infrastructure — roads, waystations, bridges — still exists but is deteriorating without maintenance.

## Distances and Travel

- **Gorundtzal to Kethenvari**: Five days south by road (when the road was safe). Now closer to seven or eight, avoiding Communion patrols.
- **Gorundtzal to the Mountain Passes**: Two days north. The passes lead to neutral territory — mountain clans who want nothing to do with the conflict.
- **Gorundtzal to the nearest Communion garrison**: Three days southeast. Patrols range further.
- **Gorundtzal to the western forests**: One day west. Dense, old-growth, and full of ruins from an older time. The "rootbound" communities live here.

Travel assumes on foot at a normal pace. Horses are rare and valuable since the fall.
