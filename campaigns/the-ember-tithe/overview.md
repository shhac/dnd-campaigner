# The Ember Tithe

## Setting

**World**: Valduri (homebrew) — a low-magic, post-conquest kingdom
**Era/Time Period**: Three weeks after the Fall of Kethenvari, the capital. The kingdom is shattered, its people scattered.
**Starting Location**: Gorundtzal, a ruined waystation on the northern frontier

## Tone & Themes

**Tone**: Dark romance horror — a hero's journey opening that steadily darkens as intimacy and dread weave together
**Core Themes**:
- The cost of connection — bonds that sustain the hero consume those closest to them
- Transformation as slow horror — feeling your own mind change before your body follows
- Impossible choices — every path forward demands a sacrifice
- Domain building from ruin — carving something worth protecting out of ash and occupation
- The gap between what conquerors believe and what they've done

## The Hook

Three weeks ago, the Ash Veil Communion burned Kethenvari. The kingdom of Valduri fell in a single night — its armies scattered, its nobility executed or fled, its people left to the mercy of invaders who believe mercy is a form of spiritual weakness. The hero was nobody special before the Mark appeared: a branching, ember-veined pattern that spread across their chest and arm in the chaos of the capital's fall. It pulses with warmth. It amplifies every feeling. And when the hero forms genuine bonds with others, those bonds sustain the hero's life — while slowly draining something essential from those they care about.

The hero doesn't know any of this yet. They know only that they survived when others didn't, that something changed inside them, and that a handful of other survivors are gathering at Gorundtzal, the ruined waystation where the northern trade road used to begin. There is nothing left behind them. Whatever comes next, they'll build it here.

## The Mark

The branching pattern on the hero's chest and arm — like the veins of a burning leaf, faintly luminous in low light. It appeared during the fall of Kethenvari and cannot be removed.

### What the Mark Does
- **Amplifies genuine emotion** — the hero feels everything more intensely. Joy, grief, desire, rage. This is both a gift and a curse.
- **Creates bonds** — when the hero forms a real connection with someone (not transactional, not forced), the Mark recognizes it. The bond sustains the hero, stabilizing whatever the Mark is doing to them.
- **Drains bonded companions** — slowly, imperceptibly at first. The companions lose something of themselves. Their humanity, their sense of self, their original nature. This manifests as transformation.

### What the Hero Knows
- The Mark appeared during the fall. They don't know why.
- They feel more intensely since it appeared. They assumed this was trauma.
- They feel drawn to certain people — a pull they can't explain.
- Without bonds, the hero weakens. This isn't immediately obvious but becomes clear over sessions.

### What the Hero Doesn't Know (GM Secret)
- The bonds are parasitic. The hero is sustained at the cost of those bonded to them.
- The Mark is ancient — far older than the Communion, far older than Valduri. It has appeared before.
- The Rootbound know what it is. The Communion fears it. Neither will tell the full truth willingly.

## Transformation System

> **Critical Design Note**: Transformations are the campaign's central horror. They must be handled with care and patience.

### Psychological Changes (First Phase)

Transformation begins in the mind, long before the body follows. This is the primary horror for the first several sessions.

- **New urges** — a companion begins wanting things they never wanted before. A scholar craves raw physical exertion. A gentle person fantasizes about violence. A pragmatist becomes consumed by beauty.
- **Shifted priorities** — what mattered before matters less. New obsessions creep in. The companion notices and is disturbed.
- **Thoughts that don't belong** — intrusive ideation that feels alien. "That thought wasn't mine." The companion questions their own motivations.
- **Emotional amplification in specific directions** — not general intensity (that's the hero), but narrow channels. One companion feels rage more easily. Another feels desire. Another feels protective instinct that borders on territorial.

The horror is in the noticing. Companions are aware something is changing. They may confide in the hero, in each other, or hide it. The hero may recognize the pattern or remain in denial.

### Physical Changes (Second Phase — SLOW)

Physical transformation takes many sessions to become visible. Early signs are ambiguous — could be stress, could be the frontier, could be nothing.

- **Sessions 1-3**: No visible physical changes. Psychological shifts only.
- **Sessions 4-6**: Extremely subtle. A companion's eyes catch light differently. Their skin feels warmer or cooler. Their hair texture shifts slightly. Anyone not paying close attention wouldn't notice.
- **Sessions 7-10**: Changes become noticeable to those who know them well. Still deniable. Still attractive — perhaps more so. Beauty shifts rather than diminishes. An unsettling magnetism.
- **Sessions 10+**: Changes are visible to strangers. Still not monstrous. The companion looks different, other, but remains compelling. Only deliberate neglect or acceleration pushes toward full horror.
- **Full horror threshold**: Requires many sessions AND the hero ignoring or exploiting the bond. This is not a default outcome — it's a consequence of choices.

### Attractiveness Preservation

Physical changes should maintain or shift attractiveness, not destroy it, for most of the transformation arc. This is by design — it makes the horror more insidious.

- Skin might take on an unusual tone — alabaster, bronze, faintly luminous — but it's striking, not repulsive
- Eyes might change color or gain unusual depth — mesmerizing, not frightening
- Movement might become more fluid or precise — graceful, not inhuman
- Voice might shift in resonance — more compelling, not distorted

The beauty becomes *different*. Uncanny. Magnetic in a way that makes others uneasy without knowing why. This makes the hero's choice harder: the companion is becoming something else, but they're still beautiful, still themselves (mostly), still someone the hero cares about.

### No Predetermined Path

Each companion has multiple possible transformation directions. The path is shaped by:

- **The companion's choices** — what they embrace, what they resist, what they do under pressure
- **The hero's choices** — how they treat the bond, what they encourage or discourage
- **Circumstances** — what challenges they face, what environments they spend time in, what other forces they encounter

Example directions for a single companion might include:
- Predatory grace (if they lean into aggression and physicality)
- Elemental attunement (if they spend time in specific environments)
- Empathic dissolution (if they absorb too much of others' emotions)
- Territorial guardian (if they become obsessed with protecting a place or person)

The GM should track tendencies and lean into whichever direction emerges most naturally from play. Amalgamations of multiple paths are possible and expected.

### Slowing and Delaying Transformation

Transformations can be slowed or temporarily reversed through:

- **Rituals** — specific practices (some discovered, some taught by the Rootbound) that ground a companion in their original self
- **Behavioral anchors** — engaging in activities deeply tied to who they were before the bond. A musician playing their instrument. A healer treating wounds. A scholar studying texts.
- **The hero's restraint** — consciously limiting the intensity of the bond. This weakens the hero but slows the companion's change.
- **Separation** — distance from the hero slows transformation but doesn't stop it entirely, and the bond's pull makes separation agonizing for both parties.

### Recovery

Each companion has a unique, horrible cost to claw back their humanity. The cure is thematically opposite to the flaw the transformation exploits.

- A companion transforming through rage might need to forgive the person who wronged them most
- A companion losing themselves to desire might need to choose celibacy and solitude at the moment it would hurt most
- A companion becoming territorial might need to voluntarily exile themselves from what they've claimed

Recovery is never clean. Something is always lost. But it is possible — and the campaign should make clear that fighting for it matters.

## Major Factions

### Ash Veil Communion
- **Type**: Religious military order / occupying force
- **Goals**: Cauterize the underground channels causing the Grey Wasting; bring spiritual order to a corrupted land
- **Relationship to Party**: Hostile occupiers, but fracturing internally into subfactions with different approaches

### Valduri Remnant
- **Type**: Scattered resistance / refugee network
- **Goals**: Survive, regroup, eventually reclaim the kingdom — or build something new from its ashes
- **Relationship to Party**: Potential allies, though desperate and fractured by grief and blame

### The Rootbound
- **Type**: Ancient nature-mystics tied to the land itself
- **Goals**: Preserve the deep channels beneath Valduri, which they believe are the land's living veins
- **Relationship to Party**: Cryptic potential allies who know what the Mark is but won't say plainly

## Key Locations

### Gorundtzal
A ruined waystation on the northern frontier, once the starting point of the kingdom's main trade road. Now a gathering place for survivors, deserters, and the desperate. Stone walls still stand but the roof is mostly gone. A well that still works. Enough shelter for a few dozen people if they don't mind the wind. This is where the campaign begins — and potentially where the hero builds something.

### Kethenvari (The Fallen Capital)
The seat of Valduri's power for three centuries, now occupied by the Communion. The hero fled from here. It exists in memory and rumor — a place to reclaim or mourn, depending on how the campaign unfolds. Referenced but not visited early.

### The Northern Frontier
The borderlands between settled Valduri and the wild uplands. Sparse settlements, old watchtowers, deep forests, and the remnants of trade infrastructure. The Communion's control thins here — they hold the capital and the central provinces, but the frontier is contested ground.

## The Threat

**Main Antagonist/Threat**: The Ash Veil Communion — but not as a monolith. The true threat is the intersection of occupation, the hero's parasitic bonds, and the ancient forces beneath Valduri that both the Communion and the Rootbound understand differently.
**Their Goal**: The Communion came to cauterize underground channels they believe cause the Grey Wasting (a slow blight spreading across their homeland). Valduri was collateral — the kingdom sat atop the channels. Their own structures are fracturing under the strain of occupation.
**Timeline**: Open-ended. The Communion isn't going away, but their cohesion is degrading over months. The hero's transformation timeline is measured in sessions.
**What Happens If They Succeed**: If the Communion completes its cauterization, the channels beneath Valduri die — and with them, whatever the Rootbound protect, whatever the Mark draws power from, and possibly the land's ability to sustain life.

## Campaign Arc (GM Eyes Only)

### Act 1: Ashes and Roots
The hero arrives at Gorundtzal, meets initial companions and NPCs, and begins building a foothold. The Mark's effects are subtle — amplified emotions, unexplained pulls toward certain people. Companions begin noticing psychological changes in themselves. The Communion is a distant threat; the immediate challenges are survival, shelter, and trust. The Rootbound make cryptic contact.

### Act 2: The Burning Garden
The hero's domain grows. Political entanglements with the Valduri Remnant and Communion subfactions. Transformation becomes undeniable — companions are visibly changing, and the hero must confront what the bonds are doing. Faction conflicts escalate. The Communion's internal fractures create opportunities and dangers. The Rootbound reveal more about the Mark and the channels — but their price is steep.

### Act 3: The Tithe Comes Due
Full consequences. The hero must choose what to sacrifice — their own power, their companions' humanity, or the land itself. The Communion's final push to cauterize the channels forces a confrontation. Every bond the hero has formed becomes a variable in the final equation. Multiple endings depending on accumulated choices: the hero who sacrificed everything, the hero who sacrificed everyone, the hero who found a third path.

## Naming Palettes

### Everyday Names (Creole-derived)
Used for common Valduri people — merchants, soldiers, farmers, frontier folk.
- Examples: Currodze, Pellinaste, Verdakho, Lavrenne, Ossivane, Drakkenne
- Style: French-Portuguese-Caribbean creole feel. Flowing consonant clusters, vowel-rich endings, occasional hard stops.

### Ancient/Mystical Names (Amharic-derived)
Used for the Rootbound, ancient references, mystical places, and old Valduri nobility.
- Examples: Takkele, Dessalegn, Birukta, Mesfin, Zelalem, Ayantu
- Style: Amharic-Ethiopian influence. Rounded vowels, doubled consonants, dignified rhythm.

### Communion Names (Kartvelian-derived)
Used for members of the Ash Veil Communion and their institutions.
- Examples: Tsikara, Gvirokha, Mkvari, Dzveli, Nakhevari, Sulkhan
- Style: Georgian-Kartvelian influence. Dense consonant clusters, guttural stops, imposing weight.

## House Rules

- **Low magic**: No spellcasting classes. Supernatural effects come from the Mark, the Rootbound's practices, and ancient artifacts. Healing is mundane unless the Mark or Rootbound rituals are involved.
- **Theater of mind**: Narrative combat, no grid. Positioning is described, not tracked.
- **The Mark**: Full mechanics in `mechanics/the-mark/gm-guide.md`. Bond strength, transformation progress, and hero stability are tracked per session.
- **Transformation**: Full system in `mechanics/transformation/gm-guide.md`. Sensory narration guide in `mechanics/transformation/sensory-guide.md`.
- **Romance and Intimacy**: Full framework in `mechanics/romance/gm-guide.md`. Progression milestones, scene templates, consent navigation, inter-companion dynamics.
- **Domain building**: Settlement system in `mechanics/domain-building/gm-guide.md`. Stats, improvement projects, crisis events, seasonal calendar.
- **Permadeath**: Characters can die permanently. Bonds severed by death have consequences for both the hero and remaining companions.
- **Human(?)**: All characters are human. The question mark is the point — the creeping dread of "what are we becoming?" No elves, dwarves, or other fantasy races.

## Session Zero Notes

- **Content approach**: The romance and horror elements involve intimacy, body horror (slow and specific), psychological manipulation (by the Mark itself), addiction dynamics, and moral compromise. These are features, not bugs. The campaign follows where the characters go — nothing is closed off by default. See `preferences.md` for full content guidance.
- **Player expectations**: This is a dark romance horror campaign. Relationships matter. People will change. Some will be lost. The campaign explores what it means to be human when the boundaries blur, what love costs when it's parasitic, and whether informed consent is possible when the thing you're consenting to reshapes your capacity to refuse. The question isn't whether there's a cost — it's what you're willing to pay, and whether you can still recognize yourself after paying it.
- **Character connections**: The party forms around the hero at Gorundtzal. Companions arrive over sessions — not all at once. Initial party is 3-4, growing to 4-5 active members from a designed pool of 8 companions. Not all are expected to survive.
- **Human(?)**: All characters are human. The question mark is the entire campaign.

## GM Guidance: The Mark in Play

### Early Sessions (1-3)
- The Mark is a mystery, not a system. The hero feels things more intensely. They're drawn to people. This could just be trauma.
- Don't explain anything mechanically. Let the hero discover through experience.
- When a genuine bond forms, describe the warmth, the rightness, the subtle pulse of the Mark. Make it feel good.

### Middle Sessions (4-8)
- Companions begin confiding about changes. Frame this as personal conversations, not mechanical updates.
- The hero starts to suspect the connection. Don't confirm — let them theorize.
- Introduce the cost gradually. A companion says something out of character. Another has a moment of alien hunger. The hero notices but can rationalize.

### Late Sessions (9+)
- The truth is unavoidable. The hero knows what the bonds do. Now every interaction carries weight.
- Physical changes are visible. Still attractive. Still compelling. But wrong in ways that can't be ignored.
- Recovery becomes a possibility — but the costs are terrible. Frame them as character-defining moments.

### The Horror-Romance Intersection
Different relationships should hit different notes:
- **External threat**: A bond that makes both parties stronger but draws the Communion's attention
- **Internal corruption**: A bond where the companion's transformation mirrors the hero's worst fears about themselves
- **Impossible choice**: A bond where saving the companion means sacrificing something the hero built

The single most important design principle: **transformation should make companions MORE desirable, not less, until the very end.** The horror is not that the hero is repulsed — it's that they're *not*. They should be. The companion is losing themselves. But the hero can't stop wanting them. Intimacy with a transforming companion feels *better* than before — heightened senses, amplified emotion, electric touch. The hero should be addicted to something that's harming the person they love.

When describing transformation, always lead with the attractive element before the unsettling one:
- "Her eyes have developed a depth that makes looking away feel like loss — and they haven't blinked in the last three minutes."
- "His touch carries a warmth that makes your skin sing — and his fingertips leave faint luminous traces that fade in seconds."
- "She moves with a grace that stops your breath — and her shadow moves half a beat behind her."

### Romance and Intimacy in Play
- Romantic scenes use sensory detail and emotional depth. Focus on what it FEELS like — vulnerability, electricity, the weight of knowing.
- Let intimate scenes breathe. Don't cut away artificially — follow the scene's momentum, time-jump naturally when the emotional peak has passed. If a player drives deeper, follow their lead.
- The Mark is always present in romantic scenes — warmth, pulse, the subtle sense of being fed. After every intimate scene, note the transformation cost privately.
- Romance must emerge from player agency and genuine roleplay, not GM manipulation. The Mark rewards emotional intimacy — the GM must not weaponize this to railroad romance.
- Not every companion connects through physical intimacy. Some transform through intellectual resonance, shared purpose, or emotional confrontation. The system supports every psychological configuration.
- See `mechanics/romance/gm-guide.md` for progression milestones, scene templates, and the consent spectrum.

### Combat in The Ember Tithe
Low magic means combat is grounded — no fireballs, no healing spells. What makes it distinct:
- **The Mark in combat**: Bond strength grants subtle advantages. A bonded companion near the hero fights with heightened reflexes, sharper instincts. This isn't magical — it's the bond's amplification of the companion's existing abilities. The more transformed the companion, the more pronounced the effect.
- **Transformation capabilities**: As companions transform, they gain inhuman capabilities that surface in crisis — impossible strength, predatory speed, sensory acuity beyond human norms. These moments are simultaneously useful and terrifying.
- **The cost**: Using transformation-granted capabilities in combat accelerates the transformation. The companion who saves the party with inhuman strength wakes up the next morning with hands that don't look quite right.
- **Non-lethal paths**: Most threats in Act 1 should offer retreat or negotiation options. The party is fragile. Gorundtzal's walls (once repaired) provide defensive advantages.
- **Communion encounters**: Professional soldiers, not monsters. They fight methodically. Killing them has political consequences. Capturing them creates moral dilemmas (they're people too).
