# Act 3: The Tithe Comes Due

## Status
LOCKED

## Key Secrets
- The "third path" referenced in Rootbound oral tradition was a marked individual who chose to bond with the channels directly rather than through other people. They became part of the network — not human anymore, but not monstrous either. The channels sustained themselves through the host's willing integration rather than parasitic extraction from bonded companions. The cost: the marked individual's individual existence ends. They become the land itself.
- The Communion's "final cauterization" is a massive ritual that would seal the entire channel network permanently. This would stop the Grey Wasting (which is real — the channels' dormant state leaks corruption into surrounding lands) but would also kill the land's regenerative capacity. Valduri becomes dead earth within a generation. The Communion knows this and considers it an acceptable trade.
- The channels are not natural. They are the remains of a constructed system — infrastructure built by a civilization predating both the Rootbound's ancestors and Valduri. The system was designed to distribute life energy across the land. The Mark is its maintenance protocol. The Ember Tithe is its operating cost. The Grey Wasting is what happens when the system fails.
- Each companion's transformation has a final form that corresponds to the channel system's archetypes — guardian, conduit, tender, watcher, destroyer, healer, bridge. These are not random; they reflect the channels' attempt to rebuild its maintenance infrastructure through the people bonded to the marked host. The companions aren't being consumed — they're being recruited. Whether this makes the process acceptable is the campaign's central moral question.
- The hero can sever bonds voluntarily. This stops the companion's transformation immediately but causes permanent damage to the hero (weakness, pain, potential death if too many bonds are severed). It also damages the channel network. The channels retaliate against bond-severing with the same indifference a body retaliates against surgery — pain, bleeding, scarring.

## NPC Hidden Motivations
- **Tsikara**: Knows the final cauterization will kill Valduri's land. He's made peace with this. In his theology, corrupted earth should be allowed to die so that clean earth can replace it — even if that takes generations. He is utterly sincere.
- **Sulkhan**: Will refuse to participate in the final cauterization if he learns its full consequences. He's a soldier, not a zealot. He'd rather lose the war than destroy a country. This makes him a potential ally at the worst possible moment.
- **Birukta**: Prepared to kill the hero if they choose to let their companions be fully consumed. She's seen it happen before. She carries a weapon treated with the cauterizing substance, capable of destroying the Mark (and probably the hero with it).
- **Ossivane**: His pragmatism overrides his morality under pressure. If the Communion threatens total destruction, he'll consider sacrificing the hero to the channels as a weapon — force the "third path" integration to weaponize the network against the invaders. He sees this as acceptable math.

## Planned Events
- The Communion begins the final cauterization preparation — excavation teams move toward the northern frontier
- The hero must choose: sever bonds (save companions, weaken self and channels), sustain bonds (preserve power but at companions' cost), or attempt the third path (sacrifice individual existence)
- A companion reaches the threshold of full transformation — beautiful, inhuman, and still choosing to stay. The hero faces the question: is this companion still a person? Do they want to be saved?
- The Rootbound reveal the full history, including the third path and its cost
- Communion subfactions fracture openly — Sulkhan's defection, Dzveli's attempted coup, the Ashen Penitents' desertion
- The hero's accumulated choices determine which endings are available

## Possible Endings
1. **Sacrifice**: The hero integrates with the channels, ending their individual existence. Companions are restored. The channels stabilize. The land lives. The person is gone.
2. **Consumption**: The hero sustains the bonds and lets the companions transform fully. The channels have their maintenance infrastructure. The "people" the companions were are gone, replaced by something powerful and inhuman. The hero rules a domain sustained by creatures that used to love them.
3. **Severance**: The hero cuts all bonds, nearly dying in the process. Companions are restored but damaged. The channels fail. The Grey Wasting spreads. Valduri dies slowly — but the people are free.
4. **The Third Path (true)**: The hero finds a way to bond with the channels that doesn't require consuming companions OR sacrificing their own existence. This requires accumulated choices across the campaign — alliances with the Rootbound, understanding from the Communion's texts, the royal bloodline's resonance, and the companions' willing participation. It's the hardest ending to achieve and the only one where no one has to be destroyed.
5. **Compromise**: Some bonds are severed, some are sustained. Some companions are saved, some are lost. The channels partially survive. The Communion partially succeeds. Nothing is clean. This is the most likely ending for most playthroughs.

## Foreshadowing
- The hero dreams of being the land — feeling every tree, every stone, every person walking on the earth above the channels. It feels like peace. It feels like oblivion.
- Companions who are deeply transformed begin to hear the channels — they describe it as a hum, a warmth, a voice that doesn't speak in words. They are becoming part of the system.
- The Old Shrine at Gorundtzal cracks. The branching patterns shift. Something beneath is waking up — or dying.
