# Act 3: The Tithe Comes Due

## Status
LOCKED

## Key Secrets

### The Third Path
The "third path" referenced in Rootbound oral tradition was a marked individual (Mesfin) who chose to bond with the channels directly rather than through other people. He became part of the network — not human anymore, but not monstrous either. The channels sustained themselves through his willing integration rather than parasitic extraction. The cost: Mesfin's individual existence ended. He became the land itself. His three companions chose to follow. They became the old-growth forest west of Gorundtzal. The trees are them — or what they became. The Rootbound have tended this forest for two centuries as a sacred duty.

### The Final Cauterization
The Communion's "final cauterization" is a massive ritual that would seal the entire channel network permanently. This would stop the Grey Wasting (which is real — the channels' dormant state leaks corruption) but would kill the land's regenerative capacity. Valduri becomes dead earth within a generation. The Communion knows this and considers it an acceptable trade.

### The Infrastructure Reveal
The channels are not natural. They are the remains of a constructed system — infrastructure built by a civilization predating both the Rootbound's ancestors and Valduri. The system distributed life energy across the land. The Mark is its maintenance protocol. The Ember Tithe is its operating cost. The Grey Wasting is what happens when the system runs unmaintained.

### Companions as Infrastructure
Each companion's transformation corresponds to the channel system's archetypes — guardian, conduit, tender, watcher, destroyer, healer, bridge. These aren't random; they reflect the channels' attempt to rebuild its maintenance infrastructure through bonded individuals. The companions aren't being consumed — they're being *recruited*. Whether this makes the process acceptable is the campaign's central moral question.

### Bond Severance
The hero can sever bonds voluntarily. This stops transformation immediately but causes permanent damage to the hero (weakness, pain, potential death if too many bonds are severed) and damages the channel network. The channels retaliate with the same indifference a body retaliates against surgery — pain, bleeding, scarring. The severed companion experiences withdrawal: rage, despair, disorientation, and eventually a hollow calm. Something that was part of them is gone.

## NPC Hidden Motivations

- **Tsikara**: Knows the final cauterization will kill Valduri's land. He's made peace with this. Corrupted earth should be allowed to die so clean earth can replace it — even if that takes generations. He is utterly sincere. He pities the hero.
- **Sulkhan**: Will refuse to participate in the final cauterization if he learns its full consequences. He's a soldier, not a zealot. He'd rather lose the war than destroy a country. Potential ally at the worst possible moment.
- **Birukta**: Prepared to kill the hero if they choose to let companions be fully consumed. She's seen it happen before — a marked individual who fed the system by draining those who loved them until nothing human remained. She carries a weapon treated with cauterizing substance, capable of destroying the Mark (and probably the hero with it). She loves the hero, in a distant, maternal way. That's what makes her dangerous.
- **Ossivane**: His pragmatism overrides morality under pressure. If the Communion threatens total destruction, he'll consider forcing the hero into the third path — weaponize the network against the invaders. To him, one person's existence is acceptable math against a kingdom's survival.
- **Mesfin** (through the channels): Not conscious in a human sense, but his pattern resonates more strongly as Act 3 progresses. The hero experiences his memories with increasing clarity: the love he felt, the choice he made, the peace he found. Mesfin's "voice" is seductive — not malicious, but the peace he offers requires the same surrender. He genuinely believes integration is beautiful. He may be right. He is definitely no longer equipped to understand why someone would refuse.

## Planned Events

### The Communion's Final Push
- Cauterization excavation teams move toward the northern frontier. The timeline compresses.
- Communion subfactions fracture openly — Sulkhan's defection, Dzveli's attempted coup within the Covenant Remnant, the Ashen Penitents' mass desertion.
- The final cauterization site is identified: the channel nexus beneath Kethenvari. If the Communion completes the ritual there, the entire network dies.

### The Transformation Reckoning
- A companion reaches the Threshold stage — beautiful, inhuman, and still choosing to stay. They can do things no human can. They dream of the channels every night. They describe it as "going home." The hero must answer: is this companion still a person? Do they want to be saved? Do they have the right to refuse saving?
- A companion asks the hero to stop trying to reverse their transformation. "I can feel what I'm becoming and it's not terrible. It's vast. Let me go." The hero must choose between respecting agency (which may be compromised by the transformation) and fighting for who the companion used to be.
- If the hero has been intimate with a transforming companion, the intimacy changes. Touch carries echoes of the channels. The companion's skin is warm in patterns that map underground networks. Making love to them is making love to the land. The hero must decide if this is beautiful or horrifying. The answer might be both.

### The Choice Architecture
The hero's accumulated choices determine which endings are available:

**To unlock the Sacrifice ending**: The hero must have at least one bond at strength 9-10 and must have experienced Mesfin's memories in full.

**To unlock the True Third Path**: Requires ALL of:
- Alliance with the Rootbound (trust, not just contact)
- Knowledge from the Communion's texts about the civilization that built the channels
- Lavrenne's bloodline resonance activated (she must be bonded or allied)
- At least two companions who have reached Informed Choice and chosen to stay
- The hero must have attempted recovery for at least one companion (showing they fight for people, not just power)

**Other endings** (Consumption, Severance, Compromise) are always available.

### The Rootbound's Full Revelation
- Birukta tells the hero everything: the civilization, the maintenance protocol, the previous bearers, the three paths.
- She shows the hero Mesfin's forest. She lets them touch the trees. The hero feels three people — warm, content, not-quite-gone. The companions who came with them feel the trees react to the Mark. The forest leans toward the hero. The roots shift underfoot.
- Birukta then shows the hero her weapon. "If you become what I'm afraid of, I will use this." She means it. She's crying when she says it.

## Possible Endings

### 1. Sacrifice
The hero integrates with the channels, ending their individual existence. Companions are restored — transformation reverses, painfully, over weeks. The channels stabilize. The land lives. The hero becomes Gorundtzal's heartbeat, felt in the warmth of the well water, the health of the soil, the dreams of those who sleep within the walls. They are gone. They are everywhere.

### 2. Consumption
The hero sustains the bonds and lets companions transform fully. The channels have their maintenance infrastructure. The "people" the companions were are gone, replaced by something powerful and inhuman that loves the hero with a devotion that transcends individuality. The hero rules a domain sustained by creatures that used to be women and men who chose to love them. It works. The land thrives. The Grey Wasting retreats. But at what cost?

### 3. Severance
The hero cuts all bonds, nearly dying in the process. Companions are restored but damaged — hollow, grieving, some permanently scarred by the withdrawal. The channels fail. The Grey Wasting spreads. Valduri dies slowly — but the people are free, and human, and themselves. The hero lives, weakened, with the Mark dormant and cold on their chest.

### 4. The True Third Path
The hero finds a way to bond with the channels that doesn't require consuming companions OR sacrificing their own existence. Using the Rootbound's knowledge, the Communion's texts, and Lavrenne's bloodline resonance, the hero creates a new maintenance protocol — one based on willing, informed, shared connection rather than parasitic extraction. The companions' transformations stabilize at their current level — inhuman but controlled, powerful but still themselves. Gorundtzal becomes the first node in a new network, maintained not by a single tortured host but by a community that chose this together. It's messy. It's imperfect. Some companions are more changed than others. But everyone is still a person. The land lives. The hero lives. The cost is perpetual vigilance — the system needs tending, always.

### 5. Compromise
Some bonds severed, some sustained. Some companions saved, some lost. The channels partially survive. The Communion partially succeeds. Nothing is clean. This is the most likely ending — and the most human. The hero carries scars, grieves losses, and builds what they can from what remains. Gorundtzal endures. The Mark endures. The story continues.

## Foreshadowing

- The hero dreams of being the land — feeling every tree, every stone, every person. It feels like peace. It feels like oblivion. They wake up and aren't sure which one frightens them more.
- Companions at Advanced transformation hear the channels. They describe it as a hum, warmth, a voice without words. They are becoming part of the system. Some of them prefer the voice to conversation with the hero. That hurts more than the transformation itself.
- The Old Shrine cracks. The branching patterns shift — they've been slowly rearranging since the campaign began, and now the pattern matches the hero's Mark exactly. The stone is warm to the touch. At night, it glows.
- The western forest whispers the hero's name. Not literally. But when the wind moves through the old oaks, the sound is shaped like recognition. Mesfin remembers. The forest remembers. The land is waking up.
