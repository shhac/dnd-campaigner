# Act 2: The Burning Garden

## Key Secrets

### The Settlement as Parasite
The hero's domain-building success is not entirely natural. The Mark amplifies the hero's charisma and emotional resonance — people follow them because the channels are enhancing the hero's presence. The settlement itself feeds on its inhabitants' emotional investment in the hero. Gorundtzal's unnatural health (sweet water, fast healing, out-of-season growth) is borrowed vitality — drawn from surrounding territory, which slowly dies. By mid-Act 2, the contrast between lush Gorundtzal and the dying frontier should be visible.

### The Military Rival
General Ossivane is real, alive, and building a rival power base in the western forests. He's encountered the Rootbound and has his own understanding of the channels — incomplete and filtered through military pragmatism. He sees the hero as either a weapon to use or a threat to neutralize. He'll approach with alliance in mind, but his true priority is command.

### Gvirokha's Gambit
Gvirokha's decision to release Takkele was calculated, not merciful. He's testing whether information about the Communion's activities will cause the refugees to fight or flee. If they fight, the Communion can justify escalation. If they flee, the frontier clears itself. He is, however, genuinely troubled by the cauterization — his calculation and his doubt coexist.

### The Ember Tithe as Technology
The Communion's texts reference a previous civilization that maintained the channels — not the Rootbound (who are descendants of that civilization's lowest caste) but a sophisticated culture that understood the channels as technology, not mysticism. "Ember Tithe" was that civilization's term for the Mark's function: a levy paid in human essence to keep the system running. The civilization collapsed when the last marked individual refused to pay the tithe and the channels went dormant.

### The Royal Bloodline
Lavrenne, the noblewoman claiming authority by birth, has a connection to the channels that neither she nor anyone else suspects. The Valduri royal line was originally established to serve as custodians of the channel nexus at Kethenvari. Over centuries, they forgot this purpose. But the bloodline still resonates with the channels — which is why the Communion targeted the nobility specifically.

## NPC Hidden Motivations

- **Ossivane**: Wants to unite the Valduri Remnant under military discipline. He sees the hero as a charismatic rallying point but doesn't understand the Mark. If he learns what it does, his reaction depends on pragmatism vs. morality — weapon to exploit or abomination to destroy.
- **Lavrenne**: Claims authority but is genuinely uncertain. She feels a pull toward the hero that she interprets as political instinct. The channels recognize the connection between the Mark and the royal bloodline. She doesn't know this. If she bonds with the hero, her transformation direction may lean toward **symbiotic** — the bloodline's dormant connection awakening.
- **Dzveli (Covenant Remnant)**: Pushing for human sacrifice to complete the cauterization. The channels resist more strongly as more are sealed. His solution is monstrous but his diagnosis is correct. He may attempt to kidnap marked individuals for ritual purposes.
- **Birukta (Rootbound elder)**: Knows the full Ember Tithe history but believes sharing it will doom the hero to repeat past failures. She'll resist until the hero demonstrates they prioritize companions over power. She carries a weapon treated with the cauterizing substance — her insurance policy.

## Planned Events

### Domain and Political
- Ossivane's emissaries arrive at Gorundtzal proposing an alliance — with conditions that would subordinate the hero's authority
- Lavrenne's retinue clashes with the hero's informal authority, creating political tension within the settlement
- Pellinaste's intelligence-selling is discovered (or nearly discovered) through Communion patrols arriving with knowledge they shouldn't have — creating a trust crisis

### Transformation and Horror
- A bonded companion's physical transformation becomes visible to others, forcing the hero to address it publicly or privately. How the settlement reacts defines its character.
- The hero discovers that Gorundtzal's unusual vitality has a dark source — the surrounding frontier is dying. A hunting party returns to find that a grove they visited last month is now dead earth.
- A companion at Visible transformation stage does something inhuman in a moment of crisis — saves someone with impossible strength, moves with predatory speed, or speaks with a voice that carries harmonic overtones. It's heroic. It's also terrifying. The settlement witnessed it.

### Romance and Intimacy
- The inter-companion dynamic reaches a crisis point. A companion whose bond is weaker confronts a companion whose bond is stronger — not about the hero specifically, but about the changes they've both been experiencing. "You dream about the warmth too, don't you?"
- A companion who has reached the Crisis of Knowledge milestone (learned what bonds do) confronts the hero about consent. "Did I choose this? Or did the Mark choose for me?" This conversation should be among the most emotionally intense in the campaign.
- If the hero has formed romantic bonds with multiple companions, the jealousy dynamic surfaces physically — a companion experiences the Jealousy Sickness (nausea, vertigo) when the hero is intimate with another in their presence.

### Communion Escalation
- The Communion increases pressure — a garrison established closer to Gorundtzal
- The Covenant Remnant kidnaps or targets someone connected to the hero, escalating the conflict
- Nakhevari's patrol discovers Gorundtzal's growth and he must decide whether to report it

### The Deep Revelation
- A Rootbound elder invites the hero to a deep ritual at the channel node beneath Gorundtzal. The ritual reveals: the channels' true nature (infrastructure, not nature), the Mark's purpose (maintenance protocol), the previous bearers' fates, and the cost of the tithe across centuries. The hero experiences Mesfin's memories — fragmented, emotional, overwhelming.

## Foreshadowing

- Companions bonded longest dream of the underground channels — vast networks of warmth and light. The dreams are beautiful. The companions don't want to wake up. That's the horror.
- The Grey Wasting patch in the western forest grows. Dead earth spreading in a perfect circle. Animals flee. The Rootbound are frightened — not of the Wasting, but of what the channels are doing to compensate.
- The hero's decisions carry more weight than they should — people agree too easily, follow too willingly. A new arrival notices and says so: "Everyone here watches you like you're the sun. That's not normal."
- The well water changes. Sweeter some days, iron-bitter others. It tracks the hero's emotional state without anyone realizing it.
- The old-growth forest west of Gorundtzal is warm even in winter. Snow melts at its border. The trees lean toward Gorundtzal. The Rootbound say nothing about this, but they visit the forest more often.
