# Act 1: Ashes and Roots

## Status
UNLOCKED

## Key Secrets
- The Mark appeared because the hero was at Kethenvari when the Communion breached the underground channels beneath the city. The channels, damaged and desperate, bonded to the nearest compatible human — someone experiencing extreme emotion at the nexus point. The Mark is the channels' survival mechanism.
- The bonds the hero forms are parasitic. The channels sustain the Mark (and the hero) by drawing life energy from bonded companions through the medium of genuine emotional connection. The companions' transformations are the visible cost of this extraction.
- The Rootbound have seen the Mark before — their oral tradition records at least three previous instances of the Ember Tithe, each ending differently. In one, the marked person sacrificed themselves to restore the companions. In another, the companions were fully consumed and the marked person became something inhuman. In the third (the most fragmentary), a "third path" was found that the Rootbound can no longer fully describe.
- The Communion's cauterizing substance is derived from the Grey Wasting itself — refined, concentrated, weaponized. Applying it to the channels doesn't heal them; it kills them permanently. The Communion believes this is the same thing.
- Pellinaste has been selling information to the Communion. This hasn't directly harmed the party yet, but her reports about Gorundtzal's growing population will eventually draw Communion attention.

## NPC Hidden Motivations
- **Currodze**: Hiding her desertion guilt behind competence. She abandoned her garrison post and people died. She'll work herself to exhaustion at Gorundtzal to atone, but she'll never admit what she's atoning for. She's also noticed the hero's Mark and remembers seeing similar patterns on a Rootbound elder years ago — she hasn't mentioned this because she doesn't trust her own memory.
- **Pellinaste**: Selling intelligence to the Communion while also building genuine connections at Gorundtzal. She's not a villain — she's hedging. If the Communion wins (which seems likely), she wants to be useful to them. If the settlement survives, she wants to be useful there too. Her loyalty is for sale, but it might also be earnable.
- **Takkele**: Genuinely wants to help, but doesn't fully understand her own value as an intelligence source. She's terrified that Gvirokha let her go as bait — that her escape was orchestrated to lead the Communion to refugee groups. She doesn't know if this is true, but the fear paralyzes her sometimes.
- **Ayantu (Rootbound)**: Watching the hero because the Mark demands it. The Rootbound elders are divided on whether to help the hero or kill them. Ayantu believes in help but knows the cost of the Ember Tithe. She'll test the hero's character before committing.

## Planned Events
- **Takkele's arrival** (sessions 1-2): Communion deserter reaches Gorundtzal with critical information about the excavations beneath Kethenvari and the Communion's internal fractures
- **First bond activation**: The Mark responds to the hero's first genuine emotional connection. Describe as warmth, rightness, the companion's presence feeling like a hearth fire. Do not explain mechanically.
- **Psychological shift begins** (sessions 2-4): A bonded companion experiences the first signs — intrusive thoughts, shifted priorities, emotional responses that don't feel like their own. Play this in character conversations, not narration.
- **External threat** (sessions 2-3): A Communion patrol deviation, bandit probe, or refugee crisis tests the settlement and the hero's leadership
- **Rootbound contact** (sessions 3-5): Ayantu or Dessalegn approaches the party. They recognize the Mark. They call it by its old name. They offer limited help — grounding rituals, channel knowledge — in exchange for the hero's willingness to face what the Mark means.
- **The Old Shrine resonance**: When the hero passes the Old Shrine in the market square, the Mark pulses in recognition. The branching patterns on the stone match the patterns on the hero's skin. This is noticeable but not explained.
- **Winter arrives**: The first frost forces decisions about resources, shelter, and who stays. This is a survival pressure, not a plot event, but it shapes everything.

## Horror Seeds (Plant These in Act 1)

These are specific moments the GM should look for opportunities to deploy:

- **The Sleep-Walker**: A bonded companion is found outside at night, standing at the Old Shrine with palms pressed flat against the branching patterns. They don't remember walking there. The stone is warm under their hands. The hero's Mark pulses in response.
- **The Animal Reaction**: A dog or horse reacts with terror to a bonded companion — hackles up, backing away, teeth bared. Animals sense the transformation before humans. The companion is hurt and confused.
- **The Mirror Moment**: A companion catches their reflection and freezes — not because they look different, but because they *like* what they see in a way they never did before. A formerly modest person captivated by their own face.
- **The Wrong Appetite**: During a meal, a companion is repulsed by cooked food. Raw meat smells *right*. They catch themselves staring at blood pooling from a butchered rabbit and feeling something that uses hunger's language. They eat the cooked meal anyway. The hero notices they barely touch it.
- **The Dream Report**: A companion tells the hero about a dream — vast warm light beneath the earth, the feeling of connection to everything. They describe it with longing, then stop: "I've never dreamed like that before. It felt more real than being awake." The horror is the preference.

## Romance Seeds (Plant These in Act 1)

- **The Wound-Tending Scene**: After danger, physical proximity, vulnerability, pain and care mingled. The Mark pulses. Silence that says everything.
- **The Night Watch**: Two alone while others sleep. Quiet confession that turns into something more. "I keep dreaming about you — like you're inside me."
- **The Territorial Flare**: A companion who has no romantic claim feels sudden irrational rage when another companion sits too close to the hero. The rage passes in seconds. They're shaken. "I don't know what that was."
- **The First Touch**: The moment physical contact crosses from practical to loaded. A hand that lingers. A brush of fingers. The Mark's warmth surging at the contact point. Both parties notice. Neither pulls away.

## Foreshadowing

- The well water at Gorundtzal is always slightly warm and tastes of iron — it draws from the channel network. Track its changes: as the hero grows stronger, the water tastes sweeter. As the Communion advances, it tastes of ash.
- The Old Shrine's branching patterns are identical to the hero's Mark — both are maps of the same underground system. The hero should discover this gradually, not all at once.
- Bonded companions sometimes feel warmer than they should, especially near the hero — as if something is flowing between them. This is most noticeable during intimate moments.
- The frontier forest is more alive than it should be this time of year — green where it should be browning, flowers blooming out of season. The channels are close to the surface here. The forest leans toward Gorundtzal.
- Takkele's cauterizing substance smells the same as the distant chemical scent that occasionally drifts from the south — the Communion's camps and the Grey Wasting share a source.
- Sounds near the Old Shrine carry differently. Conversations echo with a half-second delay. Music played in the market square has resonance it shouldn't. Something beneath is listening.
