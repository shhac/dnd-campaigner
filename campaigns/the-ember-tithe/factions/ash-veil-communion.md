# Ash Veil Communion

**Type**: Religious Military Order / Occupying Force
**Influence**: Regional (dominant in occupied Valduri, continental power in Tskhenavri)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

The Ash Veil Communion is a theocratic military order from the eastern empire of Tskhenavri. They worship through purification — fire, ash, sacrifice. Their theology holds that spiritual corruption flows through underground channels like infected blood, and only cauterization can cure it. They invaded Valduri to reach and destroy the channel network beneath the kingdom, which they believe is the source of the Grey Wasting — a slow blight spreading into their homeland. Whether they're right about the cause or simply desperate for a solution is one of the campaign's central ambiguities.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: Cauterize the corrupted channels beneath Valduri to stop the Grey Wasting from spreading into Tskhenavri. Bring spiritual order to a land they consider tainted.
**True Goal**: The same, but fractured. Three subfactions now pursue this goal in incompatible ways, and the cohesion that made the invasion possible is degrading under the strain of occupation.

## Methods

- Overwhelming military force for initial conquest, followed by occupation and control of key sites
- Ritual cauterization of underground channels using a burning substance refined by their priests
- Conscription of local labor for excavation work
- Religious conversion (offered, not forced — but refusing carries social consequences)
- Systematic documentation of channel locations using ancient maps and new surveying

## Structure

**Leadership**: High Priest Tsikara (overall command), supported by military commander Sulkhan and a council of senior priests
**Hierarchy**: High Priest > Senior Priests > Priests > Acolytes > Soldiers > Conscripts
**Membership**: Approximately three thousand soldiers and five hundred priests/acolytes in Valduri. Many more in Tskhenavri.

### Subfactions

#### The Cauterists
The dominant faction. Orthodox hardliners who believe the mission is righteous, the methods are necessary, and any suffering caused is justified by the greater threat of the Grey Wasting. Led informally by Gvirokha, who embodies their contradictions — utterly convinced of the cause but capable of individual mercy (as shown by letting Takkele escape).

#### The Ashen Penitents
A growing minority who question whether the Communion has the right to destroy another people's land to save their own. They don't doubt the Grey Wasting is real — they doubt the morality of the cure. Some advocate negotiation with Valduri survivors. Others simply want to go home. They have no clear leader, which makes them less dangerous politically but harder to suppress.

#### The Covenant Remnant
The smallest and most extreme faction. They believe the cauterization requires more sacrifices — human sacrifice, specifically, to sanctify the burning. They draw on older, darker traditions within Communion theology that the mainstream disavows. Their influence is growing as the occupation stalls and frustration mounts. Led by a priest named Dzveli, who speaks softly and terrifies everyone.

## Resources

- **Wealth**: Significant — funded by the Tskhenavri empire, though supply lines are stretching thin
- **Military**: Army — three thousand disciplined soldiers with cavalry, siege equipment, and supply lines
- **Magic**: None (low magic setting). Their "rituals" are psychological and chemical, not supernatural — but the cauterizing substance has genuine effects on the channels.
- **Political**: Dominant in occupied Valduri, declining in Tskhenavri (the invasion was supposed to be quick)

**Colors**: Ash-grey primary, burnt orange accents
**Recognition Signs**: Ritual scarification (parallel lines on forearms for acolytes, branching burns on shoulders for priests), specific prayer cadences, the smell of burnt offerings

## Hooks & Opportunities

- Takkele's information about internal fractures creates opportunities for manipulation or alliance with individual subfactions
- The Ashen Penitents might be turned against the hardliners if given a reason
- Sulkhan's discomfort with the religious elements could be exploited
- Gvirokha's relationship with Takkele is a potential backchannel
- The Covenant Remnant's push for human sacrifice could alienate moderate Communion members
- Intercepted communications or captured patrols could reveal the Communion's internal conflicts

## GM Notes

The Communion should never feel monolithic. Individual soldiers are people — some believe, some follow orders, some are having doubts. The subfaction structure creates political texture: different Communion members want different things, and the hero can play them against each other.

Key dynamics to track:
- **Tsikara's grip on power**: He holds the Communion together through personal authority. If his confidence wavers — or if the subfactions can be pushed further apart — the occupation becomes unstable.
- **The cauterization timeline**: The Communion is working through the channels systematically. Each node they cauterize makes the underground network weaker — and makes the hero's Mark weaker too, though this isn't immediately obvious.
- **Supply line strain**: The invasion was supposed to be quick. It wasn't. Tskhenavri is getting impatient. If supplies slow or stop, the Communion becomes more desperate.
- **The Grey Wasting is real**: The Communion isn't wrong about the channels being connected to the blight in their homeland. They may be wrong about cauterization being the only solution. This moral ambiguity is deliberate — the enemy has a legitimate grievance.

The Communion knows about the Mark. Their texts reference "the Ember Tithe" — a phenomenon where the channels choose a host and sustain that host through the life force of bonded individuals. They consider this an abomination, proof of the channels' corruption. If they discover the hero is marked, they will prioritize killing them.
