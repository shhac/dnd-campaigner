# Valduri Remnant

**Type**: Scattered Resistance / Refugee Network
**Influence**: Local (fragmented across regions)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

The remnants of Valduri's people — not an organization, but a shared identity among those who survived the fall and refuse to accept Communion rule. They have no central leadership, no unified strategy, and no consensus on what "resistance" even means. Some want to fight. Some want to survive. Some want to negotiate. All of them are hungry, frightened, and angry. What holds them together is grief and a kingdom-shaped hole in their identities.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: Survive, regroup, reclaim Valduri — or build something new from its ashes
**True Goal**: There is no unified true goal. The Remnant is a collection of competing visions:
- Former military officers want to organize a fighting resistance
- Former merchants want to establish trade networks that bypass the Communion
- Refugees simply want safety, food, and shelter
- Some quietly believe submission is the only realistic option

## Methods

- Refugee networks sharing information, supplies, and routes
- Informal militias forming around former soldiers
- Smuggling goods and people past Communion patrols
- Scattered sabotage — bridges burned, supply carts raided, information planted
- Diplomatic overtures to the mountain clans and coastal communities

## Structure

**Leadership**: None unified. Several figures claim authority:
- General Ossivane (rumored to be gathering forces in the western forests)
- A council of merchants in the coastal city of Drakhenne (operating under Communion noses)
- Various local leaders who've taken charge of refugee groups (like Currodze at Gorundtzal)

**Hierarchy**: Informal and local. Whoever feeds you is in charge.
**Membership**: Thousands of displaced Valduri, but only hundreds actively resisting. Most are focused on survival.

## Resources

- **Wealth**: Poor — what people carried when they fled. Some hidden caches from the merchant guilds.
- **Military**: Militia — scattered former soldiers with salvaged equipment. No organized force yet.
- **Magic**: None (low magic setting)
- **Political**: None — the kingdom's political structure collapsed with the capital

## Symbols & Recognition

**Symbol**: No unified symbol yet. Some use the Valduri royal crest (a river through a valley); others reject it as a symbol of a failed system.
**Colors**: No unified colors
**Recognition Signs**: Shared experiences — survivors identify each other by the stories they tell, the places they fled from, the people they lost

## Relationship to Party

**Current Standing**: Potential allies — the hero is one of them, whether they want the responsibility or not
**History**: The hero is a Valduri survivor. Whatever they build at Gorundtzal becomes part of the Remnant by default.

## Hooks & Opportunities

- Building Gorundtzal into a functioning settlement makes the hero a Remnant leader by default
- Ossivane's rumored army in the western forests could be a powerful ally — or a rival for leadership
- Lavrenne's claim to authority by birth will create political conflict with the hero's earned authority
- The merchant networks in Drakhenne could provide supplies and intelligence if a connection is established
- Former soldiers arriving at Gorundtzal can be organized into a militia, but they need purpose and discipline

## GM Notes

The Valduri Remnant isn't a faction the hero joins — it's a faction the hero helps create. In the early sessions, "the Remnant" is just a name for scattered survivors. The hero's actions at Gorundtzal — building walls, organizing people, making alliances — are what turn scattered refugees into something with purpose.

This creates natural political tension:
- **Ossivane** (if he exists) represents military resistance — fight the Communion directly
- **Lavrenne** represents the old order — restore the monarchy, restore normalcy
- **The hero** represents something new — domain building, practical leadership, complicated by the Mark

The hero doesn't have to choose one vision. They can blend them, reject them, or ignore them. But the Remnant's future shape will be influenced by these competing pulls.

Key dynamics:
- Every survivor who arrives at Gorundtzal brings skills AND problems — feuds, trauma, divided loyalties
- As Gorundtzal grows, it becomes a target. The Communion will notice. Other Remnant groups will want to absorb or dominate it.
- The hero's bonds with companions make them supernaturally effective as a leader (the Mark amplifies charisma and emotional connection), but this effectiveness is built on exploitation. The irony should be visceral.
