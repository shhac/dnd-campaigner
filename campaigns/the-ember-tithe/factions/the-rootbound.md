# The Rootbound

**Type**: Ancient Nature-Mystic Tradition
**Influence**: Local (western forests and frontier communities)
**Campaign**: The Ember Tithe

## Overview

The Rootbound are not an organization — they're a way of life. Scattered communities of frontier Valduri who maintain old practices tied to the land itself: offerings at certain stones, avoidance of certain clearings, rituals at planting and harvest that have nothing to do with any god. Mainstream Valduri dismiss them as superstitious rustics. The Communion doesn't know they exist as anything more than backward villagers. In truth, the Rootbound are the last people who remember (however imperfectly) what the underground channels are and how to tend them.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: Live in harmony with the land, maintain the old practices, be left alone
**True Goal**: Preserve the channel network that runs beneath Valduri. They believe the channels are the land's living veins — damage them and the land dies. The Communion's cauterization terrifies them.

## Methods

- Oral tradition passed through generations of tenders
- Rituals at channel nodes (the flat stones with branching patterns found throughout the frontier)
- Deliberate secrecy — they don't evangelize, recruit, or explain themselves
- Careful observation of outsiders before making contact
- When they must act, they act through proxies, suggestions, and manipulated circumstances rather than direct intervention

## Structure

**Leadership**: Consensus among elders. No single leader. Authority is earned through knowledge, not appointment.
**Hierarchy**: Elders > Tenders (those who maintain channel sites) > Community members
**Membership**: Perhaps two hundred people across a dozen scattered communities in the western forests and frontier hills

## Resources

- **Wealth**: Poor by mainstream standards. They trade for necessities and produce the rest.
- **Military**: None. They are not fighters. A few hunters with bows, but no organized defense.
- **Magic**: Significant — not magic in the traditional sense, but their practices genuinely affect the channel network. They can sense the channels' health, communicate through them across distances, and perform rituals that strengthen or soothe damaged nodes. This is the closest thing to real magic in the campaign.
- **Political**: None. Deliberately invisible to power structures.

## Symbols & Recognition

**Symbol**: Branching patterns — the same motif found on the Old Shrine, the Palace of Branches, and the hero's Mark. The Rootbound recognize these as maps of the channel network.
**Colors**: No uniforms, but Rootbound tend to wear undyed cloth and wooden jewelry carved with channel patterns
**Recognition Signs**: Knowledge of channel sites, certain phrases in an older dialect, the ability to sense channel health (warmth or cold at specific locations)

## Relationship to Party

**Current Standing**: Watching. The hero's Mark has drawn their attention. They're not sure whether the hero is a blessing or a catastrophe.
**History**: No direct contact yet. The hero may have grown up hearing dismissive references to "rootbound folk" in the frontier communities.

## Relationship to Other Factions

| Faction | Relationship |
|---------|--------------|
| Ash Veil Communion | Unknown to the Communion, but the Rootbound consider them an existential threat |
| Valduri Remnant | Technically part of Valduri, but always marginalized. Now their knowledge is suddenly relevant. |

## Hooks & Opportunities

- The Rootbound know what the Mark is. They call it "the Ember Tithe" — the channels choosing a host to sustain them through bonds with others.
- They can teach the hero to slow companions' transformations through grounding rituals
- Their knowledge of the channel network is essential for understanding the Communion's plan and finding alternatives to cauterization
- They can sense when channels are damaged or dying — useful intelligence about the Communion's progress
- An alliance gives the hero access to frontier survival knowledge, hidden paths, and a network of watchers
- Their practices could be key to the companions' recovery — but the Rootbound's help comes with conditions

## GM Notes

The Rootbound are the campaign's truth-keepers. They know (in fragmentary, mythologized form) what the channels are, what the Mark does, and what the Ember Tithe means. But they won't dump this information. They're cautious, oblique, and frankly frightened of the marked hero.

Their fear is not irrational. In their oral tradition, the Ember Tithe has happened before — and it ended badly. The channels chose a host, the host bonded with others, and the others transformed into something inhuman. The Rootbound remember this as the reason the channels went underground in the first place: the last time they were fully active, they consumed people. The channels aren't evil, but they're not benevolent either. They're a system, and the Mark is how that system sustains itself.

What the Rootbound can offer:
- **Grounding rituals** that slow transformation — these are behavioral anchors codified into practice. They work, but they require discipline and sacrifice from the companion.
- **Channel sense** — the ability to feel the network's health. This gives the hero strategic intelligence.
- **Historical context** — fragmentary but crucial. The Rootbound's oral tradition, combined with the Communion's texts and the Palace of Branches records, can eventually reveal the full picture.
- **The third path** — the Rootbound believe there's an alternative to the Communion's cauterization that preserves both the channels and the people bonded to them. They don't know what it is. Finding it is potentially the campaign's endgame.

What they demand:
- Respect for the channels and the old practices
- Protection from the Communion (they know they'll be targets if discovered)
- The hero's willingness to face what the Mark really means — no denial, no half-measures
- Potentially: the hero's willingness to sacrifice their own power to save their companions

Ayantu should be the first contact — wise, measured, testing the hero with questions that seem simple but aren't. Dessalegn can be a more relatable point of contact — young, scared, willing to share more than the elders approve of. Birukta is the endgame — the one who knows the most and trusts the least.
