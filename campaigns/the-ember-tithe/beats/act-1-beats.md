# Act 1 Beat Sheet: Ashes and Roots

**Opening State**: The hero arrives at Gorundtzal, a ruined waystation on the northern frontier, three weeks after the fall of Kethenvari. About thirty survivors are gathered here. No one is in charge. Winter is two weeks away. The hero carries the Mark — a branching pattern on their chest and arm that pulses in low light. They don't know what it means.

---

## Beat 1: Arrival and Assessment

**Goal**: Establish the setting, introduce key NPCs, give the hero their first choices

### What Happens
- The hero arrives at Gorundtzal to find a functional ruin — walls with a gap, a working well, scattered survivors
- Currodze is the first meaningful NPC contact — she's been organizing supplies and immediately assesses the hero as another resource to manage
- Pellinaste is visible at the edge of the square, trading cautiously from her cart
- The survivors are disorganized, afraid, and cold. Some are arguing about whether to stay or push north to the mountain passes.

### Party Opportunities
- Talk to Currodze (practical information, supply status, immediate needs)
- Talk to Pellinaste (trade goods, frontier knowledge, wider situation)
- Explore Gorundtzal (the gap in the wall, the watchtower, the Old Shrine, the root cellar)
- Address the arguing survivors (leadership opportunity)
- Examine the Mark in private (it pulses, it's warm, it's not going away)

### Key Decisions to Prompt
- Does the hero step up to organize, or hang back?
- What's the first priority — the wall, food, scouting, or people?
- How does the hero respond to being assessed by Currodze — with deference, partnership, or assertion?

### Tone
Quiet desperation with a thread of possibility. This could be something. It could also fall apart.

---

## Beat 2: The First Bond

**Goal**: Establish the Mark's emotional amplification. The hero forms a genuine connection with someone — not romantic yet, but real. The Mark responds.

### What Happens
- Through shared work, conversation, or crisis, the hero connects genuinely with a companion
- The Mark warms. The hero feels a rightness, a pull. This feels good — and that's the trap.
- The companion may or may not notice anything. The hero notices.
- This should happen organically through roleplay, not mechanically triggered

### What the GM Should Watch For
- Moments of genuine emotional exchange between the hero and a companion
- The hero confiding something real, or a companion doing the same
- Shared danger that creates trust
- Any moment where the hero feels something and the Mark responds

### GM Notes
Do NOT announce this mechanically. Describe the warmth, the certainty, the way the companion's presence feels like standing near a fire. Make it feel like emotional truth, not supernatural mechanics. The hero should think "I connected with this person" not "the Mark activated."

---

## Beat 3: Takkele's Arrival

**Goal**: Introduce the Communion's internal conflict, seed the deeper mystery, add urgency

### What Happens
- Takkele arrives at Gorundtzal — exhausted, starving, bearing Communion scarification marks
- Immediate tension: she looks like the enemy
- She begs for shelter and offers information in exchange
- She reveals: the Communion is excavating beneath Kethenvari, the channels are real, and what they're doing to them is monstrous

### Party Opportunities
- Interrogate Takkele (how much do they trust her?)
- Examine her claims (the stolen cauterizing substance, her knowledge of Communion structure)
- Debate among companions: is she a spy? A refugee? Both?
- React to the revelation that the Communion's claims about underground channels are at least partially true

### Key Decisions
- Does the hero shelter Takkele or turn her away?
- How does the party handle the information she brings?
- Does anyone examine the cauterizing substance?

### Tone
Urgent, tense, morally complicated. She's telling the truth and she looks like a threat.

---

## Beat 4: The Psychological Shift (First Signs)

**Goal**: Plant the seeds of transformation horror. A bonded companion notices something wrong in their own mind.

### What Happens
- Over the course of normal interaction, a bonded companion says or does something slightly out of character
- Not dramatic. Not monstrous. Just... off. A gentle person snaps unexpectedly. A pragmatist fixates on beauty. A stoic cries at nothing.
- The companion notices. They're disturbed. They may confide in the hero or hide it.
- The hero may recognize this as connected to the Mark or dismiss it as stress

### How to Play This
- Embed it in normal conversation. Don't spotlight it with dramatic narration.
- Let the companion's player (AI) notice and react. The discomfort should come from the character, not the GM's description.
- If the companion confides, make the conversation intimate and real. This is the horror-romance intersection: vulnerability, closeness, and the unsettling feeling that something is feeding on it.

### GM Notes
This is the first hint that bonds have a cost. It should be deniable — stress explains everything at this point. But the hero should feel a flicker of unease, even if they don't know why.

---

## Beat 5: The First External Threat

**Goal**: Create urgency, test the settlement's defenses, give the hero a chance to lead in crisis

### What Happens (choose or combine):
- **Communion patrol deviation**: The regular patrol swings closer than usual, or a second patrol appears on a different schedule
- **Bandit probe**: A group of armed survivors tests Gorundtzal's defenses, looking for weakness
- **Refugee crisis**: A large group of refugees arrives — too many to feed, some injured, some bringing trouble behind them

### Party Opportunities
- Defensive planning (the gap in the wall becomes critical)
- Leadership under pressure (who gives orders? who follows?)
- Combat or negotiation (depending on the threat)
- Aftermath: the settlement is shaken but intact (or not), and the hero's role solidifies

### Key Decisions
- How does the hero handle violence? Strategically? Reluctantly? Eagerly?
- Does the hero protect everyone equally, or prioritize companions?
- How do the companions perform under pressure — and does the Mark's influence show?

---

## Beat 6: Rootbound Contact

**Goal**: Introduce the Rootbound, connect the Mark to the larger mystery, give the hero access to new knowledge

### What Happens
- A figure appears at the edge of the western forest. They've been watching Gorundtzal.
- Ayantu (or Dessalegn) makes contact — cautiously, on their terms
- They recognize the Mark. Their reaction is a mix of awe and fear. They call it by its old name.
- They offer help — but they want to understand the hero first

### Party Opportunities
- Talk to the Rootbound contact (what do they know about the Mark?)
- Visit the Rootbound Grove (the Mark responds to the channel node there)
- Learn about grounding rituals that can slow transformation
- Establish a relationship with the frontier communities

### Key Information Available
- The Mark is called "the Ember Tithe" — but they won't explain the full meaning yet
- The channels beneath Valduri are real and alive. The Rootbound tend them.
- The Communion's cauterization is killing the channels. This will kill the land.
- The Mark makes the hero important — and dangerous. They need to be careful.

### Tone
Mysterious, respectful, slightly unsettling. The Rootbound know something the hero doesn't, and they're not sure whether sharing it is wise.

---

## Session End States

### Strong Ending Points
1. **After Beat 2** — The first bond is formed, the Mark responds, and the hero has a new emotional anchor. End on warmth.
2. **After Beat 3** — Takkele has arrived and the Communion's true activities are revealed. End on revelation and unease.
3. **After Beat 5** — The first threat has been handled and the hero's leadership is established. End on hard-won relief.
4. **After Beat 6** — The Rootbound have made contact and the Mark has a name. End on mystery.

### Open Threads for Act 2
- What is the Ember Tithe, and what does it mean for the hero and their companions?
- How far has the Communion's cauterization progressed?
- What is General Ossivane doing in the western forests?
- The companion's psychological shift — is it getting worse?
- Can the Rootbound's grounding rituals actually slow transformation?
- Who or what was the figure Takkele saw in the tunnels with branching marks?

---

## GM Notes

### Pacing
- Act 1 should take 3-5 sessions. Don't rush the early bonds — they need to feel genuine for the later horror to land.
- Beats don't need to happen in strict order. The first bond (Beat 2) should happen naturally; the external threat (Beat 5) can come whenever the settlement needs shaking up.
- The Rootbound contact (Beat 6) should come AFTER the companion's psychological shift (Beat 4), so the hero has reason to seek answers.

### Transformation Tracking
- Keep a private log of transformation tendencies for each bonded companion
- Note which directions their choices and circumstances are pushing them
- Share nothing with players. Let it emerge through narrative.

### The Mark's Behavior
- The Mark responds to genuine emotion, not proximity. Forced closeness doesn't feed it.
- When a bond deepens, describe warmth, rightness, a sense of the companion's presence even at distance
- When a companion begins to shift, the Mark may pulse differently near them — a subtle change the hero might notice

### Combat at Early Levels
- The party is fragile. Early combat should offer retreat options and non-lethal resolution paths.
- Gorundtzal's walls (once repaired) provide defensive advantages.
- Currodze's hidden weapon cache becomes available if trust is earned — this is a significant power boost.

### Character Spotlights
- Ensure every companion gets at least one meaningful scene in Act 1
- The first bond doesn't have to be romantic — it can be trust, respect, or shared purpose
- Track which companions the hero gravitates toward — this shapes transformation directions
