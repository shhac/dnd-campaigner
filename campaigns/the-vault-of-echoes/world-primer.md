# World Primer: Kanakkur

What any resident of Kanakkur would know.

---

## The City

Kanakkur is the City of Binding Contracts. Built on ancient foundations no one can date, it rose to prominence through a single innovation: contract magic. Agreements made here carry weight — literally. The ink binds, the words compel, and breaking your word has consequences written into your flesh.

The city sits on a river delta, built in tiers. The upper tiers hold the Marai Quarter (the elite estates and Counting Houses), Kovil Plaza (the civic center), and the ward-spires that anchor the city's magical infrastructure. The lower tiers descend through markets and working districts into Teff Row, where the underworld operates in the gaps between what's legal and what's enforced.

## The Contract System

Every binding agreement in Kanakkur is inscribed on enchanted parchment using debt-mark ink. When both parties sign, the contract becomes magically enforceable:

- **Debt-marks** appear on the debtor's skin — usually the forearm. They burn when the debtor acts against the contract's terms.
- **Echo-wards** throughout the city can detect debt-marks, making it nearly impossible for debtors to flee.
- **Contract breaches** trigger escalating penalties: pain, compulsion to return to the creditor, and eventually paralysis.

Most contracts are fair — merchant agreements, employment terms, property leases. The system works because people trust it. But "fair" is a matter of perspective when one party writes the terms and the other signs out of desperation.

## The Counting Houses

Four great mercantile dynasties control Kanakkur's economy:

- **Selvan House**: The largest and most powerful. Controls banking, major contracts, and the Nallur Vaults. Led by Thirunavukkarasu Selvan, called "The Composer."
- **Kovil House**: Specializes in trade and shipping. Conservative, traditional.
- **Nallur House**: Masters of ward-craft and magical infrastructure. Maintains the city's enchantments.
- **Pandyan House**: Youngest of the four. Controls real estate and construction. Ambitious, willing to bend rules.

The Houses compete but cooperate on maintaining the contract system that makes them all wealthy. The Selvan House holds an unofficial "first among equals" position.

## The Echoguard

Kanakkur's magical law enforcement. They patrol the city, enforce contract law, and protect the Counting Houses' interests. Their signature ability is echo-reading — they can "listen" to the magical echoes that permeate the city to detect contract breaches, track debtors, and investigate crimes. They wear deep blue uniforms with silver ward-sigils and carry staves that channel the echo-ward network.

Commander Murugan Velan runs the Echoguard from their headquarters in Kovil Plaza.

## Teff Row

The underworld district in Kanakkur's lower tiers. Named for the grain that sustained the city's poor in its early days — cheap, filling, and overlooked by the wealthy. Now it's where you go for things the Counting Houses don't want traded: forged contracts, counterfeit debt-mark ink, stolen goods, information, and the services of people who don't ask questions.

Mesfin the Quiet runs the largest criminal operation in Teff Row from Warka Hall. Other operators exist, but Mesfin sets the terms.

## The Unbound

A resistance movement dedicated to destroying the contract system. They see debt-marks as slavery and the Counting Houses as slavers. Most citizens consider them dangerous radicals. Some debtors consider them the only hope.

They operate in cells. Their leader, known as Bereket, has never been seen by the Echoguard. Their symbol is a broken chain drawn in debt-mark ink — a deliberate provocation.

## The Festival of Sealed Words

Kanakkur's most important civic holiday, held annually. Citizens gather in Kovil Plaza to witness the ceremonial renewal of the city's founding contracts. The Counting Houses sponsor feasts, performances, and demonstrations of contract magic. The wards are recharged, debts are publicly settled (or publicly enforced), and the Echoguard parades in full regalia.

For most citizens, it's a celebration. For debtors, it's a reminder.

## Daily Life

Most people in Kanakkur live under at least one contract — employment, housing, merchant agreements. The system is woven into everything. You contract for your apprenticeship, your market stall, your marriage. Most contracts are reasonable. But the weight of them accumulates, and for the poor, a single bad season can mean a lifetime of debt-marks.

The wealthy live in the Marai Quarter and rarely think about the system that serves them. The working class lives in the middle tiers and navigates contracts carefully. The debtors live wherever they can and avoid the Echoguard.

## Currency

The standard currency is the **seal** — a small disc of enchanted metal stamped with the Counting Houses' collective mark. Seals can't be counterfeited (the enchantment is self-verifying), which makes them reliable but also traceable. The underworld prefers unmarked trade goods, favors, and information.
