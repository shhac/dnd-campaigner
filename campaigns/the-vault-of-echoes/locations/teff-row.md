# Teff Row

**Type**: District (Underworld)
**Region**: Lower tier, Kanakkur
**Campaign**: The Vault of Echoes

## Overview

Teff Row is Kanakkur's underbelly — the district where the contract system's losers live, work, and find ways to survive outside the legitimate economy. Named for the cheap grain that fed the city's first laborers, it's now a dense warren of tenements, unlicensed markets, pawnshops, and establishments that don't appear on any official map. Mesfin the Quiet controls the largest criminal operation here, but Teff Row is too sprawling and too stubbornly independent for any one person to own completely.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Narrow streets where buildings lean toward each other, blocking most daylight. Laundry lines strung between upper floors create a patchwork canopy. Debt-marks glow on every other forearm. Ward-light is sparse — the echo-wards thin out here, creating blind spots that residents have mapped and memorized. Graffiti in debt-mark ink: broken chains, crude anti-House slogans, the Unbound's symbol.

**Sounds**: The hum of the echo-wards is quieter here — background noise rather than the pervasive presence of the upper tiers. Instead: haggling, children, the clatter of workshops, and the occasional sharp silence that means someone with authority just walked in.

**Smells**: Cooking teff (the grain flatbread that gives the district its name). Cheap lamp oil. The metallic tang of counterfeit contract ink — someone's always brewing a batch. Deeper in: sweat, desperation, and the sharp chemical smell of debt-mark removal attempts.

**Feeling**: Defiant poverty. Teff Row knows what it is and refuses to be ashamed. There's a solidarity here that doesn't exist in the upper tiers — people look out for each other because no one else will. But there's also danger: desperate people make dangerous choices, and Mesfin's enforcers ensure that solidarity has limits.

## Key Features

### Warka Hall
Mesfin the Quiet's headquarters. A converted warehouse that serves as court, social club, and operations center. The ground floor is a tavern that serves anyone. The upper floors are Mesfin's domain — offices, meeting rooms, and the records of every debt, favor, and arrangement in Teff Row. Named for the fig tree that shades its entrance.

### The Grain Market
Teff Row's legitimate commercial face. Open-air stalls selling food, basic goods, and everyday necessities. Also the primary dead-drop location for the district's information economy. Hagos's shop is one street east.

### The Warrens
A section of Teff Row where buildings have been connected by tunnels, crawlways, and rooftop bridges, creating an alternative transit system that bypasses the streets entirely. Goblins, halflings, and other small-folk navigate it easily. Humans can use it but uncomfortably. The Echoguard has tried to map it and given up.

## Points of Interest

- **Hagos's Shop**: Pawnbroker and fence. Back room available for private meetings (for a fee). Mesfin gets a report on all unusual activity.
- **The Scar Parlor**: An underground operation that removes debt-marks — painfully, imperfectly, and illegally. Run by a Vel'ashi alchemist who charges more than most debtors can afford.
- **The Unbound Drop**: A nondescript tenement that serves as one of the Unbound's dead-drop locations. The crew can establish contact here if they know where to look.
- **The Tinker's Warrens**: Goblin neighborhood in the lower section of the district. Sparks grew up here. The community maintains its own internal economy and governance.

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Mesfin's enforcers**: 2-4 person teams that patrol Teff Row's commercial areas. They don't start trouble but they finish it. They'll report anything unusual to Warka Hall.
- **Echoguard patrols**: Infrequent and predictable. The patrol schedule hasn't changed in months, which means everyone knows when they're coming.
- **Debt-mark flare-ups**: Citizens whose marks trigger — a debtor considering flight, a contract breach in progress. The pain is visible and the screams are public. A daily occurrence.

### Possible Encounters
- **Unbound recruitment**: Whispered conversations, passed pamphlets, coded greetings. The movement is active here but carefully hidden.
- **Ledgerkeeper surveillance**: Plainclothes operatives monitoring the district. Arul might recognize the tradecraft.
- **Ward-blind zones**: Areas where the echo-wards don't reach. Some are natural gaps; some have been deliberately expanded by residents who know how to dampen wards. The crew can operate freely in these zones.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The Warrens connect to the pre-foundation tunnels beneath Kovil Plaza. The goblins know this and have kept it secret for generations. Sparks might learn of it through his Tinker's Warrens connections.
- Mesfin's ward ring can detect Changelings and magical disguises. Anyone entering Warka Hall under illusion is at risk.
- The Scar Parlor's proprietor is a former Keth'vorah-equivalent from another city — someone who understands magical binding at a theoretical level. They could be recruited as a specialist for the heist.
- An underground river runs beneath Teff Row, connecting to the river delta. It's usable as an escape route but requires navigating flooded tunnels.

## History

Teff Row was the first settlement on the hill — laborers who built the original Kanakkur lived here, eating teff and sleeping in barracks. As the city grew upward and the contract system took hold, the laborers became debtors and the barracks became tenements. The district has been the city's underclass home for three centuries, generating most of Kanakkur's wealth and receiving almost none of it.

The Unbound movement originated here fifty years ago, born from a generation of debtors who decided that the system wasn't broken — it was working exactly as designed, and it was designed to exploit them.

## GM Notes

Teff Row is the crew's home base for Act 1. It provides:
- Meeting spaces (Hagos's back room, Warka Hall for Dawit's interactions with Mesfin)
- Equipment and contacts (Hagos's inventory, the Tinker's Warrens)
- Information (the Grain Market's dead drops, Mesfin's network, Unbound contacts)
- Complications (Mesfin's surveillance, Echoguard patrols, the ever-present risk of debt-mark enforcement)

The ward-blind zones are critical for heist planning. The crew needs to identify and use these zones for meetings, as anywhere under echo-ward coverage is effectively public.

Teff Row should feel alive — chaotic, noisy, human. It's the opposite of the Nallur Vaults' sterile precision. The people here are real, their problems are real, and what the crew does will affect them directly. Don't let the heist planning become abstract — ground it in the district's texture.
