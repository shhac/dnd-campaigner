# Kanakkur

**Type**: City
**Region**: River delta, central trade corridor
**Campaign**: The Vault of Echoes

## Overview

Kanakkur is the City of Binding Contracts — a tiered metropolis built on ancient foundations, where mercantile magic and civic bureaucracy have fused into a system that governs every transaction, every agreement, and every debt. The city descends from the wealthy Marai Quarter at its peak to the underworld of Teff Row at its base, with the weight of centuries pressing down on everyone below.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Ward-light glows from the spires above Kovil Plaza, casting the upper tiers in permanent amber twilight. Below, the streets narrow and the light thins. Contract offices line every major road, their windows stacked with enchanted parchment. Debt-marks glow faintly on forearms throughout the lower tiers — small constellations of obligation.

**Sounds**: The scratch of quills on contract parchment. The chime of sealed transactions — each contract produces a faint harmonic when signed. The murmur of the echo-wards, a low hum that residents stop hearing after a few years but visitors find unsettling.

**Smells**: Contract ink — copper and old paper. Spiced street food from the middle-tier markets. The ozone tang of active wards, stronger near Kovil Plaza. In Teff Row, cheaper smells: grain, sweat, lamp oil.

**Feeling**: The city watches you. Not metaphorically — the echo-wards record everything in public spaces. Residents develop a particular body language: careful, controlled, always aware that their actions are being noted. Visitors describe it as "being inside a very organized prison that serves excellent food."

## Key Features

### The Tiers
Kanakkur is built on a natural hill, but generations of construction have exaggerated the elevation into distinct social strata:
- **Upper Tier (Marai Quarter)**: Counting House estates, luxury residences, private gardens. Heavily warded. Echoguard patrols every quarter-hour.
- **Middle Tier (Merchant's Ring)**: Markets, workshops, contract offices, taverns, the Wardsmith Quarter. The working economy lives here.
- **Lower Tier (Teff Row and surrounds)**: Tenements, labor camps, pawnshops, underground markets. Echoguard presence thins. Mesfin's territory.
- **Below (The Nallur Vaults)**: Beneath Kovil Plaza, accessible only through the Echoguard-controlled entrance. The contract archives and the city's deepest secrets.

### Kovil Plaza
The civic center, sitting between the upper and middle tiers. The Echoguard headquarters, the public contract offices, the ceremonial ward-spire, and the entrance to the Nallur Vaults all face this plaza. During the Festival of Sealed Words, it fills with citizens. The rest of the year, it's an intimidating expanse of clean stone and watchful guards.

### The Ward-Spires
Seven slender towers positioned throughout the city, anchoring the echo-ward network. They pulse with visible energy during the Festival. The spires are maintained by Nallur House under perpetual contract — the oldest active contract in Kanakkur's records.

## Points of Interest

- **The Sealed Library**: Public contract archive where citizens can look up registered agreements. Staffed by clerks who are helpful about procedures and evasive about specifics.
- **The Wardsmith Quarter**: Middle-tier district where magical artisans work. Sparks' former guild hall is here, now sealed.
- **The Grain Market**: Lower-tier market that serves as Teff Row's legitimate face. Hagos's shop is one street off the market.
- **The River Gate**: The city's main entrance for river trade. Heavily warded; all visitors' names and business are recorded. A secondary, unwarded entrance exists through the old sewer system (known to Teff Row operators).

## Encounters

### Likely Encounters
- **Echoguard patrols**: Regular in upper and middle tiers, sporadic in lower tiers. They stop and question anyone who "doesn't belong" — a subjective determination based on dress, speech, and visible debt-marks.
- **Contract hawkers**: Middle-tier salespeople offering loans, employment contracts, and property deals. Some are predatory.
- **Debt collectors**: Licensed by the Counting Houses. Professional, persistent, backed by legal authority.

### Possible Encounters
- **Unbound agitators**: Occasionally distribute pamphlets or deface ward-spires with broken-chain graffiti
- **Ledgerkeeper surveillance**: Plainclothes operatives monitoring persons of interest
- **Ward malfunctions**: Rare but dramatic — echoes replaying in unexpected locations, wards triggering on the wrong targets

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The ancient foundations predate the contract system by centuries. Who built them, and why, is unknown.
- The sewer system connects to pre-foundation tunnels that run beneath the Nallur Vaults — a possible infiltration route, but warded and dangerous.
- The ward-spires are showing stress fractures in their enchantments. Nallur House has been repairing them for decades but the degradation is accelerating. The Festival's ward-recharging ceremony is no longer cosmetic — it's structural.
- A small Changeling community lives in Kanakkur's middle tier, hidden in plain sight. They have no formal organization but share information about ward detection methods.

## History

Kanakkur was founded three centuries ago on pre-existing ruins. The original settlers discovered that the foundations contained residual magical infrastructure — ley-line convergences that made enchantment unusually stable and powerful. The first ward-smiths adapted this infrastructure into the contract system, and the Counting Houses rose to power as the system's administrators.

The current four-House structure stabilized about 150 years ago. Before that, the Houses warred openly, with the contract system serving as both weapon and prize. The current peace is held together by mutual dependence and the shared understanding that destroying the system would destroy them all.

## GM Notes

Kanakkur is a character in the story. The city's ward-hum, its tiered social structure, its pervasive surveillance — these should be present in every scene. Characters never forget they're being watched. The echo-wards create a specific behavioral effect: people in Kanakkur are careful, indirect, and skilled at saying one thing while meaning another.

The tier system creates natural movement challenges for the crew. Going up (toward the Marai Quarter and the vaults) means passing through more checkpoints and heavier ward coverage. Going down (toward Teff Row) means entering Mesfin's territory. The middle tier is the crew's most natural operating zone — visible enough to blend in, low enough to avoid elite scrutiny.
