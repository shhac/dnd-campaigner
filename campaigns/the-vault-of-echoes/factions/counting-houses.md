# The Counting Houses

**Type**: Mercantile Oligarchy
**Influence**: Dominant (Kanakkur) / Regional (trade network)
**Campaign**: The Vault of Echoes

## Overview

The Counting Houses are four ancient mercantile dynasties that control Kanakkur's economy through the contract magic system. They administer the city's binding agreements, collect fees on every transaction, and maintain the ward infrastructure that enforces compliance. They are banks, courts, and government rolled into one — legitimate, respected, and built on a foundation of coerced consent.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: Maintain stability, prosperity, and the rule of contract law in Kanakkur.
**True Goal**: Preserve and expand their power. The contract system makes the Houses wealthy and irreplaceable. Any threat to the system is a threat to them personally.

## Methods

- Control the contract ink supply (only Houses can produce binding-grade ink)
- Maintain the ward infrastructure through exclusive contracts with wardsmith guilds
- Use the Ledgerkeepers for intelligence and the Echoguard for enforcement
- Lobby, bribe, and pressure any entity that threatens the system
- Present themselves as public servants maintaining civic order

## Structure

**Leadership**: Each House is led by a principal — the head of the founding family. The four principals form an informal governing council.
**Hierarchy**: Principal > House Elders > Division Heads > Senior Staff > Clerks > Contracted Workers
**Membership**: Thousands across all four Houses. Employment contracts are binding, naturally.

## Resources

- **Wealth**: Vast — they control the economy
- **Military**: None directly, but the Echoguard serves their interests
- **Magic**: Significant — they control the ward-smiths, the contract ink, and the vault infrastructure
- **Political**: Dominant — there is no government in Kanakkur beyond the Houses

**Symbol**: Four interlocking circles — each House's sigil forming a chain
**Colors**: Selvan (burgundy/gold), Kovil (deep blue/silver), Nallur (shifting ward-glass), Pandyan (green/bronze)
**Recognition Signs**: House pins worn at the collar. Contract ink stains on the hands (clerks). The faint ward-glow on clothing of senior staff.

**Current Standing**: Neutral (the party doesn't exist to them yet)
**History**: Sparks was contracted by Nallur House. Arul is secretly a Ledgerkeeper agent. Dawit killed a Selvan House enforcer. Paavai works for Selvan House.

## Hooks & Opportunities

- Pandyan House is financially vulnerable and might be turned against Selvan House with the right leverage
- Internal rivalries between Houses can be exploited — they cooperate but don't trust each other
- The contract ink supply chain has vulnerabilities that could be disrupted
- Selvan House's contract acquisitions from other Houses are suspicious — revealing the pattern could fracture the alliance

## GM Notes

The Counting Houses are the establishment. They're not a unified evil — they're a system of competing interests that maintains itself through shared dependence on the contract framework. Breaking the Houses requires understanding that they're held together by self-interest, not loyalty.

Selvan House is the primary antagonist, but the other Houses are potential wild cards. Kovil and Nallur might oppose the Ādhāra Protocol if they learned about it — permanent contracts would consolidate Selvan's power at their expense. Pandyan is desperate enough to ally with anyone who offers them an advantage.

The crew's multiple connections to the Houses (Sparks' history, Arul's employer, Dawit's crime, Paavai's position) create a web of vulnerabilities and opportunities. Every action the crew takes near the Houses risks activating one of these connections.
