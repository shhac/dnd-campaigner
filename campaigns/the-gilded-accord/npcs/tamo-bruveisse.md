# Speaker Tamô Brûveisse

**Role**: Faction Leader / Political Operator / Wild Card
**Location**: The Théâtre Ghalliverre (Commons Assembly Hall)
**Campaign**: The Gilded Accord

|---|---|

| **Age** | 44 |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

Compact, kinetic, always in motion. Dark skin, close-cropped hair, a thin scar across his left cheekbone from a dockside fight he never bothers to explain. Dresses in Assembly working clothes — clean but deliberately unadorned, the uniform of someone who wants to look like he hasn't forgotten where he came from. The only concession to his position is a bronze pin in the shape of the Assembly's scale-and-chain emblem.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Warm and direct in person, theatrical and cutting in public. Two different people depending on the audience.
**Motivation**: Power for the Commons Assembly — real power, not the consultative role they've been limited to. He wants structural change, and he'll use any crisis to get it.
**Fear**: Being outmaneuvered. He's brilliant but always aware that the Mint and the Veil have resources he can't match.

**Personality Traits**:
- Remembers every favor and every slight with equal precision. Keeps a mental ledger more detailed than the Mint's.
- Genuinely cares about the people he represents — but also genuinely willing to sacrifice individual interests for systemic change.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Shifts registers like changing clothes. In the Assembly: soaring rhetoric, rhythmic cadences, the voice of the people. In private: blunt, fast, strategic. The private Brûveisse is the real one; the public version is a performance, and he knows it.

**Mannerisms**: Points when he argues. Paces. Sits only when he's won the point. Makes a gesture of open hands when he's about to propose something — inviting agreement before it's offered.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- "Caudressô's dead, and the hill is burning. You know what doesn't burn? The docks. We're still here. We're always still here."
- "Don't mistake my cooperation for trust. I'm here because the alternative is worse."
- "The Accord gave the Commons a seat at the table eighty years after the founding. You know what the first eighty years were like? Ask the families who remember."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- Rose through the Assembly from guild representative (Chandlers' Guild) to Speaker in twelve years — the fastest ascent in Assembly history
- Led the Wage Reform Act that raised minimum dock pay by 30%
- Popular in the lower wards, feared in the upper terraces
- His public position: the investigation must succeed, the Accord must be preserved, but it must be reformed

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- He's been independently researching the Accord's history and suspects it was written to exclude the Commons
- He knows the general strike is his ultimate weapon and has been quietly organizing cells in every guild
- He has intelligence sources in the Rampart (sympathetic enlisted soldiers) but not in the Mint or Veil

### Secrets (GM only)
- He obtained partial copies of Caudressô's research through an Assembly clerk who was also helping the Councillor
- He's planning to use whatever the investigation reveals as ammunition for constitutional reform, regardless of the consequences
- He doesn't know about the Dorêve Circle specifically but suspects "old money" has been manipulating the Accord for centuries
- His candidate for Fifth Principal is himself — not officially, but he's positioning for it

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Cooperative but watchful. He assigned Kes to the investigation because Kes is loyal to Salthook, not to Brûveisse — and that honesty is useful.

**Current Disposition**: Cautious

**History with Party**: Issued the joint mandate. Will contact Kes privately with additional instructions.

## GM Notes

Brûveisse is the most politically talented person in the campaign. He's not corrupt in the traditional sense — he genuinely wants to help the Commons. But he's willing to use methods that would horrify Kes, including exploiting the crisis, suppressing information that doesn't serve his narrative, and sacrificing the investigation's integrity if reform is the prize.

His relationship with Kes is the emotional core of the Commons subplot. Kes is what Brûveisse used to be — idealistic, angry, honest. Brûveisse has learned that honesty alone doesn't change systems. The question is whether he's right or whether he's become the thing he fought against.

If the PCs expose the Accord's corruption, Brûveisse will use it. Whether he uses it for the Commons or for himself depends on how the PCs handle him.
