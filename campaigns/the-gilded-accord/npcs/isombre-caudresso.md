## Appearance

In life: A tall, lean man with an academic's stoop and a politician's eyes. White hair worn long and pulled back. Deep lines around a mouth that smiled more than his reputation suggested. Dressed in the Fifth Principal's traditional undyed linen — the only Council member without faction colors. His hands were ink-stained, always.

In death: Arranged in the Pose of Accord. Arms crossed over his chest, palms up. Expression serene — the Veilsleep stopped his heart without pain. The ink stains remain.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: In life, he was the Accord's conscience — patient, scholarly, and quietly formidable in debate
**Motivation**: He believed in the Accord as an ideal, not just a document. He spent his career trying to make the reality match the principle.
**Fear**: That he'd been defending a lie. When he discovered the truth, the fear became certainty.

**Personality Traits**:
- Listened more than he spoke. When he did speak in Council, everyone stopped to hear it — thirty-four years of measured words had earned that.
- Kept meticulous records. His cipher journal is the physical manifestation of a mind that believed truth could be preserved in writing.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Slow, deliberate, with the cadence of someone who chose each word as if it would be quoted. Fond of historical parallels. Never raised his voice in Council — the one time he did (seventeen years ago, over a proposed war tax) is still talked about.

**Mannerisms**: Wrote notes during conversations — not to be rude, but because he trusted paper more than memory. Removed his spectacles when about to say something important.

**Sample Dialogue** (from Council records and personal correspondence):
- "The Accord is not a gift the founders gave us. It's a debt they left us to repay."
- "If I've learned anything in thirty-four years on this Council, it's that the truth is always more complicated — and more necessary — than we're comfortable admitting."
- "I am not a brave man. But I find I can no longer be a silent one."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge (what was known in life)
- Served as Fifth Principal for 34 years — the longest in the Accord's history
- Appointed as a retired professor of Accord Law from the Môrivôs Academy
- Known as the "Architect of Balance" for his role in mediating faction disputes
- Had no faction affiliation — the Fifth Seat is independent by design

### Private Knowledge (discovered through investigation)
- In his final months, he was accessing restricted archives and old architectural surveys
- He had private meetings with a Rampart archivist (Zubeldia Galarreta) that were not on his public calendar
- He sent a coded letter to someone outside Môrivôs three weeks before his death — recipient unknown

### Secrets (GM only)
- He was a former member of the Dorêve Circle, inducted when he was appointed Fifth Principal
- He accepted the position believing the Circle was a benign tradition — old families maintaining institutional knowledge
- He discovered the altered Accord and the dormancy clause two months before his death
- He confronted Rôsamunde Ghalliverre; she told him to drop it. He refused.
- He hid the Founding Seal inside the Accord Chamber's walls to prevent the confirmation of a new Principal until the truth could be revealed
- His cipher journal contains everything: the altered text, the Circle's membership, the dormancy clause. He intended to present it to the full Council.
- He chose to be found in the Pose of Accord as a final message — the pose meant to honor the founders used to shame their descendants

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: Everything. The entire investigation radiates from his death.

**Potential Uses**:
- His cipher journal is the campaign's MacGuffin
- His relationships (with Rôsamunde, with Zubeldia, with the Dorêve Circle) are the threads the PCs will pull

- His character — his integrity, his courage, his fatal decision to speak — provides the campaign's moral compass even in death

## GM Notes

Caudressô is the ghost that haunts this campaign. He was a good man who discovered a terrible truth and died trying to reveal it. His murder wasn't personal — it was institutional. The Dorêve Circle killed him the way a body rejects an organ: automatically, because the alternative was destruction.

The PCs will piece together who he was through evidence, testimony, and his own writings. By the time they understand what he was trying to do, they'll face the same choice he did: speak the truth, knowing the cost, or stay silent, knowing the weight.

His cipher journal should be discovered in stages — fragments, partial decryptions, contextual clues. The full picture emerges only when all the pieces are assembled. This pacing is critical: too fast and the political tension collapses; too slow and the Thirty-Day Clock becomes meaningless.
