# Rôsamunde Ghalliverre

**Role**: Faction Leader / Suspect / Antagonist
**Location**: The Pallisette, Mint offices (Maison Dorêve)
**Campaign**: The Gilded Accord

## Quick Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race** | Human |
| **Occupation** | Principal of the Mint (La Fraternité Dorêve), Quintenary Council member |
| **Age** | 58 |
| **Status** | Alive |

## Appearance

A woman who dresses like money that doesn't need to announce itself. Silver hair worn in a precise coil. Dark eyes that calculate faster than they blink. Her clothes are exquisitely cut but never ostentatious — muted blues and grays that say "I could buy this building" without saying "look at me." She wears a single gold ring: the Ghalliverre family seal, which predates the Accord itself.

## Personality

**Demeanor**: Gracious, controlled, and patient in a way that suggests she already knows how the conversation ends
**Motivation**: Preserve the Mint's primacy and, through it, the city's stability. She genuinely believes Môrivôs cannot function without the Mint's financial infrastructure.
**Fear**: Irrelevance. A world where the Mint is diminished is a world where Rôsamunde Ghalliverre has no purpose.

**Personality Traits**:
- Never raises her voice. The quieter she gets, the more dangerous the situation.
- Uses generosity as a weapon — every gift is a debt, every favor a lever.

## Voice & Mannerisms

**Speech Pattern**: Measured, warm, with the cadence of someone used to being listened to. Frames commands as suggestions. Uses "we" when she means "I" and "the city" when she means "the Mint."

**Mannerisms**: Touches her ring when thinking. Makes eye contact that feels like a handshake — firm, brief, evaluating. Stands rather than sits when receiving visitors.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- "We all want what's best for Môrivôs. The question is whether we can agree on what 'best' means before the clock runs out."
- "I don't deal in threats, Lieutenant. I deal in outcomes."
- "Îsombré was a friend. That makes this harder. It doesn't make it less necessary."

## What They Know

### Public Knowledge
- Longest-serving current Council member after Caudressô (22 years)
- Architect of the Tôt currency stabilization that saved the economy during the last trade war
- Known for pragmatism — will work with any faction if the numbers make sense
- Caudressô was her political ally, and his death leaves a power vacuum she's publicly mourning

### Private Knowledge (requires trust/persuasion)
- She knows exactly how much each faction owes the Mint in outstanding debts
- She's been quietly acquiring property in the lower wards through intermediaries
- She has a private intelligence network separate from the Veil — financial informants in every trade house

### Secrets (GM only)
- She knows the Accord was altered because her grandmother told her on her deathbed
- She is a member of the Dorêve Circle and has known about the dormancy clause for decades
- She did not order Caudressô's murder but recognized the method immediately and knows who did
- She is preparing to invoke the dormancy clause if the Thirty-Day Clock expires
- Caudressô confronted her privately three weeks before his death; she told him to drop his research

## Relationship to Party

**First Impression**: Gracious, cooperative, concerned. She wants the investigation to succeed — or at least to appear to succeed. She'll provide resources and access, within limits.

**Current Disposition**: Guarded

**History with Party**: Issued the joint mandate. Will assign Vélissande private instructions through Mint channels.

## Plot Relevance

**Connected To**: The Dorêve Circle, the dormancy clause, the altered Accord, the Mint's financial dominance

**Potential Uses**:
- Source of resources and political access for the investigation
- Obstacle when evidence points toward Mint involvement
- The person who invokes the dormancy clause in Act 3, forcing the crisis
- Potential negotiating partner if the PCs choose reform over revolution

## GM Notes

Rôsamunde is the campaign's most complex antagonist. She's not evil — she genuinely believes the Mint's dominance keeps Môrivôs stable. The dormancy clause is her insurance policy against chaos. She'll invoke it not out of greed but out of conviction that no one else can hold the city together.

She did not order Caudressô's murder. But she knows who did (Gattô Théveresse, acting on Dorêve Circle consensus) and has chosen not to expose it because doing so would unravel everything. She told Caudressô to stop his research. He didn't. And now he's dead, and she has to live with the fact that she warned him and he didn't listen.

If the PCs can reach her — genuinely challenge her worldview — she might become an ally for reform rather than an obstacle. But reaching her requires proving that stability is possible without Mint supremacy.
