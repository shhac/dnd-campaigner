# The Mendizabal Steps

**Type**: Historic Monument / Military Site
**Region**: Old City, western Môrivôs
**Campaign**: The Gilded Accord

## Overview

A monumental stone staircase carved into the western cliff face, descending from the Rampart's original fortress to the old harbor below. Built before the Accord, when the military faction controlled the western approach. Now partly ceremonial, partly functional — Rampart officers still train on the steps, and the fortress above houses the Rampart's archives and armory. Beneath the steps: vaults that predate the city itself.

## Atmosphere

**Sights**: Three hundred stone steps, each carved with Euskaran script — the names of every Rampart soldier who died in the city's defense. The steps are wide enough for four abreast. Iron railings added centuries later, patinated green. The fortress at the top: blocky, unadorned, flying the Rampart's mountain-gate banner.
**Sounds**: Wind from the harbor, channeled up the cliff face. Boot steps echoing. The distant clash of training weapons from the fortress yard.
**Smells**: Salt air and old stone. Metal from the armory. The mineral tang of deep rock near the vault entrances.
**Feeling**: Ancient. Purposeful. This place was built to last, and it has. The weight of eleven generations of military tradition presses down from above.

## Key Features

### The Steps Themselves
Three hundred steps, each eight inches deep and five feet wide. The Euskaran names are carved in a spiral pattern — you'd need to walk the full staircase to read them all. Some names are so old the stone has nearly swallowed them.

### The Rampart Fortress (Irrintzi Zahar)
The original Rampart headquarters, now secondary to Irrintzi Hall in the Pallisette. Houses the historical archives, the traditional armory, and the Rampart's memorial hall. Goitxarri would have trained here as a cadet.

### The Vaults
Below the steps, carved into the cliff's natural caves and expanded over centuries. Originally weapons storage. Now: sealed chambers requiring Rampart authorization to access. The deepest vault has not been opened in living memory.

## Points of Interest

- **The Memorial Hall**: A room at the fortress's heart where the names on the steps are repeated in copper plaques, along with service records. The Zumarratz family takes up an entire wall.
- **The Deep Vault**: The lowest level, sealed with Euskaran locks that require specific knowledge to open. Contains the original, unaltered Gilded Accord — hidden here by a dissenting founder.
- **The Training Yard**: An open area at the top of the steps where cadets practice. Goitxarri's initials are carved into a training post.

## Secrets & Hidden Things

- The deep vault contains the original Accord document, sealed in a lead-lined case by a dissenting founder three hundred years ago
- Opening the deep vault requires the Founding Seal as a key — the lock was designed to prevent access without it
- The archivist's records contain gaps that correspond to Dorêve Circle activity — documents removed and never returned

## History

Built before the city had a name, when the western cliffs were a military fortification defending the harbor approach. The Rampart's founding family — the Mendizabal — carved the first steps as a practical measure (troops needed to reach the harbor quickly). The naming tradition began after the first siege, when forty-three soldiers died defending the harbor. Three centuries and hundreds of names later, the steps are as much memorial as architecture.

The vaults were originally natural caves, expanded during the founding era as secure storage. When the Accord was signed, one of the five founders — the Rampart's representative — secretly preserved the original document in the deepest vault, disagreeing with the alterations but lacking the power to prevent them.

## GM Notes

The Mendizabal Steps are the campaign's climactic location. The Act 3 retrieval of the original Accord from the deep vault is one of the final major scenes. The vault's lock requiring the Founding Seal creates a mechanical connection to the Accord Chamber scene — the PCs must find the Seal before they can access the truth.

This location should feel earned. The PCs shouldn't visit here casually. When they come to the Steps with purpose — to open the vault, to retrieve the document — it should feel like the campaign converging on its endgame.

Gattô Théveresse's ambush during the retrieval is the campaign's most dangerous combat encounter. The Steps' narrow construction creates a challenging tactical environment — no room to maneuver, enemies above and below, and the most important document in the city's history in their hands.
