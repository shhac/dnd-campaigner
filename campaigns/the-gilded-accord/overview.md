# The Gilded Accord

## Setting

**World**: Môrivôs, a free port city-state on the Alcyone Coast
**Era/Time Period**: The Third Century of the Accord — three hundred years after the founding pact that bound five factions into shared governance
**Starting Location**: The Pallisette, the neutral government quarter where all five factions maintain offices

## Tone & Themes

**Tone**: Tense, sophisticated, morally gray — a city that runs on negotiation, leverage, and carefully maintained appearances
**Core Themes**:
- Power as performance — who controls the narrative controls the city
- The rot beneath consensus — what compromises hold a society together, and what happens when they break
- Loyalty vs. ideology — when your faction asks you to betray your principles
- The cost of stability — how much injustice is acceptable to prevent collapse

## The Hook

Councillor Îsombré Caudressô, the longest-serving member of the Quintenary Council and architect of the current balance of power, has been found dead in the Accord Chamber — the sacred hall where no violence has occurred in three hundred years. His body was arranged in the Pose of Accord, the ceremonial position from the founding pact, with his hands placed over a document no one can find.

The Quintenary Council has exactly thirty days to confirm a Fifth Principal or the Accord dissolves and the city fractures along factional lines. Each faction suspects the others. Each faction has something to hide. And the four operatives who have been summoned to investigate — one from each faction — are expected to find the truth while protecting their faction's secrets.

The problem: someone in this room benefits from the Accord dying.

## Major Factions

### The Mint (Formal: La Fraternité Dorêve)
- **Type**: Financial oligarchy / central bank
- **Goals**: Maintain Môrivôs's position as the premier free port; protect the currency and trade agreements that make the city rich
- **Relationship to Party**: Employer of Vélissande Thoraudenne (PC)

### The Rampart (Formal: Errialde Mendizabal)
- **Type**: Military order / city defense
- **Goals**: Protect the walls, maintain the garrison, ensure the city's independence from neighboring kingdoms
- **Relationship to Party**: Employer of Goitxarri Zumarratz-Errialde (PC)

### The Commons Assembly (Formal: L'Assemblée Ghalliverre)
- **Type**: Popular legislature / guild alliance
- **Goals**: Represent the working population, expand voting rights, check the power of the Mint and the old families
- **Relationship to Party**: Employer of Kes Dolenne (PC)

### The Veil (Formal: La Chambre Éteinte)
- **Type**: Intelligence service / secret police
- **Goals**: Know everything; protect the Accord through information control, surveillance, and quiet elimination of threats
- **Relationship to Party**: Employer of Théodarrhe Nocenne (PC)

## Key Locations

### The Pallisette
The government quarter, built on a raised plateau in the center of the city. White limestone buildings arranged around the Accord Chamber. Neutral ground — faction agents may carry only ceremonial weapons within its walls. The architecture is deliberately austere, a counterweight to the opulence of the districts below.

### The Accord Chamber (Etxebarria Zahar)
The oldest building in Môrivôs. Where the original five founders signed the Gilded Accord three centuries ago. Sacred, inviolable — until now. The murder of Caudressô within its walls is not just a crime but a sacrilege that threatens the spiritual foundation of the city's unity.

### Salthook District
The working waterfront. Warehouses, fish markets, chandleries, and the cramped housing of dockworkers and laborers. The Commons Assembly's power base. Rough, vital, and increasingly angry about rising costs and stagnant wages.

### The Théâtre Ghalliverre
The Commons Assembly's great hall, a converted amphitheater where public debates and guild votes are held. Acoustics designed so a single voice can reach every seat. Political theater in the most literal sense.

## The Threat

**Main Antagonist/Threat**: A conspiracy to dissolve the Accord and replace shared governance with single-faction rule — but which faction, and through whom, is the central mystery
**Their Goal**: Exploit Caudressô's murder to prevent confirmation of a Fifth Principal within the thirty-day deadline, triggering dissolution
**Timeline**: 30 days from campaign start (urgent, with daily political developments)
**What Happens If They Succeed**: Civil war along factional lines. The Mint seizes the treasury. The Rampart locks down the gates. The Commons erupts in riots. The Veil goes to ground. Three hundred years of fragile peace collapse in weeks.

## Campaign Arc

### Act 1: The Thirty Days
The investigation into Caudressô's death. Each faction assigns an operative. The four PCs must work together despite conflicting loyalties. Clues point in contradictory directions — deliberately. Someone is running a disinformation campaign.

### Act 2: The Gilded Cage
The truth begins to emerge: Caudressô discovered that the Accord itself was built on a lie — the founding document was altered after signing, granting one faction hidden advantages. He was killed to keep this secret. The PCs must decide what to do with this knowledge while the clock keeps ticking.

### Act 3: The Fifth Principal
The final days. The PCs have the truth, or enough of it. They must choose: expose the lie and risk destroying the Accord, preserve the lie and maintain a corrupt peace, or find a third path — rewriting the Accord for a new age. Every faction has a candidate for Fifth Principal. The PCs' choice determines the city's future.

## Notable Items

### The Founding Seal (Zigilua Zahar)
- **Type**: Artifact / political symbol
- **Location**: Normally displayed in the Accord Chamber; currently missing
- **Significance**: The wax-and-metal seal that authenticated the original Accord. Without it, no new Principal can be confirmed. Its disappearance is either evidence or sabotage.

### Caudressô's Cipher Journal
- **Type**: Encoded document
- **Location**: Unknown — Caudressô's office was searched before his body was found
- **Significance**: Caudressô kept a private record of everything he learned about the Accord's true history. Whoever finds it controls the narrative.

## House Rules

- Social encounters use faction standing mechanics (see faction-standings.md)
- "Downtime" between scenes represents hours of political maneuvering — players declare what their character is doing off-screen
- Combat is rare but lethal when it occurs — this is a city of knives in the dark, not open battlefields
- Perception and Investigation checks against NPCs are contested (NPCs actively hide information)
- The 30-day timer advances based on story beats, not real time

## Session Zero Notes

- **Content boundaries**: No torture for information extraction; political violence is implicit, not graphic
- **Player expectations**: Investigation, social manipulation, faction politics. Combat exists but is a failure state in most situations — if swords come out, something went wrong
- **Character connections**: The four PCs are assigned to a joint investigation by their respective factions. They are not friends. They may become allies, or they may betray each other. Trust is earned, not assumed.

## GM Guidance: Campaign Style

### Investigation Mechanics
This campaign runs on social encounters and information gathering. Most scenes involve:
- Interviewing NPCs with competing interests
- Searching locations for physical evidence
- Decoding political subtext in faction communications
- Making faction-loyalty decisions that have mechanical consequences (standing changes)

### The Clock
The 30-day deadline creates urgency without requiring real-time tracking. Each session covers 2-4 days of in-game time. The GM advances the clock at beat boundaries. Political events happen whether or not the PCs are present — the world moves.

### Faction Loyalty Tension
Each PC serves a faction. The investigation will eventually force them to choose between faction loyalty and the truth. This is the campaign's central dramatic engine. The GM should present these choices as genuine dilemmas, not gotchas.

### Combat at Level 3
The party starts at level 3 — competent professionals, not green adventurers. Combat is rare but should feel dangerous:
- Ambushes in narrow streets
- Poisoned drinks at political dinners
- Duels with faction enforcers
- Desperate fights when covers are blown
