# The Commons Assembly (L'Assemblée Ghalliverre)

**Type**: Popular Legislature / Guild Alliance
**Influence**: Growing (local), None (regional)
**Campaign**: The Gilded Accord

## Overview

The voice of the working people. The Commons Assembly represents everyone who isn't a banker, a soldier, or a spy — which is to say, the vast majority of Môrivôs's population. Born from the guild system eighty years after the Accord's founding, the Assembly is the youngest faction and the most frustrated. They have numbers but not power, and the gap between those two things defines their politics.

## Goals

**Public Goal**: Represent the working population in governance. Expand voting rights, improve labor conditions, check the power of the Mint and the old families.
**True Goal**: Real structural power. Speaker Brûveisse wants the Commons to be equal to the other factions, not a consultative afterthought. The crisis is an opportunity to rewrite the rules.

## Methods

- Public rhetoric: Speeches, pamphlets, open debates in the Théâtre Ghalliverre
- Guild organizing: Coordinated action through the guild network — strikes, work slowdowns, boycotts
- Street-level intelligence: Common folk notice things. The Assembly has ears everywhere below the second terrace.
- Political pressure: Using their Council vote as leverage in negotiations

## Structure

**Leadership**: The Speaker (Tamô Brûveisse) is elected by guild representatives every five years
**Hierarchy**: Speaker → Council of Guilds (12 representatives) → Guild Masters → Guild Members → Associates
**Membership**: Every guild member is automatically an Assembly constituent. Roughly 15,000 people have voting rights through their guilds. Non-guild workers (day laborers, informal traders) are represented in theory but underserved in practice.

## Resources

- **Wealth**: Poor — funded by guild dues, which are modest
- **Military**: None — the Assembly has no armed force, which is both a vulnerability and a point of pride
- **Magic**: None institutionally — individual members may have abilities
- **Political**: Growing — their single Council vote rarely matters, but their ability to mobilize the populace does

## Symbols & Recognition

**Symbol**: A scale balanced on a chain — justice constrained but not defeated
**Colors**: Brown and iron-red
**Recognition Signs**: The Assembly's bronze pin (scale-and-chain) worn openly. Guild members carry their trade cards. Assembly meetings are announced by runners in iron-red sashes.

## Hooks & Opportunities

- Assembly contacts can provide street-level intelligence the other factions can't match
- The general strike (Act 3) is the Assembly's ultimate leverage — knowing about it in advance is valuable
- Brûveisse's partial copy of Caudressô's research can fill gaps in the PCs' knowledge
- The Assembly's exclusion from the Dorêve Circle means they're the only "clean" faction — a fact that cuts both ways

## GM Notes

The Commons Assembly is the campaign's moral test case. They're the faction that was genuinely wronged — excluded from the original Accord, added as an afterthought, systematically disadvantaged by structures they didn't know were rigged. Their anger is justified.

But justified anger doesn't guarantee wise action. Brûveisse is willing to exploit the crisis for structural reform, and his methods (suppressing information, organizing a general strike, positioning himself for the Fifth Seat) are morally ambiguous even in pursuit of a legitimate goal.

The Assembly's lack of involvement in the Dorêve Circle makes them simultaneously the most trustworthy faction and the most volatile. If the truth about the altered Accord becomes public, the Commons will be furious — and their fury could tear the city apart faster than the conspiracy itself.

Kes is the PCs' window into this faction. His relationship with Brûveisse — admiration curdling into suspicion as the Speaker's methods become clearer — is one of the campaign's key character arcs.
