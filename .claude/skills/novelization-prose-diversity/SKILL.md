# Prose Diversity Guide

Loaded by novelizer-writer and novelizer-editor. Canonical reference for avoiding repetitive prose.

## Forbidden Phrases

Zero tolerance. Delete and replace with something specific to this character, this moment, this world.

### Emotional Clichés
- "a testament to [noun]" → Describe the specific evidence
- "the weight of [noun] settled on/over" → Physical sensation: tight chest, stiff neck, aching jaw
- "something inside [pronoun] broke/cracked/shifted/stirred" → Name the specific feeling and its physical expression
- "eyes widened in [emotion]" → Describe the involuntary reaction: stepped back, breath caught
- "heart pounded/hammered/raced" → Different body part or character-specific stress response
- "[emotion] washed over [pronoun]" → Active construction: character DOES something
- "couldn't quite place" → Either they recognize it or they don't — commit
- "the air itself seemed to [verb]" → Describe what the character senses
- "a silence that spoke volumes" → Describe what fills the silence
- "more than [pronoun] could bear" → Show the breaking point through action
- "couldn't help but [verb]" → "[Character] [verb]ed" — own the action

### Physical Action Clichés
- "let out a breath [pronoun] didn't know [pronoun] was holding" → Exhaled. Shoulders dropped. Jaw unclenched.
- "squared [possessive] shoulders" → Character-specific: cracked knuckles, adjusted grip
- "clenched [possessive] jaw" → Ground teeth, bit inside of cheek
- "exchanged a glance" → Describe what the glance communicates
- "swallowed hard" → Dry mouth, throat clicking
- "found [possessive] voice" → Just have them speak
- "forced [pronoun]self to [verb]" → Describe the effort: hands shaking, voice thin

### Descriptive Clichés
- "hung in the air" → Describe what persists: the echo, the smell
- "sent a shiver down [possessive] spine" → Character-specific chill
- "cast long shadows" → Describe what shadows conceal or reveal
- "the silence was deafening" → Describe specific sounds filling silence
- "cut through the silence/darkness/air" → Describe the sound/light itself

### Structural Clichés
- "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." — Use once per chapter maximum
- "Something like [emotion]" — Name it or show it
- "The kind of X that Y" — Cut the explanation
- "[Character] filed that away" — Show the thought landing
- "And [pronoun] knew, with a certainty that..." — Show through changed behavior
- "It was [character] who spoke first" — Just have them speak

## Sentence Structure Diversity Rules

### Openings
- No more than 2 consecutive sentences starting with the same word
- Track opening distribution: subject-first, prepositional, participial, dialogue, temporal, conditional. No pattern >40%
- Never start 3 consecutive paragraphs the same way

### Length and Rhythm
- No more than 3 consecutive sentences of similar length
- Follow long sentences with short ones (or vice versa) at least once per paragraph
- Fragments: 1-2 per chapter for impact
- Cap paragraphs at ~120 words

### Dialogue Attribution
- Rotate: "said" tag, action beat, no tag, internal reaction
- Never same method 3 times consecutively
- "Said" is invisible — prefer over creative tags
- Action beats > dialogue tags when action adds character info

## Cross-Chapter Diversity

### Voice vs. Construction
Match the **character's voice** (vocabulary, register, worldview), NOT the **author's constructions** (sentence patterns, metaphors, transitions).

**Carry forward**: Word choices, formality, internal monologue density, emotional processing style.
**Deliberately vary**: Sentence openings, metaphor families, paragraph structures, transitions, scene openings/closings.

### Avoidance List Protocol
After reading chapter N-1, list 5 constructions/phrases used more than once. These are your **avoidance list** for the current chapter.

### Distinctive Technique
Each chapter includes at least one technique not in the adjacent chapter: flashback, list, letter, extended metaphor, single-sentence paragraph, pure dialogue stretch, sensory-only description.
